Board certification is a credential from a specialty board, and for most US-trained physicians it comes after finishing an accredited residency and passing the board's exam. For IMGs who complete a US residency, the path is largely the same as for US graduates, with extra documentation. This page covers the general requirements, how IMG training is treated, and what ABIM's published policy says. Each board sets its own rules, so confirm yours on the board's site.

## What certification is and is not

The American Board of Medical Specialties (ABMS) is the umbrella organization for the 24 member boards it lists. A member board certifies physicians in its specialty. This is different from three other things you will meet:

| Term | Who gives it | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| ECFMG certification | [[ecfmg]] | You are eligible to enter US residency as an IMG |
| Medical license | State medical board | Legal permission to practice in that state |
| Board certification | Specialty board | You completed the board's training and passed its exams |
| Board eligible | The specialty board | You meet the board's training requirements but have not yet completed the exam or certification process (definitions vary by board) |

See [state licensure for IMGs](/wiki/licensure-career/state-licensure-for-imgs) for the license side.

## What ABMS member boards generally require

ABMS states that, before certification, a physician must:

- complete four years of premedical education,
- earn an MD or DO (or another credential approved by a member board) from a qualified medical school,
- complete full-time residency training in an ACGME-accredited program (ABMS's pages describe three to five years, and FSMB quotes ABMS as three to seven, so the length depends on the specialty),
- hold an unrestricted medical license in the US or Canada, and
- pass the examinations created and administered by the member board.

ABMS's Standards for Initial Certification say that training programs must be ACGME-accredited, with minimum durations of three years for general certification and one year for a subspecialty. They also say that member boards may choose to recognize alternate pathways for candidates who have not completed such residency programs, and they give "acceptance of training at international graduate medical education programs deemed to meet the standards" of the board as an example. Alternate pathways, the standards say, should be transparent, fair, objective and equitably available to candidates with comparable credentials. These pathways are optional: your board may not offer one.

## How IMG training is treated

### If you complete a US residency

If you finish an ACGME-accredited residency, you are generally on the same path as other graduates. As an IMG you should expect the board to ask for proof of your medical education credentials. For example, ABIM requires graduates of international medical schools to have a standard ECFMG certificate without expired examination dates, or comparable credentials from the Medical Council of Canada, or documentation of training for those who entered US GME through the Fifth Pathway.

Keep your ECFMG certificate current, and keep records of your training dates, evaluations and procedures. Boards track training by year, and gaps or repeated years can affect eligibility.

### If your training was abroad

Boards differ. ABMS's standards describe alternate pathways as optional, and what the board accepts depends on its policy. FSMB's April 2026 chart repeats ABMS's list of requirements and notes that, to be board eligible, an applicant must complete an ACGME-accredited residency and be licensed in the US or Canada. We did not find a general rule that foreign training alone satisfies a board, so if your plan depends on it, read your board's policy and write to the board.

State licensure pathways for internationally trained physicians (see [alternative licensure pathways](/wiki/licensure-career/alternative-licensure-pathways)) are separate from board certification. A state license obtained through such a pathway does not by itself make you board eligible. ABMS has said it supports certification routes but that licensure alone does not give the same access to employment as board certification.

## What ABIM's published policy says

We read ABIM's December 2025 Policies and Procedures as one example. We did not read other boards' policies for this page, so check yours.

- **Training.** To be admitted to the internal medicine certification exam, a physician must have completed, by August 31 of the exam year, 36 calendar months of US or Canadian graduate medical education accredited by the ACGME, the Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada or the Collège des médecins du Québec. Residency or research before the MD or DO cannot be credited.
- **IMG credentials.** Graduates of international medical schools need one of: a standard ECFMG certificate without expired examination dates, comparable Medical Council of Canada credentials, or documentation of training for candidates who entered US GME via the Fifth Pathway.
- **Special consideration.** ABIM publishes special consideration routes. One is for international medical graduates who are full-time US or Canadian faculty and completed internal medicine or subspecialty training abroad, who must be proposed by their department chair or program director and meet requirements such as faculty rank and consecutive years of appointment. ABIM also lists other special consideration categories. Check ABIM's current Special Consideration page for the up-to-date list and any pilot programs.

If you are in another specialty, find your board on the ABMS site and read its initial certification policy and any "international" or "special" candidate pages. Do not assume ABIM's rules apply.

## Fellowship and certification

Subspecialty certification generally depends on completing the training the board requires and on prior certification in the core specialty. ACGME's fellowship requirements contain an exception that lets some programs accept an "exceptionally qualified" international graduate, but ACGME states that applicants accepted this way must be told their training may not lead to ABMS board certification. If you take that route, ask the program and the relevant board before you commit. See [fellowship after residency](/wiki/licensure-career/fellowship-after-residency).

## Why it matters for your job

Many hospitals, health systems and insurers expect board certification or eligibility, often with a deadline after hire. Your first contract may include a requirement to become certified within a period, and may link pay or continued employment to it. Ask about it when you [review your first attending contract](/wiki/licensure-career/first-attending-contract).

## A simple timeline

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| During residency | Keep ECFMG certification valid, track your training dates and procedures, and meet your program's evaluations |
| Final year | Read your board's current eligibility rules and exam registration deadlines; confirm the program will attest your training |
| After graduation | Register for the exam according to the board's schedule; maintain a valid license, which boards typically require |
| After certification | Follow the board's maintenance of certification rules, which differ by board |

> [!IMG TIP] Treat board rules as the board's to define. Fees, deadlines and exam windows change, so use the board's website and a written answer from the board, not a forum post, when your eligibility is in question.
