For most IMGs, licensure in the US still follows US residency, but a growing number of states have created pathways that let some internationally trained physicians get a limited license without completing US graduate medical education first. The Federation of State Medical Boards (FSMB) tracks them, and its charts show the picture is changing quickly. This page explains what FSMB publishes and how to check a board yourself. We list no per-state rules, because they change and an error could cost you time and money.

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: FSMB's October 2026 pathways chart is the most recent summary we read. Do not rely on this page, a forum post or a prior-year summary for a specific state. Open the board's own page.

## What FSMB says

FSMB publishes two charts that matter here, and each carries a "last updated" date at the top.

**The pathways chart (October 2026).** It says that 25 states, plus Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands, have enacted legislation allowing qualifying internationally trained physicians to obtain an initial limited license and become eligible for full licensure after a provisional period, without completing accredited North American postgraduate training. It lists a further 17 states with pending or proposed similar legislation in the recent past, and at least two states that allow time-limited licenses that cannot be converted to full licensure. It also notes that nearly every jurisdiction has some "eminence" route (for example based on faculty status, research or extraordinary ability), which is outside the chart's scope.

**The IMG GME requirements chart (April 2026).** It reports that 36 states plus DC, Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands may grant credit toward accredited GME requirements to IMG applicants who meet certain criteria, such as specialty board certification, licensure in another state or ECFMG certification. It reports that 14 states plus Puerto Rico and the US Virgin Islands do not accept credit toward accredited GME requirements for IMG applicants. A subset of jurisdictions may license IMGs without any accredited GME, and the chart sets out the minimum number of accredited GME years each board requires.

For background on the standard route, see [state licensure for IMGs](/wiki/licensure-career/state-licensure-for-imgs) and [Step 3 and licensure](/wiki/licensure-career/step-3-and-licensure).

## What the pathways tend to have in common

States write their own laws, so conditions vary. The FSMB chart describes these as recurring features, and it summarizes each state's version separately:

- **Medical education and credentials**, with verification through ECFMG or a similar body, and often ECFMG certification.
- **Prior practice abroad**, often a license in the country where the applicant trained and a period of recent practice.
- **A job offer from an employer in the state**, sometimes limited to underserved or rural areas.
- **A provisional or limited license first**, with supervision by a licensed physician, followed by eligibility for a full license after a set period.
- **Legal immigration status** and English proficiency.
- **Board involvement**, such as an in-person appearance, annual renewal, reporting duties and rules about changing employers.

One enacted law the chart summarizes, for example, ties the provisional license to continued employment with a qualifying employer for several years. We do not generalize from one state, but it shows why the details matter.

The FSMB-linked Advisory Commission on Additional Licensing Models (ACALM) has published draft guidance and recommendations. It recommends, among other things, requiring ECFMG certification, a job offer and a period of provisional licensure with supervision and assessment. The guidance is advisory, and the commission says it is not an endorsement of any legislation. States are not required to follow it.

## What these pathways are not

### They are not board certification

Specialty board certification is a separate credential. The American Board of Medical Specialties (ABMS) says each member board sets its own training requirements, and its standards describe alternate routes as optional for each board. In FSMB's own April 2026 chart, the note on specialty board eligibility states that an applicant must complete an ACGME-accredited residency and hold a US or Canadian license to be specialty board eligible. See [board certification after residency](/wiki/licensure-career/board-certification-for-imgs). Without board certification, employers and payers may treat you differently, so ask about that before you commit.

### They are not a visa

A state license does not give you the right to work in the US. Several of the pathways described by FSMB require legal immigration status, and a job offer. That means you would need a sponsor and a visa category, and your options are the ones in [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b), [green card paths](/wiki/visas/green-card-for-physicians) and the [O-1](/wiki/visas/o-1-visa-for-physicians), where applicable. Whether a given pathway works with any visa is something to ask the board and an immigration attorney.

### They are not always operating yet

FSMB notes that rule-making authority and implementation details differ. Some enacted laws leave details to board rules that may not have been written yet, and the chart tracks bills that failed or are still pending. A law on paper does not guarantee that a board is accepting applications.

## How to check a state board

1. **Find the board** through FSMB's [contact a state medical board](https://www.fsmb.org/contact-a-state-medical-board) page. Use the board's own site, not a third party.
2. **Search the board's site** for terms such as "internationally trained physician," "provisional license," "alternative pathway" or "IMG."
3. **Read the statute and the rules**, not only the summary. The FSMB chart gives a citation for each state.
4. **Check the date.** Look for the last-updated date on the page and the effective date of the law.
5. **Write to the board** with specific questions: whether it is accepting applications, what documents it needs, how it treats your training and your exam history, and how long the process takes. Keep the reply.
6. **Ask about employers.** A job offer is often required, so ask whether employers in that state have used the pathway.
7. **Check how licensure affects other plans**, including visa, malpractice coverage, credentialing and board eligibility.

## Cautions

- **Do not pay anyone for a "guaranteed" license or placement.** No private firm can guarantee a license. Boards decide.
- **Do not assume portability.** A license under a pathway may be limited to a state, an employer or a type of practice, and may not transfer easily to other states. We did not find a general rule, so ask.
- **Do not drop your Match plans without a verified alternative.** For most applicants the Match remains the main route. See [is the US right for you](/wiki/start-here/is-the-us-right-for-you).
- **Keep your records.** Boards ask for transcripts, verification of licenses abroad, exam history and employment history. See [ECFMG certification overview](/wiki/ecfmg/ecfmg-certification-overview) for the certification side.
- **Expect change.** Check FSMB's charts again before you act. This wiki is reviewed on a schedule, and a law can change the week after.

This is general information, not legal advice. A health law or immigration attorney can review your situation if you are considering a pathway.
