Diagnostic Radiology is a small, competitive specialty in which IMGs fill a modest share of positions, and its structure adds a second task: finding a first clinical year. In NRMP's 2026 Main Residency Match, Radiology-Diagnostic offered 1,268 positions across all position types, and non-U.S. citizen IMGs matched to 111 of them.

## What Radiology is

Diagnostic radiologists interpret imaging (X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound, nuclear medicine and more) and advise the clinicians who order it. Patient contact is lower than in most clinical specialties and the work is screen-based. Interventional Radiology is a separate integrated pathway with its own NRMP listing and signal count (8 in AAMC's 2027 table), covered here only in passing. Decide on fit first ([choosing a specialty](/wiki/specialties/choosing-a-specialty)).

## How positions are listed in the Match

NRMP lists Radiology-Diagnostic under three position types. The 2026 figures below come from the Results and Data report.

| Position type | Positions offered | Filled | Non-U.S. citizen IMG matches | U.S. citizen IMG matches |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PGY-1 (includes the first year) | 156 | 148 | 19 | 8 |
| PGY-2 (advanced) | 1,083 | 1,066 | 91 | 30 |
| Physician (R), PGY-2 reserved for prior GME | 29 | 24 | 1 | 2 |

So about 85 percent of the positions are advanced positions that start the year after the Match, per NRMP's definition ([categorical vs preliminary](/wiki/specialties/categorical-vs-preliminary)). An advanced applicant ranks the radiology program and also needs a PGY-1 position somewhere, which is normally a preliminary or transitional year ([preliminary and transitional years](/wiki/application/preliminary-and-transitional-years)). Some institutions link their advanced program to a preliminary program inside the Match, but NRMP's pages do not say every program does, so ask each one.

> [!KEY] ACGME's requirements, which I could read only in summary form for the 2025 edition, describe a 48-month format that needs a prior clinical year with at least 36 weeks of direct patient care, and a 60-month format that includes the clinical year. Confirm the current wording on [ACGME's Diagnostic Radiology requirements](https://www.acgme.org/globalassets/pfassets/programrequirements/2025-reformatted-requirements/420_diagnosticradiology_2025_reformatted.pdf) and each program's website.

I did not find a separate early-match process for Diagnostic Radiology in the sources I opened, so I do not claim one exists. Check the current ERAS and NRMP calendars each year.

## The 2026 numbers

In NRMP's Charting Outcomes report for non-U.S. citizen IMGs, 1,419 applicants preferred Radiology-Diagnostic for 1,268 positions (1.12 applicants per position). Of the 175 non-U.S. citizen IMGs who preferred it, 102 matched to their preferred specialty, about 58 percent calculated from NRMP's counts, compared with 41.5 percent across all specialties. That figure counts a different group from the position-type table (it is based on the first-ranked specialty), so the two sets of numbers do not match exactly.

Among consenting non-U.S. citizen IMGs who preferred Radiology-Diagnostic, the median Step 2 CK score was 250 for those who matched (75 applicants) and 251 for those who did not (52 applicants). Matched applicants had a median of 5 contiguous ranks versus 1.5 for unmatched, and a median of 6 publications versus 4. These are medians for small groups, and NRMP warns that research counts are self-reported and unverified. They show that the Step 2 CK score alone did not separate the two groups, not what a program will require of you.

NRMP's 2026 Program Director Survey had only 31 responses from 228 Radiology-Diagnostic programs (13.6 percent), and I could not extract Radiology-specific IMG-openness figures from the narrative report, so I do not give them. Look at the survey's specialty tables on NRMP's page.

## What programs commonly look for

- **Exams.** Across all specialties in 2026, 92 percent of programs required IMGs to have taken Step 1 and 84 percent required Step 2 CK for interview consideration (NRMP). Of those requiring Step 2 CK, 32 percent required a target score for IMGs.
- **Radiology exposure.** Radiology electives, observerships or research in a radiology department, and a letter from a radiologist ([observership vs externship](/wiki/clinical-experience/observership-vs-externship-vs-hands-on)).
- **Research.** Radiology programs are often academic, and imaging research is a common way to show interest ([how programs view research](/wiki/research/how-programs-view-research)).
- **A credible first year.** Choose the clinical year deliberately, since programs read how you performed in it.

## Visa and screening realities

- **Sponsorship** is decided program by program and varies by visa type. Ask before applying ([J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b)).
- **Screening filters** such as exam attempts and years since graduation apply here as elsewhere ([program screening filters](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters)).
- **Signaling.** AAMC's 2027 table gives Diagnostic Radiology 6 gold and 9 silver signals and Transitional Year 12. See [program signaling explained](/wiki/application/program-signaling-explained) and AAMC's page for how gold and silver work.

## Strategy for IMGs

1. **Apply to Radiology and a first year in parallel.** Build a rank list that covers both, and do not assume a radiology match secures a first year, or the reverse.
2. **Interview broadly.** Contiguous ranks separated matched from unmatched applicants in NRMP's report.
3. **Get radiology-specific output**: a rotation or observership with a letter, and a small project.
4. **Plan the preliminary year for your goals.** A transitional or medicine year builds relevant clinical credibility. Visa and contract dates for a one-year position matter too ([visa timeline after the Match](/wiki/visas/visa-timeline-after-the-match)).
5. **Have a backup.** A second specialty needs its own statement and letters ([applying to more than one specialty](/wiki/application/applying-to-multiple-specialties), [backup specialties](/wiki/specialties/backup-specialties)).

## Red flags

- Applying without a plan for the clinical year.
- No radiology evidence, or a statement that treats the specialty as a lifestyle choice.
- Multiple exam attempts or long clinical gaps without explanation.
- Counting on SOAP: in 2026 only 8 PGY-1 and 17 PGY-2 radiology positions were unfilled in the algorithm.

> [!NOTE] Opinion: the structure rewards organized applicants. Treat the two-part search as one project, with a combined timeline and budget ([total cost of matching](/wiki/start-here/total-cost-of-matching)).
