Family Medicine (FM) has more IMG-accessible positions than most specialties, and in recent years not all of its seats fill in the main algorithm. That helps IMGs on average, but it does not mean a quick or certain match. This page lays out the 2026 numbers and what to do with them.

## The 2026 numbers

From NRMP's *Results and Data: 2026 Main Residency Match*:

| Measure | 2026 figure |
|---|---|
| Family Medicine positions offered | 5,491 |
| Share filled by non-U.S. citizen IMGs | 17.5% |
| Share filled by U.S. citizen IMGs | 10.7% |
| Overall fill rate in the algorithm | 83.6% |
| Positions placed into SOAP | 898 |
| Matched non-U.S. citizen IMGs in FM | 962 (14.3% of matched non-U.S. citizen IMGs) |

NRMP also notes that fill patterns across specialties, including Family Medicine, varied more in 2026 as positions expanded. Fewer FM positions filled in the algorithm than in Internal Medicine or Pediatrics, which means more seats were offered in [SOAP](/wiki/interviews-match/soap-explained).

> [!KEY] A lower fill rate means more openings, not lower standards at every program. Many programs fill their IMG-eligible seats with applicants they ranked highly, and visa sponsorship, location and prior US experience still decide the list.

## What programs commonly look for

This section is practice, based on NRMP's 2026 Program Director Survey and general advising.

- **Exams taken.** 92 percent of programs required IMGs to have taken Step 1 and 84 percent required Step 2 CK for interview consideration, regardless of result. Of programs requiring Step 2 CK from IMGs, 32 percent required a target score.
- **US clinical experience and a primary-care letter.** Many FM programs value US-based experience in outpatient or community settings.
- **Genuine interest in primary care and the community.** FM programs often look for a clear explanation of why family medicine, not just "I will take what I can get". Personal statements that read as generic backups can hurt.
- **Communication and fit.** NRMP's survey found interpersonal skills and interview interactions among the top ranking factors across specialties.
- **Stability in life plans.** Programs want residents who will stay for three years, so visa status and family situation matter.

## How IMGs commonly strengthen an FM application

1. **Show primary-care commitment.** Community clinics, continuity experiences, public health or underserved-population work all help.
2. **Get a letter from a family physician** who supervised you in a US setting, if possible.
3. **Apply broadly**, especially to community programs. NRMP's 2026 Charting Outcomes shows matched non-U.S. citizen IMGs had a median of 5 contiguous ranks in their preferred specialty versus 2 for those who did not match.
4. **Know your Step 2 CK position.** Across specialties, matched non-U.S. citizen IMGs had a median Step 2 CK of 248 versus 242 for unmatched, so scores still matter.
5. **Be clear about your visa.** Programs value early honesty. NRMP's Match Participation Agreement expects applicants to disclose visa requirements.

## Visa and exam realities

- Fewer FM programs sponsor visas than IMGs would like. Sponsorship type (J-1 only, H-1B, neither) is decided by each institution. Read [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b).
- You must complete ECFMG certification before starting residency ([ECFMG page](https://www.ecfmg.org/certification/)).
- Exam attempts and recency of graduation still count. See [program screening filters](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters).

## Red flags

- Multiple USMLE attempts or long unexplained gaps.
- Generic personal statements or letters.
- Applications that look scattershot, listing FM as a last resort with no primary-care signal.
- Poor interview communication.

## Backup planning and SOAP

Family Medicine is often a realistic second specialty for IMGs whose first choice is [Internal Medicine](/wiki/specialties/internal-medicine) or [Pediatrics](/wiki/specialties/pediatrics), but only if you can write an honest, specific application for it. In 2026, 898 FM positions entered SOAP; if you are SOAP-eligible and unmatched, FM programs may appear in the unfilled list. See [if you do not match](/wiki/interviews-match/if-you-do-not-match) and prepare documents before Match Week.

> [!WARNING] Do not rely on SOAP. It is a short, high-pressure process where IMGs compete against US graduates. In 2026, IMGs together accounted for 34.3 percent of positions filled in SOAP.
