Pediatrics has the highest preferred-specialty match rate among non-U.S. citizen IMGs in NRMP's 2026 report, but that is an average over a self-selected group, not a promise. It is a specialty where genuine interest in children and child-specific experience are visible parts of the application. This page summarizes the data and what programs tend to look for.

## The 2026 numbers

From NRMP's 2026 reports:

| Measure | 2026 figure |
|---|---|
| Categorical Pediatrics positions offered | 3,126 |
| Share filled by non-U.S. citizen IMGs | 21.9% |
| Share filled by U.S. citizen IMGs | 6.8% |
| Overall fill rate in the algorithm | 94.4% |
| Non-U.S. citizen IMGs matched in Pediatrics (categorical) | 684 (10.2% of matched non-U.S. citizen IMGs) |
| Non-U.S. citizen IMGs who matched to their preferred specialty, Pediatrics | 68.5% |

The share filled by non-U.S. citizen IMGs rose in each of the last several years in the table (11.9 percent in 2022 to 21.9 percent in 2026), according to NRMP's Table 8F. The same table shows the overall fill rate staying high. So the specialty is open to IMGs, but programs choose among many applicants.

> [!NOTE] The 68.5 percent figure counts non-U.S. citizen IMGs who consented to share data and who ranked Pediatrics first. It is not the same as the match rate in NRMP's headline table. See [IMG match rates by specialty](/wiki/specialties/img-match-rates-by-specialty) for definitions.

## What programs commonly look for

This is practice, drawn from NRMP's 2026 Program Director Survey and general advising.

- **Exams.** 92 percent of programs required IMGs to have taken Step 1 and 84 percent required Step 2 CK for interview consideration, regardless of result. Among programs requiring Step 2 CK from IMGs, 32 percent required a target score.
- **Interviews.** Pediatrics was among the specialties with the highest mean number of interview invitations sent and applicants interviewed in the survey, which suggests a larger interview pool per position than some specialties.
- **Child-focused experience.** Pediatric rotations, outpatient pediatric clinics, NICU or neonatal experience, school or community child-health work and pediatric research can all show commitment.
- **Communication and professionalism.** Pediatrics programs often emphasize teamwork and family communication. NRMP's survey found interpersonal skills the most-cited ranking factor across specialties (89 percent).
- **Letters.** A letter from a pediatrician who watched you work with children and families is typically the most useful.

## How IMGs commonly strengthen a Pediatrics application

1. **Get pediatric USCE** with a letter writer. See [how to get US clinical experience](/wiki/clinical-experience/how-to-get-usce).
2. **Show a trajectory.** Advocacy, child health volunteering and teaching experience support a believable "why pediatrics".
3. **Score as well as you can on Step 2 CK.** Across specialties, NRMP's 2026 data shows matched non-U.S. citizen IMGs had a median of 248 versus 242 for unmatched.
4. **Apply broadly.** The same report shows a median of 5 contiguous ranks for matched non-U.S. citizen IMGs versus 2 for those who did not match.
5. **Be specific in your personal statement.** Avoid generic statements about loving children. Describe particular patients or projects without breaching privacy. See [personal statement guide](/wiki/application/personal-statement-guide).

## Visa and exam realities

- Sponsorship policies vary widely. Some pediatrics programs sponsor J-1 only, some H-1B, some neither. Check each program and read [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b).
- Programs often require you to be ECFMG certified by the start of training ([ECFMG certification](https://www.ecfmg.org/certification/)).
- Recency of graduation and exam attempts are common screening filters. See [program screening filters](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters).

## Red flags

- Multiple USMLE attempts or unexplained gaps.
- No evidence of interest in children or no clinical experience with them.
- A personal statement that reads like a template.
- A rank list with very few pediatrics programs.

## Backup planning

- **Family Medicine or Internal Medicine** may be possible, but each needs its own letters and statement. See [Family Medicine](/wiki/specialties/family-medicine) and [Internal Medicine](/wiki/specialties/internal-medicine).
- **SOAP.** In 2026, NRMP reported 173 categorical Pediatrics positions entering SOAP. Prepare in advance ([SOAP explained](/wiki/interviews-match/soap-explained)).
- **Reapply** after strengthening the profile ([reapplicant strategy](/wiki/application/reapplicant-strategy)).

> [!WARNING] A strong preferred-specialty rate does not protect an application with weak exams, no US experience or visa limits. Ask current residents how their programs treat IMGs.
