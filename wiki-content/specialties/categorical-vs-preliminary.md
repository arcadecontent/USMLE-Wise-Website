A categorical position is a residency that takes you to board eligibility in one specialty. A preliminary position is a single year that does not. The difference decides whether you finish a program or need to match again after year one, so confirm the type before you apply to or rank any program.

## The position types NRMP uses

NRMP's definitions, as of its current help pages:

| Type | What it means (NRMP) |
|---|---|
| Categorical (C) | A PGY-1 position that provides the full training required for board certification in a specialty |
| Primary (M) | A PGY-1 position in Internal Medicine or Pediatrics with a training emphasis on primary care |
| Preliminary (P) | A one-year position, such as Medicine, Surgery, a Transitional Year or a specialty program, that provides prerequisite training for advanced positions |
| Advanced (A) | A specialty position that starts the year after the Match, after one or more years of required preliminary training |
| Reserved (R) | A PGY-2 specialty position that starts in the Match year, limited to physicians with prior graduate medical education |

NRMP also describes a "Joint A/P" feature in its R3 system that lets an institution link an advanced program with a preliminary program. Applicants who match to the advanced program are the only ones who can match to the linked preliminary program. NRMP's pages do not say how to rank unlinked programs, so ask each advanced program whether it provides a first year, links one, or expects you to find your own.

> [!KEY] Check the position type on every program listing before applying. "Internal Medicine" at one program may be categorical and at another preliminary only, and the career consequences are very different.

## Why both exist: two kinds of specialties

Some specialties start with PGY-1 categorical positions that include the first year (for example, Internal Medicine, Pediatrics, General Surgery and, for many positions, Anesthesiology and Neurology). Others offer advanced PGY-2 positions that need a separate first year. In the 2026 Match, Anesthesiology offered 306 PGY-2 positions and Neurology 233 alongside their PGY-1 positions (NRMP). See [Anesthesiology](/wiki/specialties/anesthesiology) and [Neurology](/wiki/specialties/neurology).

An advanced applicant therefore competes in two ranks: the advanced program and a first-year position. Preliminary and transitional years can be done in medicine, surgery or a transitional program. For the full application side, see [preliminary and transitional years](/wiki/application/preliminary-and-transitional-years).

## The risks of relying on a preliminary year

1. **No guarantee of a next position.** A preliminary year does not carry a promise of a categorical position at the same hospital. You apply again for PGY-2 or advanced positions in the Match.
2. **You apply while working.** You are completing a demanding intern year while applying and interviewing. Plan your leave and your letters ([reapplicant strategy](/wiki/application/reapplicant-strategy)).
3. **Short visa and contract horizon.** A one-year position is a one-year appointment. Visa sponsorship and the dates of your status depend on program and visa type, so confirm what happens at the end of the year ([visa timeline after the Match](/wiki/visas/visa-timeline-after-the-match)).
4. **Mismatch in specialty.** A medicine preliminary year does not make you a stronger applicant in an unrelated specialty by itself. Letters from your preliminary attendings help most when they come from the specialty you want.
5. **Program quality varies.** Ask current residents about workload and teaching, and ask where recent graduates went next.
6. **Opportunity cost.** Spending a year in a one-year position can delay a better-prepared reapplication. Compare it with research or additional USCE.

> [!WARNING] Never rank a program you would not attend. NRMP's September 2026 IMG newsletter says you must obtain a waiver to decline a matched position under the Match Participation Agreement.

## How it is listed and applied for

Applicants apply through ERAS (or ResidencyCAS for EM and OB-GYN) and register for the Match separately with NRMP. In NRMP's IMG newsletter the process is: register in R3, certify your rank order list by the deadline of March 3, 2027 for the 2027 Match, and meet ECFMG requirements by then. NRMP reports Surgery-Preliminary, Medicine-Preliminary and Transitional positions separately from categorical ones in its tables.

## A practical decision guide

- **You want categorical in your specialty:** apply to categorical programs first. Use a preliminary or transitional year only as a deliberate second tier ([backup specialties](/wiki/specialties/backup-specialties)).
- **Your specialty starts at PGY-2:** plan both ranks from the start, and check each advanced program's expectations for the first year.
- **You are unmatched:** a preliminary position through SOAP may be possible, but read the risks above before accepting ([SOAP explained](/wiki/interviews-match/soap-explained)).
- **Visa required:** confirm sponsorship for the preliminary year and for the following year separately.

> [!NOTE] Opinion: a preliminary year is a reasonable choice when you accept that it might be a standalone year, and a poor one when it is your only plan for a specialty you have not otherwise made competitive for.
