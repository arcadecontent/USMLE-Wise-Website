Neurology is a realistic target for some IMGs but not an easy one. In NRMP's 2026 Main Residency Match, non-U.S. citizen IMGs filled 17.4 percent of the 1,003 PGY-1 Neurology positions, down from 23.9 percent in 2025, and the specialty filled 99.6 percent of its positions. The sections below show what that does and does not tell you.

## What Neurology is

Neurology is the medical specialty that diagnoses and manages disorders of the brain, spinal cord, nerves and muscles, such as stroke, epilepsy, movement disorders and neuromuscular disease. Most training is adult neurology; Child Neurology is a separate specialty in the Match and is not covered here. Many neurologists subspecialize afterwards, and fellowship planning is part of the specialty culture.

Neurology offers two kinds of first positions in the Match. PGY-1 positions include the first year of training. PGY-2 "advanced" positions start a year later, after one or more years of preliminary training, which you arrange separately (see [categorical vs preliminary positions](/wiki/specialties/categorical-vs-preliminary)).

## The 2026 numbers

| Measure | 2026 figure (NRMP) |
|---|---|
| Neurology PGY-1 positions offered | 1,003 |
| PGY-1 positions filled in the algorithm | 999 (99.6%) |
| Share of PGY-1 positions filled by non-U.S. citizen IMGs | 17.4% (175 matches) |
| Share filled by U.S. citizen IMGs | 4.9% (49 matches) |
| Neurology PGY-2 positions offered | 233 (100% filled) |
| Share of PGY-2 positions filled by non-U.S. citizen IMGs | 16.3% |

The non-U.S. citizen IMG share of PGY-1 positions was 23.2 percent in 2024, 23.9 percent in 2025 and 17.4 percent in 2026, while positions grew from 878 to 1,003 over the same period. NRMP does not say why in the tables, so treat the change as a signal to watch rather than a trend with a cause.

NRMP's Charting Outcomes report for non-U.S. citizen IMGs counts applicants by the specialty they ranked first, with PGY-1 and PGY-2 positions combined. In 2026, 215 of 513 non-U.S. citizen IMGs who preferred Neurology matched to it. That is roughly 42 percent, calculated from the report's counts. It compares with 41.5 percent across all specialties in the same report.

> [!KEY] A 99.6 percent fill rate means almost no positions are left after the algorithm. Do not treat SOAP as a plan for Neurology.

## What programs commonly look for

- **Exams.** In NRMP's 2026 Program Director Survey, 92 percent of programs required IMGs to have taken Step 1 and 84 percent required Step 2 CK for interview consideration, across all specialties. Among programs requiring Step 2 CK from IMGs, 32 percent required a target score.
- **Step 2 CK.** In the 2026 Charting Outcomes report, consenting non-U.S. citizen IMGs who preferred Neurology and matched had a median Step 2 CK score of 252 (166 applicants), versus 244 for those who did not match (231 applicants). The groups overlap widely, so this is a pattern, not a cutoff.
- **Neurology evidence.** Neurology clinical experience, a neurologist's letter and some neurology-related research or case reports. NRMP's survey identifies specialty-specific letters among the key interview considerations (86 percent of programs, all specialties).
- **Interview performance.** Ranking decisions across specialties lean on interpersonal skills (89 percent) and interview interactions (84 percent).

NRMP's survey also notes that Neurology program directors showed high rates of endorsement for interviewing IMGs only, without elaborating. Specialty response rates in that survey were low (30 of 224 Neurology programs), so NRMP asks readers to interpret specialty results with caution.

## Visa and exam realities

- **Visas.** Sponsorship is decided program by program, and the type (J-1, H-1B or neither) varies. Confirm before applying, and read [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b). NRMP tells applicants they are responsible for confirming a program will sponsor their visa before certifying a rank order list.
- **Exam attempts and recency.** Attempts and years since graduation are common screens ([program screening filters](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters)).
- **ECFMG certification.** You must meet ECFMG's requirements by the rank order list certification deadline, which NRMP lists as March 3, 2027 for the 2027 Match ([ECFMG certification](/wiki/ecfmg/ecfmg-certification-overview)).
- **Signaling.** Neurology is on AAMC's list of specialties participating in program signaling for the 2027 ERAS season, with 8 signals. See [program signaling explained](/wiki/application/program-signaling-explained) and confirm the current table on AAMC's page.

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: AAMC's 2027 season lists Neurology with 8 signals. Participation can change by year, so check [AAMC's signaling page](https://students-residents.aamc.org/applying-residencies-eras/program-signaling-2027-myeras-application-season) before you plan.

## How IMGs commonly strengthen a Neurology application

1. **Get neurology-specific US clinical experience** and a detailed letter. See [how to get US clinical experience](/wiki/clinical-experience/how-to-get-usce) and [turning USCE into a letter](/wiki/clinical-experience/turning-usce-into-a-letter).
2. **Add relevant research** such as a case report, poster or clinical study. See [how programs view research](/wiki/research/how-programs-view-research).
3. **Apply broadly and interview widely.** Across Neurology in 2026, matched non-U.S. citizen IMGs had a median of 6 contiguous ranks in Neurology versus 2 for unmatched (NRMP Charting Outcomes, consenting applicants).
4. **Plan the first year early** if you are targeting advanced positions. Identify preliminary medicine or transitional programs that fit your visa and timeline ([preliminary and transitional years](/wiki/application/preliminary-and-transitional-years)).
5. **Score as high as you can** on Step 2 CK, since many programs screen on it.

## Red flags

- Several exam attempts, long gaps without clinical activity, or an old graduation year without a clear explanation.
- A personal statement that treats Neurology as a second choice or a fallback.
- Letters that do not describe your clinical performance.
- Applying to advanced positions without any plan for a first year.

## Backup planning

Pick a backup that you would genuinely accept, and build it as a separate, credible application. Internal Medicine is the most common companion for Neurology, since it also supplies a preliminary year and a neurology-adjacent career path. See [backup specialties](/wiki/specialties/backup-specialties), [applying to more than one specialty](/wiki/application/applying-to-multiple-specialties), and [internal medicine for IMGs](/wiki/specialties/internal-medicine). If you do not match, the options are in [if you do not match](/wiki/interviews-match/if-you-do-not-match).

> [!NOTE] Opinion: the drop in the IMG share in 2026 is a reason to apply to more programs and prepare an alternative, not a reason to rule Neurology out. One cycle's change is a weak basis for a career decision.
