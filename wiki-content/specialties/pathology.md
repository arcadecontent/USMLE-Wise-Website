Pathology is one of the specialties where IMGs fill a larger share of positions than in most, but it is small and nearly fully matched. In NRMP's 2026 Main Residency Match, non-U.S. citizen IMGs filled 26.7 percent of the 636 Pathology positions and U.S. citizen IMGs filled 7.9 percent, and 99.7 percent of the positions filled in the algorithm.

## What Pathology is

Pathology is the specialty that diagnoses disease by examining tissue, cells and body fluids and by directing laboratory testing. NRMP lists it as "Pathology" and training can combine anatomic and clinical pathology. Pathologists work mostly in hospital and reference laboratories, with less routine face-to-face patient care than in clinical specialties. That is an advantage for some applicants and a deal breaker for others, so decide on fit before competitiveness ([choosing a specialty](/wiki/specialties/choosing-a-specialty)).

## The 2026 numbers

| Measure | 2026 figure (NRMP) |
|---|---|
| Pathology positions offered | 636 |
| Positions filled in the algorithm | 634 (99.7%) |
| Share filled by non-U.S. citizen IMGs | 26.7% (170 matches) |
| Share filled by U.S. citizen IMGs | 7.9% (50 matches) |

The non-U.S. citizen IMG share was 27.0 percent in 2025, 27.2 percent in 2024 and 30.2 percent in 2023, so the share has been fairly steady near the high 20s in recent years, with a gradual decline from 2023.

In NRMP's Charting Outcomes report for non-U.S. citizen IMGs, 957 applicants preferred Pathology for 636 positions (1.50 applicants per position), and 169 of the 375 non-U.S. citizen IMGs who preferred it matched. That is about 45 percent, calculated from the report's counts, compared with 41.5 percent of non-U.S. citizen IMGs matching to their preferred specialty across all specialties.

> [!KEY] Because the specialty is small, a few positions either way changes the shares noticeably. Read one year's percentages as a snapshot, and look at several years in NRMP's tables.

## What programs commonly look for

- **Exams.** In NRMP's 2026 Program Director Survey, 92 percent of programs (all specialties) required IMGs to have taken Step 1 and 84 percent required Step 2 CK for interview consideration. Of those requiring Step 2 CK from IMGs, 32 percent required a target score.
- **Step 2 CK.** Among consenting non-U.S. citizen IMGs who preferred Pathology in 2026, the median Step 2 CK score was 244 for those who matched (128 applicants) and 235 for those who did not (156 applicants), per NRMP. The ranges overlap.
- **IMG openness.** In the same survey, NRMP's narrative says Pathology program directors most frequently endorsed that they often interview and rank both U.S. IMGs and non-U.S. IMGs (71 percent). Only 24 of 174 Pathology programs responded, so NRMP's caution about response rates applies.
- **Pathology exposure.** Rotations, observerships or research in pathology, and a letter from a pathologist who can describe your work.
- **Reasoned motivation.** Programs read for evidence that you understand the daily work, which involves microscopy, reports and consultation, rather than attraction to a "controllable lifestyle".

## Visa and exam realities

- **Visa sponsorship.** It is decided program by program, and not all pathology programs sponsor every visa type. Confirm early and read [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b).
- **Screening filters.** Exam attempts and years since graduation matter in pathology as elsewhere ([program screening filters](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters)).
- **ECFMG certification.** Requirements must be completed by the rank order list certification deadline, which NRMP lists as March 3, 2027 for the next Match ([ECFMG certification](/wiki/ecfmg/ecfmg-certification-overview)).
- **Signaling.** Pathology is on AAMC's 2027 list with 5 signals. See [program signaling explained](/wiki/application/program-signaling-explained).

## How IMGs commonly strengthen a Pathology application

1. **Get pathology exposure in the US** if you can: a rotation, observership or shadowing in a pathology department, ending in a specific letter ([observership vs externship](/wiki/clinical-experience/observership-vs-externship-vs-hands-on)).
2. **Do pathology research.** Case reports and small projects are common in pathology departments, and research is a frequent way IMGs show interest ([how programs view research](/wiki/research/how-programs-view-research)).
3. **Interview broadly.** Matched non-U.S. citizen IMGs who preferred Pathology had a median of 7 contiguous ranks versus 3 for unmatched, among consenting applicants in the 2026 report.
4. **Explain your choice.** Write a statement that shows you know what pathologists do.
5. **Raise your Step 2 CK** if you still have the option.

## Red flags

- Treating Pathology as a fallback and saying so, or applying with no pathology evidence.
- Multiple exam attempts, or long gaps in clinical activity.
- An application whose letters and experiences point to clinical medicine while the statement claims pathology.
- Weak English written communication, which matters in report-based work.

## Backup planning

- **A second specialty.** Pathology applicants often add Internal Medicine, but that requires a separate statement and letters ([applying to more than one specialty](/wiki/application/applying-to-multiple-specialties)).
- **Preliminary or transitional year.** It keeps you in the system, but it does not secure a later pathology position ([categorical vs preliminary](/wiki/specialties/categorical-vs-preliminary)).
- **Reapplying** with new pathology experience ([reapplicant strategy](/wiki/application/reapplicant-strategy)).

SOAP is a weak safety net here: Pathology filled 99.7 percent of positions in the algorithm, leaving very few openings.

> [!NOTE] Opinion: a relatively high IMG share is a reason to apply, not a reason to apply casually. Programs that regularly take IMGs also receive many IMG applications.
