OB-GYN is among the harder specialties for IMGs to enter. In NRMP's 2026 Main Residency Match, non-U.S. citizen IMGs filled 2.7 percent of the 1,638 PGY-1 Obstetrics and Gynecology positions and U.S. citizen IMGs filled 3.1 percent, and 99.9 percent of positions filled in the algorithm.

## What OB-GYN is

OB-GYN combines surgery with primary and reproductive care for women: obstetrics (pregnancy, labor and delivery), gynecologic surgery, and outpatient care. Residency involves heavy inpatient and procedural work, including call on labor and delivery. Applicants should be able to describe what attracts them to both the surgical and the continuity aspects of the field.

## The 2026 numbers

| Measure | 2026 figure (NRMP) |
|---|---|
| OB-GYN PGY-1 positions offered | 1,638 |
| Positions filled in the algorithm | 1,636 (99.9%) |
| Share filled by non-U.S. citizen IMGs | 2.7% (45 matches) |
| Share filled by U.S. citizen IMGs | 3.1% (51 matches) |

The non-U.S. citizen IMG share was 2.7 percent in 2022, 2.5 in 2023, 2.8 in 2024 and 2025, and 2.7 in 2026, so it has been steady and small. NRMP's Charting Outcomes report counts 2,140 applicants who preferred OB-GYN for 1,638 positions (1.31 per position), and 44 of the 134 non-U.S. citizen IMGs who preferred it matched. That is about 33 percent, calculated from the report's counts, against 41.5 percent for all specialties.

NRMP also lists a small number of OB-GYN preliminary (PGY-1 only) positions: 21 offered in 2026, 10 filled. They are too few to serve as a pathway.

> [!KEY] With 99.9 percent of positions filled in the algorithm, SOAP is not a realistic path into OB-GYN.

## How you apply: ResidencyCAS

For the 2027 Match, NRMP says OB-GYN and Emergency Medicine programs use ResidencyCAS, while other specialties use ERAS. The ResidencyCAS deadline for these programs was September 17, 2026, with applications available to programs on September 23 (per NRMP's 2027 calendar). The AMA reports that OB-GYN piloted ResidencyCAS in the 2024-2025 cycle. Check ResidencyCAS and APGO's applicant page for current fees, dates and IMG document steps, since this page could not verify them there. APGO's applicant guidance tells IMGs to begin by contacting ECFMG. If you also apply to another specialty, that application usually goes through ERAS, so you manage two systems ([ERAS token](/wiki/ecfmg/eras-token) covers the ERAS side).

OB-GYN does not appear in AAMC's list of specialties participating in ERAS program signaling for 2027. Any signaling inside ResidencyCAS is separate. Third-party sources describe it, but confirm with ResidencyCAS and APGO before relying on it. See [program signaling explained](/wiki/application/program-signaling-explained).

## What programs commonly look for

- **Exams.** In NRMP's 2026 Program Director Survey, 92 percent of programs (all specialties) required IMGs to have taken Step 1 and 84 percent required Step 2 CK for interview consideration. Among those requiring Step 2 CK from IMGs, 32 percent required a target score.
- **Step 2 CK.** Among consenting non-U.S. citizen IMGs who preferred OB-GYN in 2026, the median was 242 for those who matched (38 applicants) and 239 for those who did not (76 applicants), per NRMP. These are small groups with overlapping ranges.
- **US clinical experience in OB-GYN.** Rotations or observerships on labor and delivery and in gynecology, and a letter from an OB-GYN physician who can speak to your work. Check whether a program expects a specialty-specific standardized letter in ResidencyCAS.
- **Commitment and teamwork.** Interviews usually probe why OB-GYN, handling emergencies, and working through long call.
- **Sensitivity and communication.** The field involves intimate, high-stakes conversations.

## Visa and exam realities

- **Visas.** Sponsorship varies by program. Check each program's policy and read [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b).
- **ECFMG status.** NRMP's deadline for ECFMG requirements is March 3, 2027 for the next Match ([ECFMG certification](/wiki/ecfmg/ecfmg-certification-overview)).
- **Screens.** Exam attempts and years since graduation matter ([program screening filters](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters)).

## How IMGs commonly strengthen an OB-GYN application

1. **Get US OB-GYN experience and a letter** ([how to get US clinical experience](/wiki/clinical-experience/how-to-get-usce)).
2. **Add OB-GYN research** such as a case report or quality improvement project ([how programs view research](/wiki/research/how-programs-view-research)).
3. **Interview widely.** Matched non-U.S. citizen IMGs who preferred OB-GYN had a median of 3.5 contiguous ranks versus 2 for unmatched in 2026 (consenting applicants).
4. **Choose programs with a record of IMGs.** Use [identifying IMG-friendly programs](/wiki/application/identifying-img-friendly-programs).
5. **Aim for a strong Step 2 CK.**

## Red flags

- Multiple exam attempts, long gaps or an old graduation year without explanation.
- No OB-GYN experience in the US, or letters from non-OB-GYN physicians only.
- Missing the ResidencyCAS submission window because of ECFMG document delays.
- A single-specialty application with no backup.

## Backup planning

Given the small IMG share, plan an alternative from the start. A second specialty means a second application through ERAS with its own statement and letters ([backup specialties](/wiki/specialties/backup-specialties), [applying to more than one specialty](/wiki/application/applying-to-multiple-specialties)). [Internal Medicine](/wiki/specialties/internal-medicine) and [Family Medicine](/wiki/specialties/family-medicine) are common alternatives, but each needs a credible story. If you do not match, see [if you do not match](/wiki/interviews-match/if-you-do-not-match) and [reapplicant strategy](/wiki/application/reapplicant-strategy).

> [!NOTE] Opinion: because the share of IMGs has been flat and small for five cycles, an application that depends on OB-GYN alone is a high-risk plan, whatever your scores.
