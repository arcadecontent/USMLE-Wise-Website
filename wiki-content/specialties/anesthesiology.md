Anesthesiology is competitive for IMGs and a small share of its positions go to them. In NRMP's 2026 Main Residency Match, non-U.S. citizen IMGs filled 4.4 percent of the 1,865 PGY-1 Anesthesiology positions and U.S. citizen IMGs filled 3.0 percent, and every position filled.

## What Anesthesiology is

Anesthesiology covers perioperative care, pain control, airway management and critical care in the operating room and beyond. Training is procedure-heavy and acute, with fast decisions and teamwork with surgeons and nurses. Programs enter the Match in two forms: PGY-1 positions that include the first year, and PGY-2 "advanced" positions that start after a separate first year of preliminary or transitional training ([categorical vs preliminary](/wiki/specialties/categorical-vs-preliminary)).

## The 2026 numbers

| Measure | 2026 figure (NRMP) |
|---|---|
| Anesthesiology PGY-1 positions offered | 1,865 |
| PGY-1 positions filled in the algorithm | 100% |
| Share filled by non-U.S. citizen IMGs | 4.4% (82 matches) |
| Share filled by U.S. citizen IMGs | 3.0% (56 matches) |
| PGY-2 positions offered | 306 (100% filled) |
| Share of PGY-2 positions filled by non-U.S. citizen IMGs | 1.6% |

PGY-1 positions grew from 1,509 in 2022 to 1,865 in 2026. Over those years the non-U.S. citizen IMG share stayed between 4.0 and 4.7 percent, so growth in positions did not translate into a larger IMG share.

In NRMP's Charting Outcomes report, 2,988 applicants preferred Anesthesiology for 2,290 positions (PGY-1 and PGY-2 combined, 1.30 per position), and 89 of the 230 non-U.S. citizen IMGs who preferred it matched. That is about 39 percent, calculated from the report's counts, against 41.5 percent across all specialties.

> [!KEY] Every Anesthesiology PGY-1 position filled in the algorithm, so SOAP is not a realistic route into the specialty.

## What programs commonly look for

- **Exams.** In NRMP's 2026 Program Director Survey, 92 percent of programs (all specialties) required IMGs to have taken Step 1 and 84 percent required Step 2 CK for interview consideration. Among programs requiring Step 2 CK from IMGs, 32 percent required a target score.
- **Step 2 CK.** Among consenting non-U.S. citizen IMGs who preferred Anesthesiology in 2026, the median score was 255 for those who matched (76 applicants) and 247 for those who did not (108 applicants), per NRMP. Anesthesiology had one of the higher matched medians in the report, though the ranges overlap.
- **Program director openness.** NRMP's survey reports that Anesthesiology is among the specialties receiving the highest mean number of applications, and that all responding Anesthesiology directors "often" interview and rank U.S. MD seniors. Only 36 of 304 programs responded, so interpret with caution.
- **Anesthesia evidence.** Clinical exposure or research in anesthesia, perioperative or critical care, and a letter from an anesthesiologist.
- **Calm communication.** Interviews usually probe how you handle pressure, teamwork and decision-making.

## Visa and exam realities

- **Visas.** Sponsorship varies by program and by whether the position is PGY-1 or advanced. For advanced positions you also need a first-year program that sponsors your visa. Read [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b) and check each program.
- **Screening filters.** Exam attempts and years since graduation are common screens ([program screening filters](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters)).
- **ECFMG certification.** Requirements must be met by the rank order list certification deadline, which NRMP lists as March 3, 2027 for the next Match ([ECFMG certification](/wiki/ecfmg/ecfmg-certification-overview)).
- **Signaling.** Anesthesiology is on AAMC's 2027 list with 5 gold and 10 silver signals. See [program signaling explained](/wiki/application/program-signaling-explained).

## How IMGs commonly strengthen an Anesthesiology application

1. **Get US anesthesia exposure** and a detailed letter ([how to get US clinical experience](/wiki/clinical-experience/how-to-get-usce)).
2. **Add research** in anesthesia, perioperative medicine or critical care ([how programs view research](/wiki/research/how-programs-view-research)).
3. **Apply broadly.** Matched non-U.S. citizen IMGs who preferred Anesthesiology had a median of 4 contiguous ranks in 2026 versus 2 for unmatched.
4. **Choose between PGY-1 and advanced positions deliberately.** Advanced positions mean also securing a preliminary or transitional year ([preliminary and transitional years](/wiki/application/preliminary-and-transitional-years)).
5. **Aim for the highest Step 2 CK you can reach.**

## Red flags

- Multiple exam attempts, gaps in clinical activity, or old graduation year without explanation.
- Applying only to advanced positions without a first-year plan.
- Weak procedural or acute-care evidence for an IMG whose experience is entirely non-clinical.
- Professionalism concerns in letters or the MSPE.

## Backup planning

Anesthesiology programs are competitive and the specialty is a poor fit for casual dual applications. If you add a second specialty, use one where you can build a separate credible file, often [Internal Medicine](/wiki/specialties/internal-medicine) ([backup specialties](/wiki/specialties/backup-specialties) and [applying to more than one specialty](/wiki/application/applying-to-multiple-specialties)). A preliminary year may keep you in the US system but does not guarantee an anesthesiology position later. Reapplication is covered in [reapplicant strategy](/wiki/application/reapplicant-strategy).

> [!WARNING] Do not assume that a preliminary year at a program with an anesthesiology department leads to a position there. Ask each program directly what its prelim residents have done afterwards.
