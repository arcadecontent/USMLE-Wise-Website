Internal Medicine (IM) is the largest destination for IMGs, but a large destination is not an easy one. In the 2026 Main Residency Match, 52.8 percent of matched non-U.S. citizen IMGs obtained positions in Internal Medicine, and NRMP's Charting Outcomes report shows that Internal Medicine had one of the highest ratios of non-U.S. citizen IMGs per position among all specialties. Expect a crowded field, and plan accordingly.

## Where IM stands in the 2026 data

| Measure | 2026 figure (NRMP) |
|---|---|
| Categorical IM positions offered | 11,194 |
| Share filled by non-U.S. citizen IMGs | 30.8% |
| Share filled by U.S. citizen IMGs | 9.5% |
| Overall fill rate for the specialty | 95.2% |
| Matched non-U.S. citizen IMGs in IM (all IM types as counted by NRMP) | 52.8% |

The overall fill rate shows that nearly every categorical IM position was filled in the algorithm. Programs were not desperate for IMGs; they ranked the applicants they preferred.

> [!NOTE] Numbers are from the NRMP's 2026 tables for the matching algorithm. SOAP added further placements afterward (see [SOAP explained](/wiki/interviews-match/soap-explained)).

## What programs commonly look for

The following is practice reported in national program director data and general advising, not a rule.

- **Exam results.** In NRMP's 2026 Program Director Survey, 92 percent of programs required IMGs to have taken Step 1 and 84 percent required Step 2 CK for interview consideration. Of programs requiring Step 2 CK from IMGs, 32 percent required a target score. Program directors named Step 2 CK score, Step 1 pass and specialty-specific letters of recommendation among their main interview considerations.
- **No exam failures.** Many programs screen out or deprioritize multiple attempts. Check each program's requirements.
- **Recent graduation.** Many programs set a limit on years since graduation. The cutoff varies and is published on program pages.
- **US clinical experience and letters.** Strong US-based letters in internal medicine often matter. See [turning US clinical experience into a letter](/wiki/clinical-experience/turning-usce-into-a-letter).
- **Communication and professionalism.** For ranking, NRMP's survey found interpersonal skills (89 percent) and interview interactions (84 percent) among the most-cited factors.

## How IMGs commonly strengthen an application

1. **Maximize Step 2 CK.** NRMP's 2026 data for non-U.S. citizen IMGs shows a median Step 2 CK of 248 for matched applicants and 242 for unmatched ones across specialties, a modest but real gap.
2. **Get relevant US clinical experience.** Inpatient medicine experience with a faculty member who can write a detailed letter is more useful than a long list of brief observerships. See [how much USCE programs want](/wiki/clinical-experience/how-much-usce-programs-want).
3. **Apply broadly.** Matched non-U.S. citizen IMGs had a median of 5 contiguous ranks in their preferred specialty, versus 2 among those who did not match. See [how many programs to apply to](/wiki/application/how-many-programs-to-apply-to).
4. **Use program signaling** where it fits your list ([program signaling explained](/wiki/application/program-signaling-explained)).
5. **Research and quality improvement.** Useful for some programs, especially academic ones, but not a substitute for scores and clinical letters. See [how programs view research](/wiki/research/how-programs-view-research).

## Visa and exam realities

- Many IM programs sponsor J-1 only, some also sponsor H-1B, and some sponsor neither. The details change and vary by institution. See [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b) and ask every program.
- Programs that sponsor H-1B often require Step 3 to be passed before filing. Plan your exam timeline early.
- You must be ECFMG certified before you start residency. ECFMG explains the process on its [certification page](https://www.ecfmg.org/certification/).

## Red flags programs worry about

- Multiple attempts on a USMLE Step, or long gaps with no clinical or research activity.
- Letters that are generic or from non-clinical sources.
- A personal statement that does not explain why internal medicine, or why a US residency.
- Late or incomplete applications.
- Unexplained discrepancies in dates or experiences (NRMP's Match Participation Agreement expects complete, accurate information).

## Backup planning

- **Preliminary medicine** is a one-year position. It can keep you in US training but does not guarantee a later categorical spot.
- **Another primary-care specialty** such as [Family Medicine](/wiki/specialties/family-medicine) or [Pediatrics](/wiki/specialties/pediatrics) may be an option if you can build a credible application for it.
- **Reapply** with improved elements (see [reapplicant strategy](/wiki/application/reapplicant-strategy)).

> [!WARNING] No page can tell you your chance of matching. Use your own scores, timing and visa needs, and talk to current residents at programs you target.
