Emergency Medicine (EM) is a difficult specialty for IMGs to enter, and the data from recent cycles is unusually unstable. In NRMP's 2026 Main Residency Match, EM offered 3,198 PGY-1 positions, 140 went unfilled in the algorithm, and non-U.S. citizen IMGs filled 2.9 percent of positions while U.S. citizen IMGs filled 10.0 percent. Read the whole page before treating the unfilled positions as an opening.

## What Emergency Medicine is

EM is the specialty of acute, undifferentiated illness and injury, practiced in emergency departments in shifts rather than through long continuity relationships. Training is predominantly shift-based, with a mix of resuscitation, procedures and fast triage decisions. Applicants need to be comfortable with nights, crowded departments and uncertainty.

## The 2026 numbers and the cycle changes

| Cycle | PGY-1 positions | Fill rate in algorithm | Share filled by non-U.S. citizen IMGs | Share filled by U.S. citizen IMGs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 2,921 | 92.5% | 1.5% | 6.0% |
| 2023 | 3,010 | 81.6% | 2.0% | 9.6% |
| 2024 | 3,026 | 95.5% | 4.1% | 10.7% |
| 2025 | 3,068 | 97.9% | 4.3% | 10.3% |
| 2026 | 3,198 | 95.6% | 2.9% | 10.0% |

All figures are from NRMP's *Results and Data* tables for each year, as reproduced in the 2026 report. Two lessons follow. First, EM positions have grown by 277 since 2022, but filling them has varied sharply from year to year. Second, the non-U.S. citizen IMG share rose in 2024 and 2025 and fell back in 2026, so one cycle's openness did not last.

In 2026 NRMP's SOAP table shows 46 Emergency Medicine programs offering 141 positions, of which 128 filled, compared with 27 programs and 64 positions in 2025. CORD's 2026 IMG guide notes that historically very few, if any, IMGs have matched into EM through SOAP. Do not plan on it.

In NRMP's Charting Outcomes report, 80 of the 148 non-U.S. citizen IMGs who preferred EM matched to it. That is about 54 percent, calculated from the report's counts, but that group was small (148 non-U.S. citizen IMGs ranked EM first, among 3,224 applicants in all) and self-selected.

> [!KEY] A high match rate to preferred specialty reflects a self-selected group of IMGs who got SLOEs and interviews. It does not mean EM is easy to enter. Getting an interview is the hard part.

## How you apply: ResidencyCAS, not ERAS

For the 2027 Match, NRMP says EM and Obstetrics and Gynecology programs use ResidencyCAS, while all other specialties use ERAS. The ResidencyCAS deadline for EM and OB-GYN programs is September 17, 2026, with applications available to programs on September 23. ResidencyCAS has its own fee schedule, separate from ERAS. Confirm the current schedule and fees on ResidencyCAS and CORD, since this page could not verify them there. IMGs should start with ECFMG, which handles document uploads for ResidencyCAS applicants; check ECFMG's current instructions ([ERAS token](/wiki/ecfmg/eras-token) covers the ERAS side only).

EM does not appear on AAMC's ERAS signaling list for the 2027 season. CORD guidance for the 2026-2027 cycle reportedly allows applicants five signals, but confirm on CORD and ResidencyCAS before planning. See [program signaling explained](/wiki/application/program-signaling-explained).

## What programs commonly look for

- **A SLOE.** CORD's guide says EM program directors rank eSLOEs as the most important factor in offering interviews, and that IMGs should secure at least one, preferably two, from EDs with residency programs. In an older CORD survey of EM leadership cited in that guide, 78.9 percent said they would not consider an applicant without one. The guide warns that IMGs often cannot obtain SLOEs at their own hospitals and must seek US rotations.
- **Exams.** In NRMP's 2026 Program Director Survey, 92 percent of programs (all specialties) required IMGs to have taken Step 1 and 84 percent required Step 2 CK. CORD advises IMGs to have Step 2 CK results back by mid-September so both exams show when applications open.
- **Step 2 CK.** Among consenting non-U.S. citizen IMGs who preferred EM in 2026, the median was 243 for those who matched (60 applicants) and 236 for those who did not (53 applicants), per NRMP.
- **US experience and familiarity.** CORD notes that program directors may see IMGs as higher academic risk and are often unfamiliar with international schools.

## Visa and exam realities

CORD states that financial and visa factors affect hiring: some institutions sponsor only certain visa types, and the department may bear costs. Confirm each program's policy and read [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b). NRMP also notes that ECFMG requirements must be met by the March 3, 2027 rank order list certification deadline. See [ECFMG certification](/wiki/ecfmg/ecfmg-certification-overview) and [program screening filters](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters).

## How IMGs commonly strengthen an EM application

1. **Secure SLOEs early**, since these are the gate to interviews ([how to get US clinical experience](/wiki/clinical-experience/how-to-get-usce)).
2. **Target programs that have recently matched IMGs.** CORD suggests checking program match lists and tools such as EMRA Match and Residency Explorer.
3. **Interview broadly and rank many EM programs.** Matched non-U.S. citizen IMGs had a median of 4 contiguous ranks versus 1 for unmatched in 2026 (consenting applicants).
4. **Proofread everything.** CORD names spelling and grammar errors as a major pitfall.

## Red flags

- No SLOE, or SLOEs only from non-residency EDs.
- Multiple attempts, gaps, or scores below what the program screens for.
- Applying to EM alone with no backup, which CORD says most surveyed leaders recommend against.

## Backup planning

CORD's guide tells realistic IMG applicants to apply broadly in EM and also in other specialties, and not to count on scrambling into EM later. A second specialty means a separate ERAS application with its own statement and letters, since ResidencyCAS covers only the specialties that use it (the AMA notes dual applicants generally apply to other specialties through ERAS). See [backup specialties](/wiki/specialties/backup-specialties), [applying to more than one specialty](/wiki/application/applying-to-multiple-specialties), and [internal medicine for IMGs](/wiki/specialties/internal-medicine).

> [!WARNING] Unfilled EM positions in 2026 do not mean IMGs are being offered them. Who fills them, and whether programs consider IMGs in SOAP, is up to each program.
