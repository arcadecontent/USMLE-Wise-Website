There is no data that says USCE beats research or the reverse, so choose by your gaps: what your target programs ask for, what your application lacks, and what you can afford. If you can fund only one, put it where it fixes the biggest problem in your application, usually exam performance first, then the evidence a program asks for.

## What the data do and do not say

NRMP's 2026 Charting Outcomes report for non-U.S. citizen IMGs compares applicants who matched with those who did not (among those who consented to share data). For all specialties combined:

| Measure (medians, 2026) | Matched | Not matched |
|---|---|---|
| USMLE Step 2 CK score | 248 | 242 |
| Contiguous ranks | 5.0 | 2.0 |
| Research experiences | 3.0 | 3.0 |
| Publications | 3.0 | 3.0 |
| Work experiences | 3.0 | 3.0 |

Source: NRMP's report, Table 2 (all specialties combined). In individual specialties the pattern differs, and the groups are small. NRMP states that research counts are self-reported, not verified, and that quality may vary greatly. It does not report USCE in this table, so I cannot give USCE numbers, and I will not invent them.

What follows from this: research volume alone did not separate matched from unmatched applicants on average, while exam score and the number of programs ranked did. That is a reason to protect your Step 2 CK preparation and your interview count, and it is not proof that research is useless or that USCE is essential. For specialty-level data see [IMG match rates by specialty](/wiki/specialties/img-match-rates-by-specialty).

## What programs say

NRMP's 2026 Program Director Survey lists, among factors for selecting interviewees, "Demonstrated involvement and interest in research" and specialty-specific letters of recommendation. The narrative shows that Step 2 CK (89 percent) and specialty-specific letters (86 percent) were among the most common key considerations for interviews. It does not tell you which of these produces the most interviews for an IMG. Programs set their own USCE expectations ([how much USCE programs want](/wiki/clinical-experience/how-much-usce-programs-want)).

## A decision framework

Answer these in order.

### 1. Is an exam problem unresolved?

If you still need Step 2 CK or have a score you can improve, do that first. Experience does not usually compensate for a missing or weak exam, and programs filter on exam status ([program screening filters](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters)). Both USCE and research can wait a few months, and many people run them in parallel only if they have the capacity.

### 2. What do your target programs state?

Read ten program websites. If many list USCE, recency or a US letter as a preference or requirement, that outweighs general advice. If programs mention research or you target a research-heavy specialty, research earns more weight.

### 3. What gaps does your application show?

| Your situation | Likely priority |
|---|---|
| Recent graduate, no US clinical contact | USCE and a US letter |
| Graduated several years ago, clinically inactive | Clinical activity and recency, then a US letter ([years since graduation](/wiki/application/years-since-graduation)) |
| Strong clinical record, no research, academic specialty target | Research, ideally with a finished output |
| Few or no letters from physicians who know you | A placement or project that yields a detailed letter ([turning USCE into a letter](/wiki/clinical-experience/turning-usce-into-a-letter)) |
| Limited funds and a visa constraint | The cheapest credible option you can do legally |

### 4. What can you afford and do legally?

USCE costs vary widely ([USCE costs](/wiki/clinical-experience/usce-costs)), and research positions range from paid to unpaid to fee-based ([research fellowships](/wiki/research/research-fellowships)). Immigration status limits what you can do in the US, so check the visa rules before you plan ([B-1/B-2 for observerships](/wiki/clinical-experience/b1-b2-for-observerships)). Remote research needs no visa, which can be a real advantage.

### 5. What is your timeline?

ERAS has a fixed calendar ([ERAS timeline](/wiki/application/eras-timeline)). Work back from the date letters must be uploaded. A placement that ends too late to produce a letter in time, or a research project that cannot publish before you apply, may not help this cycle. A poster or accepted abstract may be realistic where a paper is not ([posters and conferences](/wiki/research/posters-and-conferences)).

### 6. Which produces a letter and a story?

In MyERAS you list up to 10 experiences and choose up to 3 as most meaningful, with a short description of each. Pick activities you can describe in specific terms. A short, well-supervised placement with a real letter may beat a long one that yields none. The same is true for a research project with a named mentor and a clear role ([ERAS experiences section](/wiki/application/eras-experiences-section)).

## Combining them

Some options give both: a research position in a clinical department that includes clinical exposure, or a clinical placement with a small project. Be careful about status and role: a research visa does not automatically allow clinical contact, so ask in writing ([research fellowships](/wiki/research/research-fellowships)).

## Mistakes to avoid

- Spending the budget on an expensive program with promises about outcomes. Nobody can guarantee a match or interviews ([USCE red flags and scams](/wiki/clinical-experience/usce-red-flags-and-scams)).
- Counting research volume instead of quality, or publishing in predatory journals ([predatory journals](/wiki/research/predatory-journals)).
- Choosing virtual experience as a cheap substitute without checking what programs say ([telehealth and virtual USCE](/wiki/clinical-experience/telehealth-and-virtual-usce)).
- Delaying your exam or application for another experience when the application is already adequate.

> [!NOTE] Opinion: a mentor who knows you well matters more than the label on the experience. If you can get one letter that describes your clinical reasoning and reliability, either path can deliver it.
