The fee a program charges for US clinical experience (USCE) is rarely the full cost. For an IMG travelling from abroad, the total includes the placement fee, a visa and flights, housing, food, insurance and sometimes onboarding requirements. Prices are set by each program or organizer, so there is no official schedule to quote. This page breaks down the categories and shows how to judge what you get for the money. For what USCE is, start with [USCE explained](/wiki/clinical-experience/usce-explained).

## The cost categories

| Category | What it covers | Who sets it |
|---|---|---|
| Placement fee | The program, institution or organizer charging for the observership, externship or rotation | The program |
| Administrative or application fee | Processing, onboarding, badge, sometimes a separate charge | The program |
| Visa and travel | A visitor visa application fee (see [State Department fees](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees.html)), flights, local transport | Government and market |
| Housing | Short-term rent, hostel or hotel, often in an expensive city | Market |
| Food and daily costs | Meals, phone, local transport | Your choices |
| Insurance | Health and travel insurance, sometimes required by the site | Site and insurer |
| Onboarding requirements | Immunization records, tests, background checks, training | The site |
| Time cost | Weeks away from study, work or income | You |

Which visa fits an observership is nuanced. See [B-1/B-2 visas for exams and interviews](/wiki/visas/b1-b2-for-interviews-and-exams) and verify the rules for your own situation.

## What the fee usually should include

Ask each program to put these in writing:

- Length, hours per week and the clinical setting.
- Who supervises you and how much interaction you will have with patients and teams.
- Whether the program provides an evaluation or letter, and from whom. See [turning USCE into a letter](/wiki/clinical-experience/turning-usce-into-a-letter).
- Any refund or cancellation policy. Many placements are non-refundable or partly refundable, so get the terms before paying.
- Whether housing or insurance is included.

## What you get: judging value

| Format | Typical cost pattern | What to weigh |
|---|---|---|
| Observership | Often charged, sometimes free | Limited hands-on, letter potential varies |
| Externship | Often charged | More involvement, sometimes more oversight |
| Hands-on experience | Rarer and more regulated | Check rules and eligibility |

See [observership vs externship vs hands-on](/wiki/clinical-experience/observership-vs-externship-vs-hands-on) for the definitions. Opinion: the best value is usually a placement where a physician will know you well enough to write a detailed letter, not the longest or the most expensive one.

> [!WARNING] High fees do not guarantee quality or a match advantage. Be careful with organizations that promise placements, letters or interviews in exchange for payment. Check recognition with programs you plan to apply to and look for written terms.

## Hidden and commonly missed costs

- Paying a middleman on top of the program's own fee.
- Visa refusal or delay leading to forfeited fees.
- Last-minute flights and housing.
- Onboarding items that arrive after you have paid.
- Repeating placements because the first did not yield a letter.
- Exam or application deadlines that collide with the placement dates.

## How to budget

1. List the programs and formats you are considering.
2. Request a written breakdown from each.
3. Add visa, travel, housing and insurance at realistic prices.
4. Divide the total by the number of weeks for a cost per week, and compare by what you gain.
5. Hold a reserve for changes.
6. Check how much USCE target programs expect, see [how much USCE programs want](/wiki/clinical-experience/how-much-usce-programs-want).

The wider budget is in [total cost of matching](/wiki/start-here/total-cost-of-matching).

## What is different for IMGs

US graduates get clinical experience through medical school. IMGs usually buy or arrange it, which makes USCE one of the largest and least standardized costs on the path. Most programs also look at recency and setting, so cost should be weighed against how the experience will read on your application.
