Programs receive many more applications than they can read in full, so most use filters and screening tools to decide which applications get a close look. For an IMG, a few filters decide a large share of outcomes, and many of them are published. Knowing them before you apply saves money and signals.

> [!NOTE] Specific filters and cutoffs are chosen by each program and change over time. What follows describes common categories and what AAMC says about ERAS tools, not any program's actual rule.

## What ERAS lets programs do (rules)

From the AAMC 2027 applicant guide:

- **Program signals:** programs can filter applicants by signal. AAMC advises that signals are not to be used in isolation as a screening tool, but some programs may use them in screening. In AAMC's survey of the 2026 cycle, 96% of responding programs reported using signals to help decide whom to interview.
- **Geographic and setting preferences:** programs can filter by division and setting. A program only sees a division preference if it is located in the division you chose, or if you chose no preference.
- **Experiences:** programs can sort experience types, focus areas and key characteristics to find applicants who align with their mission. In AAMC's data from the 2026 cycle, more than 94% of responding program directors used the most meaningful experiences when deciding whom to invite, and 70% used the experiences section in a mission-aligned review.
- **Specialty questions and scholarly work** provide additional sortable information.

## Common screening categories (practice)

These are widely reported categories, not official rules. Check each program.

| Filter | What it often looks like | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Exam results | Passing all required Steps, sometimes on first attempt, and sometimes a Step 2 CK threshold | Read the program's stated rule; apply only where you meet it |
| Exam attempts | Programs may limit or ask about attempts | Do not assume a failed attempt can be explained away after the screen |
| Years since graduation | A maximum number of years, set by program | Check before applying; see below |
| Visa status | Sponsors J-1, H-1B, both or neither | Check; see [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b) |
| US clinical experience | A requirement for some IMGs, sometimes recent and in a specific form | Match your experience to the stated form |
| ECFMG certification | Status required to be ranked or start | Track your certification timeline |
| Letters | Number and type, such as a US letter | Plan authors early; see [letters](/wiki/application/letters-of-recommendation) |
| Location and signals | Preferences and signals sent to the program | Use honestly |
| Application completeness | Missing documents delay or exclude review | Assign all documents; see [MyERAS walkthrough](/wiki/application/myeras-walkthrough) |

NRMP reports that national IMG match patterns vary by factors such as exam results and experience, which is the reason many programs screen on them. NRMP data show patterns, not a program's policy.

## How IMGs get screened differently

- **Volume and unfamiliarity.** Programs may know less about your school, so signals such as US letters, USMLE results and USCE carry more weight.
- **Visa constraints.** A program that does not sponsor your visa will not interview you whatever your scores.
- **Time since graduation.** Programs may treat a long gap as a risk and screen on it.
- **Exam history.** Failed attempts are visible on the USMLE transcript.

## What to do about a filter you fail

1. **Do not apply** to programs whose stated rule you clearly fail, unless you have a direct written exception from the program.
2. **Ask** if the rule is unclear. Programs sometimes allow exceptions that the website does not mention, but never assume.
3. **Find alternative programs** whose rules you meet. See [identifying IMG-friendly programs](/wiki/application/identifying-img-friendly-programs).
4. **Fix what you can:** USCE, letters, research, a missing Step 3, or a more complete application are changeable. Past exam attempts are not.
5. **Explain only what you must.** For gaps and attempts, give a short factual note rather than a long defence.

> [!IMG TIP] A filter is not a judgment of you as a doctor. It is a time-saving rule. Your task is to find the programs where the filter is set to include you, and make the rest of your application strong enough to be read.

## Using this when you plan

- **Program list:** apply the filters yourself first. See [building your program list](/wiki/application/building-a-program-list).
- **Signals:** do not spend them on programs you cannot pass. See [signaling](/wiki/application/program-signaling-explained).
- **Geographic preferences:** choose honestly; programs outside the selected division will not see your answer, and some specialties do not receive these answers at all in 2027.
- **Experiences:** tag type, focus and characteristic with care because programs sort by them. See the [MyERAS walkthrough](/wiki/application/myeras-walkthrough).

## What not to do

- Do not misstate dates, attempts or experience to pass a filter. Information is checked and certified.
- Do not tag experiences with categories that do not fit.
- Do not assume a filter means a program is hostile to IMGs. Many filters are applied to everyone.
- Do not rely on forum cutoffs; use program pages.
