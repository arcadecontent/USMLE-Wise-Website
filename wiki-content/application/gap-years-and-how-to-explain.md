A gap on your application is a question a reader will ask, and **the best response is a short, truthful answer plus evidence of what you did next.** Programs do not expect an unbroken timeline from IMGs. They do want the dates to add up and the recent months to show you are still clinically engaged.

> [!NOTE] This page is about explaining time away from medicine or training. It is not advice to alter, shift or omit dates. See "What you must not do" below.

## What programs actually look at

NRMP's 2026 Program Director Survey lists "evidence of continuous medical education without gaps" among the factors programs were asked about when selecting applicants for interview. The survey does not tell us how heavily any single program weighs it, and the 2026 response rate was 12.7% of programs surveyed, so read it as a sign that gaps are on programs' minds, not as a rule.

In practice, readers tend to separate two situations:

| Situation | How it is often read |
|---|---|
| Gap during medical school | A question about progress and the MSPE; the dean's letter will usually describe it |
| Gap between graduation and application | A question about clinical currency, especially for older graduates |
| Gap after a failed attempt or a no-match | A question about whether you used the time well |
| Gap with recent clinical work, research or teaching | Often a smaller concern |

This table is our summary of common practice, not an official classification. Each program decides for itself, and some set a maximum time since graduation. See [years since graduation](/wiki/application/years-since-graduation).

## What ERAS asks you directly

The MyERAS application has an "Interruptions or Extensions" section. Per the AAMC 2027 guide, it asks two yes or no questions, and a "Yes" needs a description:

- Academic extensions, leaves, gaps or breaks in your educational program due to repeated or remediated coursework.
- Professional extensions, leaves, gaps or breaks due to professionalism sanctions or other adverse actions by your medical school or its parent institutions.

The guide adds that the section is not meant to ask about your health, disability or family status. Other gaps appear indirectly, through the dates in Education, Experiences and your exam transcript. Review each as a reader would: do the dates leave an unexplained hole?

## Write the explanation in layers

You rarely need a long defense. Use the lightest layer that answers the question.

1. **Dates in the Experiences section.** Fill the gap with what you actually did: clinical work, research, teaching, caregiving, exam preparation. Be specific about role and dates. See [writing the Experiences section](/wiki/application/eras-experiences-section).
2. **A line in the personal statement or the MSPE-related description,** only if the gap is large or the reason is part of your story. Two or three sentences: what happened, what you did, what is different now. See the [personal statement guide](/wiki/application/personal-statement-guide).
3. **A prepared interview answer.** You will be asked. Practise a calm 30 to 45 second version. See [common interview questions](/wiki/interviews-match/common-interview-questions).

### A structure that works

- **State the fact plainly:** "After graduating in [year], I spent [period] preparing for and taking Step 2 CK."
- **Say what you did:** the clinical, research or work activity, with the dates.
- **Show the present:** what you do now that keeps your skills current.
- **Stop.** Do not apologize at length, blame anyone, or over-explain.

## Common reasons and how to handle them

| Reason | Handling |
|---|---|
| Exam preparation or retakes | State it; show the result and what you changed. See [failed attempts](/wiki/application/failed-attempts-on-application) |
| Visa, ECFMG or document delays | State it briefly; show productive activity during the wait |
| Caring for family | Say so in a line; you do not need to give private detail |
| Your own illness | You choose how much to share; a short statement is enough, and ERAS does not ask |
| Work outside medicine | Describe skills and dates; link to why you are returning |
| Pregnancy or parental leave | A short factual line is enough |
| Unsuccessful earlier application | Show what you changed. See [reapplicant strategy](/wiki/application/reapplicant-strategy) |

## Fix what you can: be clinically current

For a gap after graduation, the most persuasive evidence is recent clinical activity. Recent US clinical experience with a letter, clinical work in your home country, or a documented role in a hospital are all more reassuring than a gap filled only with study. See [how much USCE programs want](/wiki/clinical-experience/how-much-usce-programs-want) and [turning USCE into a letter](/wiki/clinical-experience/turning-usce-into-a-letter).

NRMP's own advice in its IMG Charting Outcomes is that applicants do not need publications or research to match, so research is a supplement here, not a replacement for clinical activity. See [presenting research](/wiki/application/research-on-your-application).

> [!IMG TIP] Build a one-page timeline of every month since graduation, with what you did and a document that can prove it (a letter, a certificate, a contract). It makes ERAS entry accurate and gives you the facts for any interview answer.

## What you must not do

- **Do not shift dates, round them to hide a gap, or fill a gap with activity that did not happen.**
- Do not list an observership or job you cannot document.
- Do not claim a different reason than the true one.

> [!WARNING] You certify your ERAS application, and AAMC states that it investigates allegations of fraudulent or unethical application behavior. Misrepresentation can lead to serious consequences, including problems well after the Match. An honest gap is explainable; a hidden one that surfaces later is not.

## If the gap is long

If your gap is long, treat the rest of the file as the response: recent clinical work, strong letters, a clean exam record and a realistic program list built on programs that publish no strict limit. Be honest that some programs will screen on time since graduation. See [how programs screen](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters) and [am I too old or too late](/wiki/start-here/am-i-too-old-or-too-late).
