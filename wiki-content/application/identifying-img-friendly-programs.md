A program is IMG-friendly when the evidence shows it **regularly interviews and ranks IMGs, accepts the visa you need, and publishes requirements you meet**. No official label exists, so you decide by checking evidence rather than trusting a list on a forum or a vendor site.

## Why labels are unreliable

"IMG-friendly" lists on the internet are often out of date, copied from each other, or sold. Programs change policy between seasons. Even FREIDA and program websites disagree: a peer-reviewed study of pediatric programs found the database often did not match the program websites on whether a program would interview applicants who required a J-1 or H-1B visa. Your decision should therefore rest on several sources plus a direct answer.

## Evidence to look for

### On the program's own website

| Signal | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| Resident roster with medical schools | Current residents who are IMGs, including from schools like yours, is the clearest sign |
| Stated visa policy (J-1 only, H-1B accepted, none) | Whether you can be hired at all |
| Explicit USMLE rules (minimums, attempts, Step 3 for H-1B) | Whether you pass the first screen |
| "Years since graduation" limit | Many programs set one; check before applying |
| US clinical experience requirement | Often required in some form |
| Statement on IMG or international applicants | A positive statement helps, but its absence is not proof |
| Interview and ranking statistics, if posted | Counts of IMG interviews are the best evidence |
| Program director or leadership who trained abroad | Weak signal, but sometimes relevant |

### In databases and data

- **FREIDA** (AMA) lets you search accredited programs and view details such as visa information and the share of international graduates, depending on what each program reports. It is a useful first screen but not a final authority.
- **ACGME** public program information confirms that a program is accredited and shows its sponsoring institution.
- **NRMP Charting Outcomes** and Main Match reports show IMG match patterns by specialty. These are national or specialty-level facts, not program policies, so use them to choose specialties and set expectations.
- **AAMC ERAS statistics and Residency Explorer** show applicant and program data that help you judge your competitiveness. See [building your program list](/wiki/application/building-a-program-list).

### In other people

- Residents from your country or school who trained at the program.
- Your school's alumni network.
- Mentors who have worked with IMGs through the Match.

Their experience is useful for context but is not the basis for a rule. Always compare it with what the program publishes.

## A quick scoring approach (opinion)

For each candidate program, give a simple mark for each question:

1. Does the roster include IMGs, ideally more than one per class?
2. Does it state it sponsors the visa you need?
3. Are its published exam and graduation-year rules met by you?
4. Does it publish a USCE requirement you meet?
5. Has the coordinator confirmed these in writing?

Programs with five yeses go to your target or safer tier. Programs with one or more unknowns need a message before you spend an application on them.

## How to verify with the program

Visa and exam rules are exactly the details that change. Do not guess.

1. **Read the program website first.** Ask only about what is not answered there.
2. **Find the right contact:** usually the program coordinator, listed on the program page or FREIDA.
3. **Send a short, specific email.** State your status in one line (graduation year, exams passed, visa type needed) and ask the one or two questions you need answered, such as whether the program sponsors J-1 or H-1B for incoming residents and whether it has any exam or attempt requirements.
4. **Be polite and brief.** Do not ask whether they will "consider" you in general.
5. **Keep the answer** with the date, and record it in your spreadsheet.
6. **Do not rely on a reply from an unofficial source**, such as a recruiter or a forum post.

> [!WARNING] Do not send mass-copy emails that are obviously identical or that ask for special treatment. Programs receive large numbers of emails during application season, and a poor email can create a bad first impression.

## What to verify specifically

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Which visa types does the program sponsor? | Some sponsor only J-1; some also H-1B; some neither. See [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b). |
| Are there USMLE score or attempt rules? | A failed attempt may be an automatic screen at some programs. |
| Is Step 3 required for H-1B? | Some programs require it before they will file. |
| Is there a limit on years since graduation? | A common hard filter. |
| What US clinical experience do you require, and must it be recent? | Rules differ in length and in what counts. See [how much USCE programs want](/wiki/clinical-experience/how-much-usce-programs-want). |
| Do you require an in-person or hands-on rotation? | Observerships may not count. |
| Do you use program signals or other filters? | Affects how you use signals. See [signaling](/wiki/application/program-signaling-explained). |

## Red flags

- A program with no IMGs in recent classes and no IMG statement.
- A website that is out of date or has contradictory visa statements.
- Third-party sites claiming they have "inside information" for a fee.
- Promises of guaranteed interviews or matches.

> [!IMG TIP] Programs that are friendly to IMGs usually make their requirements easy to find. Hard-to-find or inconsistent information is a reason to ask before you apply, not a reason to assume.

## After you have the evidence

Place the program in a tier and decide whether to spend a signal on it. See [how programs screen applications](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters) for what you may encounter, and the [program list method](/wiki/application/building-a-program-list) for how to organize it.
