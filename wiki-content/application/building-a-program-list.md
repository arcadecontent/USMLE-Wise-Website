The best program list for an IMG is built by **eliminating programs that cannot take you first**, then sorting what remains by fit and likelihood. Doing it in that order saves money and protects your signals. This method works for any specialty.

> [!NOTE] This is a practical method, not an official procedure. The data sources it points to are real, but the tiers and rules of thumb are opinion.

## Step 1: Write down your profile

Before opening a database, write one page with:

- Specialty (and a backup specialty, if relevant). See [choosing a specialty](/wiki/specialties/choosing-a-specialty).
- USMLE results and every attempt. Include Step 3 status.
- Year of graduation and school.
- US clinical experience: where, how long, hands-on or observership, and any letters.
- Research and publications.
- Visa need: J-1, H-1B or no visa needed.
- Location limits: where you can and cannot live, family ties.

This page is what you will compare against each program's published requirements.

## Step 2: Gather the hard requirements

Hard requirements are the facts that decide whether a program can say yes at all.

| Requirement | Where to find it |
|---|---|
| Accepts IMGs at all | Program website, FREIDA |
| Visa types sponsored (J-1, H-1B) | Program website, FREIDA, email |
| Minimum USMLE rules and attempt rules | Program website |
| Maximum years since graduation | Program website |
| USCE (US clinical experience) required, and in what form | Program website |
| Number of letters and who must write them | Program website, ERAS |
| Application deadline and interview dates | Program website |

Many of these vary widely. See [how programs screen applications](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters) for the filters you are likely to meet and [identifying IMG-friendly programs](/wiki/application/identifying-img-friendly-programs) for how to check.

## Step 3: Search and collect candidates

Use more than one source, because each has gaps:

- **FREIDA** (AMA) lists accredited programs with filters and program details. Treat it as a starting point; a published study of pediatric programs found its information often disagreed with program websites.
- **Program websites**, which are usually the most current.
- **ACGME's** public program lists confirm accreditation.
- **AAMC Residency Explorer** and ERAS statistics show how applicant profiles compare with matched applicants, and AAMC lists these among the tools to use when deciding where to apply.
- **NRMP Charting Outcomes** for IMGs shows how factors such as scores, research and USCE relate to match results.
- **Your network:** alumni of your school who matched, senior residents and mentors.

Put every candidate in a spreadsheet with columns for each hard requirement, source and date you checked it.

## Step 4: Remove programs that cannot take you

Delete or park any program where:

- It does not accept IMGs.
- It does not sponsor your visa type.
- It has an exam, attempt or graduation-year rule you do not meet.
- It requires US experience you do not have and cannot get in time.
- Information is missing and the program does not answer your email.

Do this honestly. A program that "might" interview you but publishes a rule you fail will probably filter you out.

## Step 5: Tier the remaining programs

A common approach is three tiers (our opinion):

| Tier | Description | Share of list (rough idea) |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | Competitive, strong reputation, or few IMGs in recent classes | Small |
| Target | Meets your profile and has taken IMGs with similar profiles | Largest |
| Safer | Clearly welcoming to IMGs and your requirements are comfortably met | Meaningful |

Evidence for tiering comes from program websites (resident rosters and countries of medical school), your own comparison against published requirements, and conversations with residents. Do not rely only on prestige.

## Step 6: Add connection and logistics

For each remaining program, note:

- Any connection: you rotated there, your letter writer trained there, an alumnus is a resident.
- Location and cost of living, visa processing time, ability to travel to interviews.
- Whether the program participates in signaling (you cannot signal non-participating programs).

These notes feed your decisions on [signals](/wiki/application/program-signaling-explained).

## Step 7: Decide the size

The right number depends on your profile and specialty. NRMP's applicant survey shows that unmatched applicants reported applying to more programs than matched applicants across all applicant types, which means application count alone does not decide outcomes: fit does. See [how many programs to apply to](/wiki/application/how-many-programs-to-apply-to) for a method.

## Step 8: Review before you submit

- Check every program again for changes to rules and deadlines.
- Ask a mentor in your specialty to review the list and tiers.
- Make sure every program has a letter set and personal statement assigned.
- Confirm signals are on participating programs you applied to.

> [!IMG TIP] Keep the spreadsheet after you submit. You will use it for interview scheduling, thank-you messages and, if needed, the reapplication plan. See [reapplying](/wiki/application/reapplicant-strategy).

## Common mistakes

- Starting from a list of "top" programs instead of from your requirements.
- Trusting a single database without verifying.
- Adding programs "just in case" that clearly screen out your profile.
- Not recording the date you checked information, so you cannot tell what is stale.
- Applying broadly in several specialties without a plan for documents and signals.
