If you did not match, the most useful thing you can do is **diagnose where the application failed before you change anything**. Reapplying with the same application, the same program list and the same signals usually repeats the result. A good plan identifies the specific bottleneck and fixes that.

> [!NOTE] This page describes strategy, not guarantees. Outcomes depend on specialty, profile and the year. For the immediate steps after a no-match, see [if you do not match](/wiki/interviews-match/if-you-do-not-match).

## Step 1: Diagnose where you were lost

Your season gives you data. Sort it into one of these patterns (our framework):

| What happened | Most likely problem | Focus on |
|---|---|---|
| Very few or no interview invitations | Screening: your application did not pass filters or was not read | Eligibility, list quality, letters, USCE, exam record, signals |
| Several interviews but no match | Interview performance or rank list | Interview practice, communication, rank list size |
| Interviews only at programs outside your specialty plan | Specialty fit | Specialty choice or a preliminary year |
| Applied late or with missing documents | Process | Timeline and documents |

Ask for feedback. Some programs will give brief feedback, particularly where you rotated or have a contact. Do not argue with the response; collect it.

## Step 2: Audit the application honestly

Go section by section through your MyERAS application, ideally with a mentor who has read applications. Use the [MyERAS walkthrough](/wiki/application/myeras-walkthrough) as a checklist.

- **Exams:** is the record clean? A new pass on a Step can strengthen the file, but exam attempts cannot be changed.
- **Graduation year:** each year adds distance from graduation and some programs screen on it. See [how programs screen](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters).
- **US clinical experience:** recent, hands-on and with a letter is what most programs ask for. See [how much USCE programs want](/wiki/clinical-experience/how-much-usce-programs-want).
- **Letters:** were they specific and from people who saw you work? Replace weak ones.
- **Experiences:** were the descriptions clear and did the most meaningful entries show reflection? See [writing the Experiences section](/wiki/application/eras-experiences-section).
- **Personal statement:** was it specific to the specialty? See the [personal statement guide](/wiki/application/personal-statement-guide).
- **Program list:** how many programs could actually consider you? See [building your program list](/wiki/application/building-a-program-list).
- **Signals:** were they spent on realistic programs? See [signaling](/wiki/application/program-signaling-explained).

## Step 3: Decide what to change

Prioritize changes that are **real and visible to programs**, not cosmetic.

1. **Eligibility problems first.** If your list contained many programs you did not qualify for, fixing the list may change the result more than any other improvement.
2. **Add new evidence.** Recent US clinical experience with a strong letter, research with a defined role, or a meaningful completed Step.
3. **Rewrite, don't recycle.** Update experiences and the statement to reflect the year since the last application. Programs may see that you have applied before, so show growth rather than repeating the old text.
4. **Rethink specialty or pathway.** If your specialty was out of reach for your profile, consider a related specialty or a preliminary or transitional year as a stepping stone, understanding that these do not guarantee a later categorical position.
5. **Widen carefully.** NRMP's 2025 applicant survey found unmatched applicants of every type reported applying to more programs than matched ones, so widening the list alone is not a fix. Widen where programs can actually consider you.

## Step 4: Fill the gap year with purposeful work

A year of nothing visible is the weakest position. Useful activities (practice, not requirements):

- Structured US clinical experience, with a clear role and supervisor who can write a letter.
- Research that can produce something concrete: an abstract, a poster, a manuscript in progress, with your role clearly defined.
- Teaching or quality-improvement work that adds to your experiences.
- Exam steps you still need, in the order your timeline allows.

Keep a record of dates, supervisors and outputs now, because you will need it in the Experiences section.

## Step 5: Handle the questions you will be asked

Programs and interviewers may ask what you did since the last cycle. Prepare a calm, short, factual account: what happened, what you changed and what you learned. Avoid blaming programs or the system. For interview preparation, see [common interview questions](/wiki/interviews-match/common-interview-questions).

## Step 6: Reuse what ERAS lets you reuse

The 2027 applicant guide lists a request status "Imported" for letters brought in from a previous season. That means previously uploaded letters can be imported in some cases. Check the current guide for how it works, and decide whether older letters still serve you. The rest of the MyERAS application needs to be rebuilt or updated.

## Step 7: Reset the timeline and budget

Work backward from the opening of ERAS using the [ERAS timeline](/wiki/application/eras-timeline). Decide your realistic budget early using the guidance in [how many programs to apply to](/wiki/application/how-many-programs-to-apply-to). Starting the cycle with a clear plan is a visible difference to programs.

> [!IMG TIP] Ask two or three people who have seen applications to review your file independently. If they point to the same weakness, that is probably your bottleneck.

## What to avoid

- Reapplying immediately with the same documents.
- Paying for services that promise a match.
- Letting the year pass without new, verifiable evidence.
- Hiding prior application attempts if a program or the application asks.
- Making a major strategic change (specialty, country plan) without testing it against the data.
