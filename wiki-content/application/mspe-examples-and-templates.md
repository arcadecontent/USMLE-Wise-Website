The most useful MSPE examples are annotated ones that follow the six sections AAMC recommends, because they show you what a complete Medical Student Performance Evaluation contains and what a program reader looks for in each part. You cannot write or edit your own MSPE, so there is no template for you to fill in. Your school writes it, and your job is to understand it, request it on time and make sure the rest of your application agrees with it.

If your school does not issue an MSPE in this format, or issues none, the second half of this page is for you: what to ask your school, how to ask (with an email template), what to do in ERAS and what to confirm with ECFMG. The [MSPE and transcripts for IMGs](/wiki/application/mspe-and-transcripts-for-imgs) page covers how school documents reach ERAS. This page adds the examples, the annotation and the practical requests.

> [!WARNING] Every MSPE excerpt on this page is an **illustrative composite written by the USMLE Wise team**. None comes from a real school or a real student, and square brackets mark placeholders. These are not templates for you to submit. **Never write, alter or submit your own MSPE in place of your school's.** ERAS runs an Integrity Promotion Program that investigates allegations of fraudulent or unethical behavior, and misrepresenting a document can follow you well beyond one Match season.

## What AAMC recommends an MSPE contain

AAMC's recommendations describe the purpose of the MSPE as an honest and objective summary of a student's attributes, experiences and academic accomplishments, based as far as possible on verifiable information, with comparisons to the student's institutional peers where possible. AAMC says plainly that the MSPE is a summary letter of evaluation, not a letter of recommendation. The recommendations are guidance to medical schools, not a rule you can enforce, and they were written for US schools. Many international schools follow them only in part.

AAMC's MSPE page says a broader review of the MSPE is underway, so check that page for the current template before you rely on the structure below. The six sections, as the recommendations describe them, are in this table.

| Section | What AAMC says it contains | What a program reader takes from it (opinion) |
|---|---|---|
| Identifying information | The student's legal name and year in school, and the name and location of the medical school | Who you are and where you trained |
| Noteworthy characteristics | A short bulleted list of the most salient characteristics, which can include significant challenges or hardships. It is not a long biography | What is distinctive about you that the rest of the file does not say |
| Academic history | Matriculation and expected graduation dates, extensions, leaves of absence, gaps or breaks, dual degrees, repeated or remediated coursework and any adverse actions | Whether your path was continuous and how any interruption was handled |
| Academic progress | Performance and professional attributes in preclinical and clinical work, with a separate statement on professional standards, grade components and faculty narratives | How you actually performed with patients and teams |
| Summary | A summative assessment of comparative performance relative to peers, with the method of comparison stated | Where you stand, and how the school decided |
| Medical school information | The school's mission, curriculum, evaluation system with a translation of its grades, USMLE requirements for promotion, how the MSPE is written and whether students may review it | How to read everything above, especially if the school is unfamiliar |

AAMC also recommends including details on professionalism (both deficient and exemplary), explaining how final grades and comparative data are derived, and giving a school-wide comparison if a final overall rating is used. The exact limits on the length of the document and on the noteworthy characteristics list are in the recommendations. We do not restate them here, so read the source.

## MSPE examples, section by section

The excerpts below show the kind of content and tone each section uses. Real MSPEs vary by school, and an international school's document may look different. Read each excerpt, then the annotation beneath it.

### Section 1: Identifying information

*Illustrative composite written by the USMLE Wise team. Not a real student's MSPE.*

> **Medical Student Performance Evaluation**
>
> Student: [Legal name]
>
> Medical school: [School name], [City, Country]
>
> Year in school: [Graduating class or final year]
>
> Date of this document: [Month, Year]

**What programs read.** Mostly an identity check. A reader compares the name and school with the rest of your file.

**What to check.** Your name should be consistent across your school documents, your ECFMG records and your ERAS application. Inconsistent spelling can cause avoidable delays, so see [name and document issues](/wiki/ecfmg/name-and-document-issues) and confirm ECFMG's name rules on ECFMG's pages.

### Section 2: Noteworthy characteristics

*Illustrative composite written by the USMLE Wise team. Not a real student's MSPE.*

> **Noteworthy Characteristics**
>
> - Worked part-time as a medical interpreter during the preclinical years and continued to interpret for patients and families during clinical rotations.
> - Coordinated a student-run community blood pressure screening program for [duration] and trained [number] junior students to take part.
> - Completed a research project on [topic] with a faculty mentor and presented it at a regional student conference.

**What programs read.** The section gives a reader context that grades cannot: work, service, language skills and challenges, in short bullets. AAMC recommends a short bulleted list with each item kept brief, and says the characteristics should add something not found elsewhere in the application. It adds that lengthy biographical descriptions are not recommended. AAMC's MSPE FAQs say research or scholarly work may be included at the discretion of the MSPE writer and the student, as one of the noteworthy characteristics.

**What to check.** AAMC says the characteristics can be identified by the student with a mentor or advisor, or by the MSPE author, so ask your school how it chooses them. If it asks you for input, give facts you can document. AAMC's applicant guide also suggests that your most meaningful experience descriptions should not repeat the same information as your MSPE noteworthy characteristics or your personal statement. See the [Experiences section guide](/wiki/application/eras-experiences-section).

### Section 3: Academic history

*Illustrative composite written by the USMLE Wise team. Not a real student's MSPE. It includes optional items to show how they are phrased. Most MSPEs contain fewer.*

> **Academic History**
>
> [Student] matriculated in [Month, Year] and is expected to graduate in [Month, Year].
>
> [Student] took an approved leave of absence from [Month, Year] to [Month, Year] under the school's leave policy, then returned and resumed the program.
>
> [Student] was required to repeat [course name] after a failing grade in [term]. The course was remediated through [the school's remediation process] and passed in [term].
>
> [Student] is not enrolled in a dual or combined degree program. No adverse actions were imposed by the school.

**What programs read.** Dates, interruptions, repeated courses and adverse actions. AAMC's recommendations say this section covers extensions, leaves, gaps or breaks, repeated or remediated coursework and adverse actions, based on school policies. The MSPE FAQs say a failing grade should be noted along with a succinct description of how it was remediated.

**What to check.** Every date here must agree with your CV, your ERAS dates and your transcript. A gap that the MSPE mentions and your application omits looks worse than one you explained. If the MSPE shows an interruption, see [gap years and how to explain them](/wiki/application/gap-years-and-how-to-explain) and [failed attempts on your application](/wiki/application/failed-attempts-on-application).

### Section 4: Academic progress

*Illustrative composite written by the USMLE Wise team. Not a real student's MSPE.*

> **Academic Progress**
>
> *Preclinical courses.* Preclinical courses were graded pass or fail, and [Student] met all requirements. Faculty comments describe careful preparation for small-group sessions.
>
> *Professional standards.* [Student] met the school's standards for professional behavior. The school defines professionalism as [definition] and assesses it through [method]. No concerns were reported. Faculty commended [Student] for staying late to help a team complete a patient's discharge plan.
>
> *Internal Medicine clerkship, [dates].* Grade: [grade]. Grade components: [clinical assessment, weight], [subject examination, weight], [case write-up, weight]. Faculty narrative: "A reliable member of the team who follows through on tasks without being asked. Presents patients clearly and reasons through a differential diagnosis. Should continue to work on prioritizing when a patient has several active problems." Distribution of grades for this clerkship: [chart or table].

**What programs read.** This is where a reader spends the most time. AAMC's recommendations call clerkship evaluations a crucial piece of information, "considered by many to be the most important section of the MSPE in determining applicants for interview selection and rank order list." They recommend giving the components of each clerkship grade and their weights, faculty narratives that are not edited for content, areas of strength and areas for improvement when faculty provided them, a statement on professional standards, and comparative data inside the body of the MSPE.

**What to check.** If your school's document gives only a final grade, ask whether it can add the components, the narrative comments and the grade distribution. A reader who cannot see what a grade means will often discount it. Keep your own clinical letters strong and consistent with this section. See [letters of recommendation](/wiki/application/letters-of-recommendation).

### Section 5: Summary

*Illustrative composite written by the USMLE Wise team. Not a real student's MSPE.*

> **Summary**
>
> Based on the evaluations above, [Student] performed in the [performance category] among [class size] students in the graduating class. This placement was determined by [method], using [which components count and how they are weighted]. USMLE scores, research and community service were [not] considered in this placement. Faculty describe [Student] as a dependable, well-prepared clinician with particular strengths in [area].

**What programs read.** The comparison and how it was built. AAMC's recommendations say the summary should give a summative assessment of comparative performance relative to peers, and that the information used to compare students must be clearly stated. They say a final adjective or overall rating should be included only if a school-wide comparison gives it context. AAMC's FAQs add that schools that do not rank students or use percentiles do not need to begin doing so, and should instead describe the criteria for each descriptor.

**What to check.** If your school does not rank, ask it to state its categories and criteria. An unexplained label is hard for a program reader to interpret, especially for an unfamiliar school.

### Section 6: Medical school information

*Illustrative composite written by the USMLE Wise team. Not a real school's wording.*

> **Medical School Information**
>
> [School] is a [public or private] medical school in [City, Country] with a mission of [mission]. The curriculum is organized as [description], with core clerkships in [timing]. Grades are given as [scale], which correspond to [explanation of each grade]. Students must pass [school requirements, including any USMLE requirement] to be promoted and to graduate. Narrative comments in this document come from faculty evaluations and are edited only for length, grammar and spelling. The MSPE is composed by [office]. Students [may or may not] review the document before it is sent.

**What programs read.** A decoder for everything above. AAMC's recommendations list the school's mission and unusual features, the timing of coursework, the evaluation system with a translation of its grades, the USMLE requirement for promotion or graduation, how narrative comments are used, how the MSPE is composed and whether the student may review it.

**What to check.** For an IMG this section can matter most, because programs may not know your school. If your school's document lacks a clear description of its grading and curriculum, ask whether a separate school information sheet can be attached. The recommendations describe two ways to transmit this section: loaded into ERAS as one document attached to each MSPE, or linked from a URL in the MSPE. Ask your school which it uses.

## What programs look for when they read an MSPE

Programs differ, and none of this is a rule. NRMP's Program Director Survey reports which factors programs use to select applicants for interview, so check the current edition on nrmp.org for how the MSPE and clerkship grades are weighed. We do not quote a figure here. In practice, readers often scan in roughly this order.

| What a reader scans | Where it is | Why it matters (opinion) | What hurts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clerkship narratives and grades | Academic progress | Shows clinical performance, which exam scores cannot | Only a final grade, with no components or comments |
| Comparative standing and how it was derived | Summary | Lets a reader compare you with applicants from other schools | A label with no explanation |
| Professionalism statement | Academic progress | Programs want to know you are safe to work with | A concern the application does not explain |
| Interruptions, repeats and adverse actions | Academic history | Tests whether the file is honest and complete | A date or event the MSPE shows and the application omits |
| Dates that agree across documents | Whole document | Consistency builds trust | A mismatch with ERAS, your CV or your transcript |
| How to read this school's grades | Medical school information | Unfamiliar schools need translation | No explanation of the grading scale |
| Something distinctive | Noteworthy characteristics | A short list can make a reader pause | A list that repeats your CV |

## Is there an MSPE template for students?

No, and a search for "medical school performance record template" can mislead you here. The MSPE is a school-written document, and the template AAMC links on its MSPE page is a school-side document, not something a student fills in and submits. What students can do with the AAMC structure is use it as a checklist: read your own MSPE against the six sections, notice what is missing, and ask your school whether it can add anything useful, such as a grade translation or the clerkship components.

## What if my school does not issue an MSPE?

Some schools issue a dean's letter in another format. A few issue nothing. What to do depends on what your school can provide, and the first step is always to ask early and in writing. The table is practice and opinion, not an ECFMG rule. Confirm the current process on ECFMG's pages before acting.

| What your school offers | What it usually means | What to ask or do |
|---|---|---|
| An MSPE in AAMC-style format | Closest to what programs expect | Confirm the delivery route and timing |
| An MSPE or dean's letter in the school's own format | Common outside the US | Ask whether it can add a grade translation, clerkship summaries and comparative information. Confirm with ECFMG how it is handled |
| A letter of good standing, a graduation or enrollment confirmation | Confirms status, with little evaluation | Ask whether a more evaluative letter is possible, and strengthen your clinical letters |
| A transcript only | Grades without narrative | Ask the dean's office for a dean's letter, and tell ECFMG |
| Nothing | No school evaluation document | Tell ECFMG's ERAS support team and ask what it can do. Build the rest of the file to carry context |

In order:

1. **Ask your school what it can issue, in writing.** Use the email template in the next section. Ask for the format, who signs it, how long it takes, how it will be sent, and whether it will be in English.
2. **Answer the MyERAS question honestly.** The AAMC applicant guide says IMG residency applicants must indicate in MyERAS whether they or their medical school will provide the MSPE to the ERAS Documents office at ECFMG. You can change the answer until you certify and submit.
3. **If your school will provide nothing, tell ECFMG's ERAS support team** and ask what it can do for your situation. We could not read ECFMG's pages while writing this, so confirm the current process, any alternative document and any translation rule on ECFMG's pages.
4. **Make the rest of your file carry the context.** In our opinion, programs read a missing MSPE as missing information. Dates on your CV and in ERAS should agree, any gap should have a short factual explanation, and clinical letters from people who supervised you should be strong and specific. See [gap years and how to explain them](/wiki/application/gap-years-and-how-to-explain).
5. **If you apply to emergency medicine or OB/GYN,** your application goes through ResidencyCAS, not ERAS. Check the ResidencyCAS Quick Start Guide for how it handles the MSPE and transcript, and confirm the IMG steps on ECFMG's pages. The [emergency medicine for IMGs](/wiki/specialties/emergency-medicine) page explains the route.

## How to ask your school for your MSPE: an email template

Treat the school as a vendor with a deadline. A polite, specific, dated request gets answered more often than a general one, and a written record helps if you later need to ask ECFMG what to do. Send it to the dean's office or student affairs office, and keep copies of every message.

> [!NOTE] These emails are illustrative composites written by the USMLE Wise team. Replace every bracket, and change the wording to suit your school's customs. Do not ask the school to write anything specific or favorable. Ask only what the document will contain and when it will be sent.

**Email 1: the first request**

> Subject: Request for MSPE or dean's letter for residency application: [Your full name], [student or graduate ID]
>
> Dear [Name or Office of the Dean],
>
> I am a [graduate or final-year student] of [School name], class of [year], and I am applying for residency training in the United States in the current application cycle. I am writing to ask what document the school can provide for my application and how it will be sent.
>
> Could you please tell me:
>
> 1. Whether the school issues a Medical Student Performance Evaluation (MSPE) or a dean's letter, and in what format;
> 2. Whether it will include clerkship summaries, a description of the school's grading system and comparative information, if the school provides them;
> 3. Whether the school will send it directly to ECFMG's ERAS Documents office, or whether I should send it myself, and who the contact person is;
> 4. Whether it will be available in English, or whether I should arrange a translation;
> 5. The date by which it will be ready, and how I will be told when it has been sent.
>
> Programs begin reviewing applications on a fixed date in the fall, and document processing takes time, so I would be grateful for a reply by [date]. I can send any form or instruction sheet the school needs.
>
> Thank you for your help.
>
> Sincerely,
>
> [Full name]
>
> [Email, phone, student or graduate ID]

**Email 2: if the school says it does not issue an MSPE**

> Subject: Alternative school document for residency application: [Your full name], [ID]
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> Thank you for your reply. I understand that the school does not issue an MSPE. Could the school instead provide a letter from the Dean's office confirming my dates of enrollment and graduation, my academic standing, and, if possible, a summary of my clinical performance and a description of the school's grading system? I would also like to know whether the school will send it to ECFMG directly.
>
> If the school cannot provide any document beyond the transcript, I would be grateful for a short written confirmation of that, which I will share with ECFMG. Could you please reply by [date]?
>
> Thank you,
>
> [Full name], [ID]

**Email 3: a follow-up after no reply**

> Subject: Follow-up: document request, [Your full name], [ID]
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> I wrote on [date] about my residency application documents and have not yet had a reply. I would be grateful for a short update on whether the school can issue the document and when it will be sent. My application deadlines depend on this, and I am happy to provide anything the school needs.
>
> Thank you,
>
> [Full name], [ID]

If the school stays silent, escalate in writing to a named senior contact, and ask ECFMG what alternatives exist. Do not leave this until the last weeks before the application opens.

## How to explain a missing or non-standard MSPE in ERAS

There is no personal-statement-sized place for this, and the explanation should be short and factual. These are practice and opinion.

- **Answer the MyERAS document question truthfully.** It is the place where you tell ERAS who will provide the MSPE. Do not leave it for the final day.
- **Use the Interruptions or Extensions question only if it applies.** The AAMC applicant guide asks whether you had academic extensions, leaves, gaps or breaks due to repeated or remediated coursework, or professional ones due to sanctions or other adverse actions, and asks for a description if you answer yes. It says the section is not intended to collect health, disability or family information. It is not the place to explain a missing MSPE.
- **Add one factual sentence elsewhere only if a reader would otherwise wonder.** If your school's document is not an MSPE, a short sentence in an interview answer or in a relevant section is usually enough, such as this illustrative composite: "My school does not issue a Medical Student Performance Evaluation. It provides a dean's letter and an official transcript, which were sent to ECFMG on [date]. I am happy to provide any further information programs need."
- **Do not apologize or blame the school.** State what happened and what you did about it.
- **Be ready to talk about it at interview.** A program may ask, and a calm, factual answer is easy to give if you have kept your emails.

For EM and OB/GYN applicants using ResidencyCAS, check that service's instructions for any way to note this, and do not assume the MyERAS wording applies.

## When the MSPE contains a weakness

An MSPE can contain things you cannot edit: a low standing, a remediated course or a leave. Do not try to alter or hide it. Programs compare the MSPE with the rest of your application, so a weakness the MSPE mentions and your application omits looks worse than one you addressed in a sentence. If something in it is factually wrong, such as a wrong date, ask your school to correct it. AAMC's MSPE FAQs say changes or additions after submission should be made as addenda, and the original changed only to correct factual errors. See the [MSPE and transcripts for IMGs](/wiki/application/mspe-and-transcripts-for-imgs) page and [how programs screen applications](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters).

## A checklist before you certify

- You have read your school's document (or written confirmation of what it will be) against the six sections above.
- Your school has confirmed in writing what it will send, when, and by which route.
- MyERAS (or ResidencyCAS, for EM and OB/GYN) shows the correct information about who provides the MSPE and the transcript.
- Every date in the MSPE agrees with your CV, ERAS dates and transcript.
- Any interruption or repeated course in the MSPE is explained briefly elsewhere in your application.
- Non-English documents have been translated according to ECFMG's current instructions.
- You know the date programs begin reviewing applications. See the [ERAS timeline](/wiki/application/eras-timeline).
- You have confirmed every ECFMG step on ECFMG's pages, including the [ERAS token](/wiki/ecfmg/eras-token) process.

> [!NOTE] No MSPE, however strong, can guarantee an interview or a match, and a missing or weak one is not the end of an application. The MSPE is one part of a file that also includes exam results, clinical experience, letters and timing. See [MSPE for IMGs](/wiki/application/mspe-and-transcripts-for-imgs) for how documents reach programs.
