There is no separate CV to upload in MyERAS: in the 2027 AAMC applicant user guide, the ERAS CV is a view that MyERAS builds from the Application sections you fill in. A strong ERAS CV example is therefore a well-completed application, and this page gives you a section-by-section template for it, include and leave-out lists, formatting rules and short illustrative entries, plus a master CV you can use everywhere a CV is requested outside ERAS.

> [!WARNING] Every entry on this page is an **illustrative composite written by the USMLE Wise team**, with placeholders in square brackets. None is a real applicant's text. Do not copy them: enter only what you did, with dates you can prove.

## What the "ERAS CV" actually is

Many applicants search for an "ERAS CV template" and assume they must write and upload a document. The 2027 user guide says something different, and it is the first thing to check against your own cycle's guide.

- **The CV is a view, not a file you write.** The guide says the CV "populates the information you entered in the application section" and is "nothing more than the MyERAS application formatted differently for the benefit of those programs that prefer to view applications in a CV format." Open it with **View/Print CV**, in the same place as **View/Print MyERAS Application**.
- **The documents you manage are different.** In the 2027 guide they are the personal statement, letters of recommendation, USMLE transcript, [[mspe]] and medical school transcript, the photo, and, for IMGs, the ECFMG Status Report (which ECFMG uploads for you). A CV is not on that list.
- **Programs may still ask.** Some programs, observership hosts and research groups ask for a CV in their own application, and your letter writers will ask for one. Practice: keep one master CV outside MyERAS, built from the same facts.

That gives you three things to keep consistent: the MyERAS application and its CV view, your master CV, and the story in your [personal statement](/wiki/application/personal-statement-guide) and experiences. A mismatch in dates or titles between any two is the most common avoidable question at an interview.

> [!NOTE] Sections, fields and limits change between ERAS cycles. This page follows the 2027 cycle guide. If you apply in a later season, compare each section with the current guide, and check any limit shown in MyERAS.

## ERAS CV template: section by section

This table follows the order of the MyERAS Application tabs. The middle column is what AAMC's 2027 guide says the section holds. The last column is practice.

| MyERAS section | What the guide says it holds | How to fill it well (practice) |
|---|---|---|
| Personal Information | Account details, contact information, address, work authorization, match information, exam identification numbers | Keep it current all season. Answer work authorization truthfully and consistently with your ECFMG record. |
| Biographical Information | Optional self-identification, language fluency, military information, geographic preferences, setting preference, hometowns | Be honest, not strategic. List languages you can use with patients, at the level you can really use them. |
| Education | Higher education, medical education, postgraduate training, interruptions or extensions, honors and awards, professional memberships | Copy names, degrees and dates from your diploma, transcript and [[mspe]]. |
| Experience | Up to {{fact:eras_experiences_max}} selected experiences in the {{fact:eras_cycle_year}} cycle, up to {{fact:eras_most_meaningful_max}} flagged most meaningful, an optional impactful experiences question, and hobbies and interests | See [writing the Experiences section](/wiki/application/eras-experiences-section) and the [experiences examples](/wiki/application/eras-experiences-examples). |
| Licenses and Certifications | State medical licenses (or None), questions on license status, malpractice and convictions, board certification, other certifications, DEA registration | If you have nothing to enter, select None where offered. Answer every question truthfully. |
| Scholarly Work | Peer-reviewed articles, book chapters and abstracts, oral presentations and poster presentations | Enter each item under the right type, with honest status. Preview it as a program would. |
| Specialty Questions | Additional questions that only some specialties ask (in the 2027 guide, Anesthesiology, Neurological Surgery and Plastic Surgery - Integrated) | Answer them in their own voice. Do not paste text from your personal statement. |

In your Education section, the medical education entry for the school you graduated from is the minimum. If you did not attend other schools or training programs, the guide asks you to select None rather than leaving a section blank.

### Honors and awards in ERAS

In the 2027 guide this subsection asks about your status in honor societies (Alpha Omega Alpha for MD applicants, Gold Humanism Honor Society, and Sigma Sigma Phi for DO applicants). Check your own MyERAS for any other award fields. If a prize or scholarship has no field of its own, do not invent a place for it. Mention it in the description of the experience it belongs to, only if it fits naturally, and keep it consistent with your MSPE.

### Licenses and certifications in ERAS

State medical licenses are US state licenses, so most IMG applicants have none. Select None and answer the license-status, malpractice and conviction questions truthfully. For certifications, the guide separates board certification (a yes or no) from other medical or health care related certifications, which you can add without a stated limit, and DEA registration. Examples of other certifications are life-support courses, with the issuing body and dates. A home-country medical license is not a US state license. List it on your master CV, and in MyERAS use a field only if one clearly fits. If you are unsure, ask AAMC support rather than guess.

### Professional memberships in ERAS

Add current memberships in professional organizations, associations or societies. Membership alone is a small signal, so list the ones you really belong to, and put active roles (a committee, an office, a project) in an experience entry instead.

### Interruptions or extensions

This section asks whether you had academic extensions, leaves or gaps due to repeated or remediated coursework, and professional ones due to professionalism sanctions or other adverse actions. AAMC states it is not intended to solicit information about your health, disability or family status. If you answer yes, give a short, factual description that matches your MSPE and transcript. See [failed attempts on your application](/wiki/application/failed-attempts-on-application) and [gap years and how to explain them](/wiki/application/gap-years-and-how-to-explain).

## What to include on your ERAS CV, and what to leave out

| Include | Leave out |
|---|---|
| Every degree and training period, with exact dates that match your diploma, transcript and MSPE | Entries you cannot document or discuss for a few minutes |
| Real roles, with the title you actually held and a plain statement of your level of responsibility | Inflated titles, or observation described as hands-on work |
| Scholarly work, with honest status and your real author position | Work that exists only as an idea or draft. The Scholarly Work statuses in the guide are submitted, accepted or in press, and published. |
| Certifications you hold now, with issuing body and dates | Expired items presented as current |
| Languages you can use with patients | Skill ratings, objective statements and "references available on request" lines |
| Memberships that are current | The same activity repeated in several sections to fill space |
| Experiences that show different sides of you | Trivial entries added only to use every slot |

Two points on personal data. Practice: leave marital status, religion and photos off your master CV. MyERAS has its own photo document, and programs use it mostly to recognize applicants on interview day, so check each program's use of it. AAMC also lets you choose whether to release your birth date to programs, so decide that on purpose in Personal Information.

## Formatting rules

You do not format the ERAS CV, but you control everything that goes into it.

1. **Draft in a plain text editor.** The MyERAS application has no spell or grammar check, so proofread outside it. AAMC recommends plain text for personal statements because other word processors can carry hidden formatting. The same habit is safe for every field you paste.
2. **Use the authors format for scholarly work.** Enter authors in the order they appear in the publication, as Lastname then first and middle initials, separated by commas, and use the bold button on your own name. Example format: Lastname AB, Lastname CD, **Yourlastname EF**. Mark whether you are first author. Keep it consistent with the [authorship guide](/wiki/research/authorship-and-author-order).
3. **Group related work.** Scholarly collections let you group items, for example a poster that became a paper, under a short title.
4. **Keep dates in one format.** Use month and year everywhere, and check every date against your official documents.
5. **Preview as programs see it.** Open View/Print MyERAS Application (the PDF) and View/Print CV, and use the preview as program button inside Scholarly Work.
6. **Bullets and line breaks.** Entry descriptions are free-text boxes, and the guide does not describe bullet formatting for them. Practice: write short plain sentences, avoid pasted formatting, and check how the text looks in the preview. Your master CV can use bullets freely.
7. **Proofread before you certify.** Certifying and submitting is a one-time action. The application locks, programs receive the same copy, and only Personal Information can change afterward.

## Short illustrative entries

These are composites. They show structure, not content to copy.

**Medical education (composite)**

- Institution: [Medical school], [City, country]
- Degree: [Degree exactly as printed on the diploma]
- Start and end dates: [Month Year] to [Month Year]

**Interruptions or extensions (composite)**

> I repeated [subject] in [year] after not meeting the pass standard at the first attempt. I passed at the second attempt and completed every later course on schedule.

What works: one fact, the outcome, no excuses, and the same account your MSPE gives. Do not use this box to explain a health or family matter that the section does not ask about.

**Scholarly Work: peer-reviewed journal article (composite)**

- Type: Journal article (peer reviewed)
- Authors: Lastname AB, Lastname CD, **Yourlastname EF**
- First author: No
- Status: Published, [Month Year]
- Journal, volume, issue and pages: as printed on the article
- PMID (optional): [your article's PubMed identifier]

**Scholarly Work: poster presentation (composite)**

- Type: Poster presentation
- Authors: Lastname AB, **Yourlastname EF**
- First author: Yes
- Event: [Meeting name], [City, country], [Month Year]
- Add a second event if you presented it again.

**Scholarly Work: accepted manuscript (composite)**

- Type: Journal article (peer reviewed)
- Status: Accepted or in press, with the date of acceptance
- Preprint link: only if one exists

**Other certification (composite)**

- Certification: [Name of certification, such as a basic or advanced life-support course]
- Issuing body and date: [Issuing body], [year], [expiry if any]

**Professional membership (composite)**

- Organization: [National or international society or association]
- Status: current member since [year]

> [!IMG TIP] Search each of your own publications on PubMed before you enter it, and copy the author order, journal name and PMID exactly. A citation that does not match the real record raises doubts about everything else.

## What is different for IMGs

- **Names and documents.** Use your name exactly as it appears on your ECFMG record, and keep titles and dates consistent with your diploma. See [name and document issues](/wiki/ecfmg/name-and-document-issues) if yours differ across documents.
- **Two documents your CV must agree with.** The ECFMG Status Report is uploaded by ECFMG and automatically assigned to every program an IMG applies to. It shows certification status and the month and year exams were passed. Your USMLE scores go separately, as a transcript you authorize and assign through ERAS. Your CV should not contradict either.
- **Years since graduation.** Programs read your dates for gaps. See [years since graduation](/wiki/application/years-since-graduation), and explain any gap briefly and factually.
- **Local titles.** Say what you actually did and with what supervision, because titles such as intern, house officer or medical officer differ by country.
- **Reapplicants.** MyERAS lets you view and download earlier applications and CVs from its History tab and import your most recent application data. In the 2027 guide, personal, biographical, education, experiences and licenses data can be imported, but scholarly work cannot (that section was redesigned), and documents such as the photo, personal statement, medical school transcript and MSPE must be uploaded again. Import what you can, then review every section: dates, roles and statuses have changed. See [reapplicant strategy](/wiki/application/reapplicant-strategy).

## A pre-certification audit

1. Open View/Print MyERAS Application and View/Print CV. Read both as a program director would.
2. Compare every date with your diploma, transcript, [[mspe]] and ECFMG record.
3. Check each scholarly work entry against PubMed or the journal page: author order, bold name, status.
4. Check experience types. Are observerships under Education/training and research under Research?
5. Look for repeated entries across sections and delete them.
6. Ask someone who has read many applications to spend a minute with it. Ask what they remember.
7. Fix the answers to "so what?" questions: what did you do, and what changed?

## Where else you need a CV

AAMC's guidelines for letter writers say that, if you have not supplied them, the writer should request your CV and a draft personal statement. Send your master CV in your [letter request packet](/wiki/application/letters-of-recommendation-examples) so the writer's facts match yours. Observership and research hosts commonly ask for one as well. If a program asks for a CV after you apply, send the same document, not a new version with different dates.
