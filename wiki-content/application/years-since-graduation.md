Many residency programs screen on how long ago you graduated, and **there is no single national number: each program sets its own limit, publishes it or does not, and applies it differently by specialty.** If your graduation year is several years back, your task is to find which programs will actually read your file and to show you are clinically current.

> [!NOTE] We found no national rule on years since graduation in the sources we reviewed. What follows describes how programs commonly handle it. Check each program for its own wording.

## Why programs use this filter

Programs worry about clinical currency and about the unknown. A long gap since graduation raises questions: has the applicant kept up clinical skills, will they adapt to a US hospital, and do they have the knowledge for the first year? Some programs also use the filter as a quick way to narrow thousands of applications. See [how programs screen applications](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters).

NRMP's 2026 Program Director Survey is useful but limited here. It asked programs about factors such as "evidence of continuous medical education without gaps" when selecting applicants for interview, and 12.7% of surveyed programs responded. It does not give a standard cutoff for years since graduation. To find programs that fit your profile, see [identifying IMG-friendly programs](/wiki/application/identifying-img-friendly-programs).

## What the limit looks like in practice

| Type of statement | What it means | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| A stated maximum (for example, a number of years) | Applications outside it are often not reviewed | Do not apply unless the program confirms an exception in writing |
| "Preference for recent graduates" | A soft filter | Apply if the rest of your file is strong and you have recent activity |
| "Graduated within X years of the program start" | The clock may run to the start of training, not to the application date | Work out the date carefully |
| No statement | Not necessarily no filter | Ask the coordinator or look at who the program has interviewed or hired |

Programs may count from the graduation date on your diploma, from the date of your degree, or from the date of ECFMG certification. If a program does not say, ask in one short email.

## How to find the answer for each program

1. Open the program's website page for applicants, then FAQs.
2. Check the program's FREIDA listing.
3. Search the page for "years," "graduated," "recent graduate" and "gap."
4. If it is still unclear, ask the coordinator one specific question and keep the reply.
5. Record the answer in your list. See [building your program list](/wiki/application/building-a-program-list).

AAMC's applicant guide advises applicants to research programs individually, and notes that signals are intended as one data point among many. Do not spend a scarce [signal](/wiki/application/program-signaling-explained) on a program with a stated limit you exceed.

## How to respond if you are past the usual window

You cannot change your graduation date. You can change what a reader sees around it.

1. **Recent clinical activity.** The most direct answer to the concern. A hands-on US placement with a supervisor who writes a letter, a recent clinical job abroad, or a documented hospital role all address it. See [how much USCE programs want](/wiki/clinical-experience/how-much-usce-programs-want) and [turning USCE into a letter](/wiki/clinical-experience/turning-usce-into-a-letter).
2. **Recent exam results.** A recent solid Step 2 CK, and where appropriate Step 3, show the knowledge is current. See [Step 2 CK](/wiki/exams/step-2-ck).
3. **No unexplained months.** Fill the timeline honestly. See [gap years](/wiki/application/gap-years-and-how-to-explain).
4. **A coherent story.** Why medicine, why now, why this specialty. See the [personal statement guide](/wiki/application/personal-statement-guide).
5. **Realistic program and specialty choice.** Programs that regularly take older IMG graduates exist in several specialties, and NRMP's data show IMGs match across specialties at different rates. See [IMG match rates by specialty](/wiki/specialties/img-match-rates-by-specialty).
6. **Plan for a longer path.** If the barrier is the clock, ask whether a preliminary year or another route is realistic. See [preliminary and transitional years](/wiki/application/preliminary-and-transitional-years).

> [!IMG TIP] Write one sentence for your interview: "I graduated in [year]; since then I have [specific clinical or academic activity], most recently [activity in the last 6 to 12 months]." If that sentence is weak, fix the activity, not the wording.

## What not to do

- Do not change or round your graduation date, the date of your degree or your ECFMG dates.
- Do not describe a gap as clinical work when it was not.
- Do not apply broadly to programs with a limit you plainly exceed and hope the system misses it. Programs can see the date.

> [!WARNING] Dates in ERAS are certified, and misreporting is treated as an integrity issue by AAMC. If a program stated a limit and you apply anyway, be ready to be honest about your dates the moment it asks.

## Honest bottom line

For a long-ago graduate, the realistic set of programs is smaller, and the margin for other weaknesses is thinner. That does not make a match impossible. It does make list quality, recent clinical evidence and a clean exam record more important, and it makes spending applications on programs you cannot pass the most expensive mistake.
