In the 2027 MyERAS application, research and publications go in the **Scholarly Work** section, and the work that shaped you as a researcher also belongs in **Experiences**. **Enter every item at its true status, star the three you can discuss best, and be ready to explain your role in each.** This page covers the mechanics and the honesty rules. For whether research is worth doing, see [how programs view research](/wiki/research/how-programs-view-research).

> [!CHANGED] For the 2027 ERAS season (guide reviewed November 2026), AAMC redesigned the old Publications section as Scholarly Work. Applicants cannot import publications from a previous season into the 2027 application. Check the [AAMC applicant guide](https://students-residents.aamc.org/media/16871/download) for current details.

## What the Scholarly Work section accepts

According to the AAMC 2027 guide, the types are:

| Type | Status fields | Extra fields |
|---|---|---|
| Journal article (peer reviewed) | Submitted, accepted or in press, published | Journal name, volume, issue, pages, date, optional URL and PMID |
| Book chapter (peer reviewed) | Not listed | Title, authors |
| Journal abstract (peer reviewed) | Submitted, accepted or in press, published | Same as article |
| Oral presentation | Not applicable | One or more events with meeting name, date, city and country |
| Poster presentation | Not applicable | One or more events with meeting name, date, city and country |

You also record the authors in the order they appear and say whether you are first author. If you have no entries, you must select None.

The guide includes three tools that matter for strategy:

- **Star up to three** items as your most meaningful scholarly work.
- **Group related items** into scholarly collections, such as a poster, an abstract and a paper from the same project. Collection titles have a 100 character limit.
- **Preview as program** to see how programs will view the section.

## Label status honestly

Programs may check, and NRMP notes that some programs verify publications for applicants they are interested in. NRMP also says applicant-reported research counts are not verified and quality varies greatly. So honesty is both an ethical rule and a practical one.

| Where the work is | How to list it |
|---|---|
| Idea or protocol only | Not in Scholarly Work; describe in Experiences |
| Data collection in progress | Experiences section |
| Manuscript submitted | Journal article, status Submitted, with the date |
| Accepted | Accepted or in press, with the date and (if available) preprint URL |
| Published | Published, with citation details |
| Presented at a meeting | Poster or oral presentation, with the event |

Do not list a submitted paper as accepted, do not call a conference abstract a journal paper, and do not list a preprint as peer reviewed. If you are unsure which type fits, ask your Designated Dean's Office as the guide suggests, or ask the lead author.

> [!WARNING] AAMC runs an Integrity Promotion Program that investigates allegations of fraudulent or unethical application behavior. Listing work that does not exist, inflating your authorship position, or misstating status is the kind of thing it exists to examine. Misrepresentation can follow you into licensing and credentialing.

## Authorship: say what you did

Your name on a paper is not a description of your work. In the Experiences section, say what you did: study design, data collection, analysis, writing. Use accepted standards for authorship. The ICMJE defines authorship criteria, and our guide to [authorship and author order](/wiki/research/authorship-and-author-order) explains what is ethical. Do not accept or pay for authorship you did not earn, and avoid predatory venues. See [predatory journals](/wiki/research/predatory-journals).

## Use Experiences for the story

The Experiences section is where programs read about your role, supervisor, dates, and what you learned. For each research position:

1. Name the project, setting, supervisor and dates.
2. State your specific role in two or three plain sentences.
3. Name the output: poster, abstract, manuscript in progress.
4. Link it to something you can discuss in an interview.

Avoid duplicating the same text in Scholarly Work and Experiences. See [writing the Experiences section](/wiki/application/eras-experiences-section) and the [MyERAS walkthrough](/wiki/application/myeras-walkthrough).

## Quantity versus quality

NRMP's 2024 IMG Charting Outcomes says applicants do not need publications or research projects to match successfully. In its tables, unmatched IMGs in some specialties reported as many or more abstracts, presentations and publications than matched ones. Research is a supplement to a strong exam record, clinical experience and letters, not a substitute. A short list you can discuss in depth reads better than a long list you cannot.

> [!IMG TIP] Before you certify, read each Scholarly Work entry aloud and ask: could I explain the question, the method, my role and the main result in one minute? If not, you may not want it among your three stars.

## Preparing for interview questions

Expect "tell me about this project." Prepare for each starred item:

- The research question and why it mattered.
- Your role, in first person, without exaggeration.
- One limitation of the work.
- What you learned, and how it connects to your specialty.

If the work is from a long time ago, be ready to say what you have done since. See [common interview questions](/wiki/interviews-match/common-interview-questions).

## If you have little research

Say nothing false and do not pad. Spend your effort on what carries more weight for your profile, such as recent clinical activity, letters and a realistic program list. If you want to add research, see [finding a research position](/wiki/research/finding-a-research-position), [posters and conferences](/wiki/research/posters-and-conferences) and [case reports](/wiki/research/case-reports). Start early: an item that appears in the ERAS Scholarly Work section as "submitted" before you certify is useful; one that you finish after interview season is useful in a different way, as an update to programs.
