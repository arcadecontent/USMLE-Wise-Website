The Step 3 readiness checker helps you decide whether you look ready to book or sit USMLE Step 3, and what to fix first if you do not. It is not a Step 3 score predictor. It never outputs a predicted score or a pass probability, because we know of no public data that would make one honest for an international medical graduate. What it does is check the evidence that usually separates a candidate who is ready from one who is hoping.

You answer a few questions about eligibility, question practice, independent self-assessments, case simulations (CCS), timing, logistics and wellbeing. You get one of three results, the specific items that are not fully met, an ordered list of up to three next actions, and links to the free guides behind each one.

## How to use the readiness checker

1. **Answer for where you are today,** not where you plan to be. The result is only as honest as the answers.
2. **Use your own results.** For the self-assessment question, judge by what the assessment report itself shows. Not every report states a passing level. If yours does not, use its content-area breakdown and your trend.
3. **Start with the first action.** The list is ordered so the item that blocks everything else comes first.
4. **Come back after you finish something.** Passing a checkpoint changes the result, and so does a bad week.
5. **Treat it as one input.** A mentor who has seen your actual practice results and error log will do better than any short quiz.

## What the three results mean

| Result | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Looks ready to book** | The evidence we check is in place. That is a reasonable basis to book, not a guarantee. | Confirm logistics, keep a date-change rule and plan the two days, including rest. |
| **Close: fix these first** | Most pieces are there, but at least one item is only partly met or not met. | Fix the listed items before you book, or before you sit if you are already booked. |
| **Not yet: build these first** | A requirement is unmet, your latest independent result reads as below passing, or several core areas are open. | Start with the first action. Attempts are limited, so building the base first is cheaper than testing and hoping. |

The preparation items are our opinion of sensible minimums, drawn from the [Step 3 study plan](/wiki/exams/step-3-study-schedule). USMLE does not set them. The eligibility items are USMLE rules, and the Bulletin is the final authority.

## How the result is decided

The rules are fixed and visible, and they contain no scores:

- **Not yet** if any eligibility answer is a blocker, if your latest independent result reads as below passing, or if two or more eligibility or preparation items are not met.
- **Looks ready** only if every eligibility and preparation item is fully met, no planning item (logistics, date rule, wellbeing) is unmet, and at least one planning item is fully met.
- **Close** for everything else.

Two things follow. One good self-assessment cannot give you the top result, because the checker looks for a steady trend across more than one. And a single gap in a core area cannot give you the top result either, even if everything else is strong.

> [!NOTE] Because the first part of the check is eligibility, someone who has not yet passed Step 2 CK will see a Not yet result even with excellent practice results. That is not a judgment of your preparation. Step 3 cannot be booked until the earlier exams are passed.

## Is there a Step 3 score predictor?

People search for a Step 3 score predictor or calculator, and many pages offer one. We do not, for three reasons:

- **No data supports it.** USMLE's score interpretation guidance publishes comparison tables for first-time examinees from LCME-accredited schools. We did not find an equivalent for IMGs, and we found no public data linking practice-test results to Step 3 results for IMGs.
- **The official practice tools say so themselves.** NBME describes its self-assessments as a way to evaluate readiness. For its Step 1 and Step 2 CK assessments it says the estimate of passing is not a guarantee, and its Step 3 assessment page describes multiple-choice questions, answer explanations and longitudinal feedback rather than a pass estimate. See [NBME self-assessments](/wiki/exams/nbme-self-assessments).
- **Step 3 is more than multiple-choice.** CCS is part of the Step 3 result, and no multiple-choice practice test measures it. A single predicted number would hide that.

If you find a calculator, conversion chart or "predictor" on a forum or social media, it is not published by NBME or USMLE. Use it, if at all, as a conversation point, never as a reason to book or to cancel.

## How services are suggested

USMLE Wise sells coaching and a Step 3 course, so you should know how a suggestion is made:

- A suggestion appears only when one of your answers shows a related gap, such as no CCS practice or no error review.
- You see at most one, with the reason it came up.
- Free guides always come first. Every next action links to one.
- Nothing is suggested for eligibility, logistics or wellbeing, and nothing is suggested while an eligibility requirement is open.
- If the result is Looks ready to book, nothing is suggested.

> [!NOTE] USMLE Wise has a commercial interest in the service the checker can suggest. The tool is meant to be useful on its own, and you can follow every step it gives you without buying anything.

## What it cannot tell you

- **Your score, or whether you will pass.** It does not estimate either.
- **Whether your answers are accurate.** It cannot check your practice results, so an optimistic answer gives an optimistic result.
- **Whether you are eligible.** The eligibility questions only flag what to confirm. USMLE and ECFMG decide eligibility, so check the current Bulletin and ask the organization that handles your registration. See [USMLE eligibility for IMGs](/wiki/exams/usmle-eligibility-for-imgs).
- **When you should take Step 3.** Timing depends on your licensure and career plans as well as readiness. See [when to take each Step](/wiki/exams/when-to-take-each-step).
- **Anything about visas or fees.** Those need the official sources and the right professional.
- **Your health.** The wellbeing question is a planning prompt, not an assessment. If you are struggling, talk to someone you trust or a health professional.

## After you get your result

1. Do the first action before anything else. If you are booked and the first action is a blocker, read [scheduling with Prometric](/wiki/exams/scheduling-with-prometric) for how rescheduling works.
2. Put dates on the plan. The [Step 3 study plan](/wiki/exams/step-3-study-schedule) has example timelines, and the [timeline planner](/wiki/tools/timeline-planner) shows where Step 3 sits in your wider application.
3. Re-run the checker after each checkpoint. Your own trend over several runs is a better guide than any single result.
4. For a wider view of your application, try the [IMG readiness self-evaluation](/wiki/tools/self-evaluation).
