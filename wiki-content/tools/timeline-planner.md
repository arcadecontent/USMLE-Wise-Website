Residency applications run on a calendar that does not wait for you. This planner turns that calendar into a checklist for your situation: choose the July you want to start training, tell it where you are with exams, ECFMG, experience and letters, and it lists what is left, in date order, with a link to the guide for each step.

## How to read your plan

- **The date** on the left is when a step is usually due, working backward from the season you chose.
- **Critical** marks steps that hold up everything else, such as eligibility, credential verification, the exam passes and submitting ERAS.
- **Date has passed, Coming up** and **Later** compare each date with today.
- **Planning buffer** marks dates that include extra room we added on purpose.
- The banner at the top tells you whether a later cycle is more realistic.

## What the planner assumes

The season is built around a handful of anchors that are stable from year to year, and a few buffers that are our own judgment:

| Anchor or buffer | What the planner uses | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Residency start | July of the year you choose | Typical |
| Match | March of that year | Typical |
| Rank order list | Winter before the Match | Typical |
| Interviews | Autumn into winter | Typical |
| ERAS submission | Early September of the year before you start | Typical |
| ECFMG eligibility and credentials | About 9 months before submission | Planning buffer |
| Step 1 | About 9 months before submission | Planning buffer |
| Step 2 CK | By about June of the application year | Planning buffer |
| Letters and experience | By early summer of the application year | Planning buffer |

> [!WARNING] The real dates change every year. Confirm the ERAS opening and submission dates, the ECFMG token timing, the Match calendar and USMLE scheduling rules with the organizations that set them. The [ERAS timeline guide](/wiki/application/eras-timeline) explains how each piece works.

## What it cannot tell you

- **Whether you will match.** No planner can. For context, read [IMG match statistics explained](/wiki/start-here/img-match-statistics).
- **Which visa you can get.** The planner only shows when visa work usually starts. See the [J-1 vs H-1B guide](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b) and confirm with your program.
- **How strong your application is.** It tracks timing, not quality.
- **Specialty differences.** Emergency Medicine and OB-GYN may use a different application service for the 2027 Match, and each specialty has its own norms. Read the guide for yours.

## Using it well

1. Start with the year you actually want, then try the next year to compare. Moving one cycle later often removes several "past" warnings at once.
2. Be honest about letters and experience. They take the longest to arrange and are the hardest to rush.
3. Work from the top of each group. The first item in a group is usually the one the others depend on.
4. Re-run it every few months. Passing an exam or finishing a placement changes the picture.
5. Copy the link and share it with a mentor so you are both looking at the same plan.
