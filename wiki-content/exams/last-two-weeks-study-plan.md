In the last two weeks before USMLE Step 1 or Step 2 CK, stop adding new material and taper: take one main full-length self-assessment early, repair your weakest areas from your own error log, rehearse the exam format, sort out logistics and protect your sleep. This page gives a day-by-day plan you can adapt, with notes on Step 3 near the end.

> [!NOTE] Rule, practice and opinion are labeled on this page. The exam rules come from USMLE. The day-by-day plan is practice-based opinion from the USMLE Wise team, not an official recommendation, and no plan can guarantee a result. Your own assessment data should override any template. This site is not affiliated with USMLE or NBME.

## The principles behind the plan

1. **Taper, do not cram.** The last two weeks are for consolidating what you know and fixing the gaps you have already found. New resources at this stage mostly add noise.
2. **Your error log is the syllabus.** Study what your own mistakes show, not what a list says is high-yield. See the error-log method in the [Step 2 CK study schedule](/wiki/exams/step-2-ck-study-schedule).
3. **Rehearse the real format.** USMLE's exam pages list the current structure: Step 1, for test dates on or after {{fact:usmle_step1_new_format_date}}, has {{fact:step1_block_count}} blocks of {{fact:usmle_block_minutes}} minutes in a session of about {{fact:step1_session_hours}} hours, and Step 2 CK, on or after {{fact:usmle_step2ck_new_format_date}}, has {{fact:step2ck_block_count}} blocks of {{fact:usmle_block_minutes}} minutes in a session of about {{fact:step2ck_session_hours}} hours. Confirm the current numbers for your test date on the official page.
4. **Practice the logistics too.** A calm morning is part of your preparation, not an extra.
5. **Sleep is part of the plan.** Protecting sleep in the last week is, in our opinion, worth more than one extra evening of review.

> [!CHANGED] Step 1 and Step 2 CK moved to shorter blocks for test dates on or after the dates above. Older plans and videos assume longer blocks. Simulate the current block structure and break rhythm, not the one in an old guide.

## Before you start: three decisions on Day 14

- **Is your date still right?** Decide using your assessment trend and the rule you wrote when you booked, not your mood. If you are clearly short, read the section on moving your exam date in [NBME self-assessments](/wiki/exams/nbme-self-assessments) and [scheduling with Prometric](/wiki/exams/scheduling-with-prometric) before the final days, since rescheduling close to the test date may carry a fee. Check the current figure on usmle.org.
- **Which assessment is your last full-length one?** Choose the official self-assessment for your Step (see [NBME self-assessments](/wiki/exams/nbme-self-assessments)). Other providers' assessments are not calibrated by NBME, so treat their scores with extra caution.
- **What is on your weak-area list?** Keep it short and specific. A list of everything is not a plan.

## The day-by-day plan for Step 1 and Step 2 CK (opinion)

"Volume" is relative to your normal dedicated-study day. Adjust the order if your exam date falls on a different weekday, and treat the plan as a template.

| Day | Volume | Focus | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | Heavy | Final full-length simulation | Take your official full-length self-assessment under exam conditions: your appointment start time, the current block structure and real breaks, with no pausing. |
| 13 | Medium | Debrief | Review every missed and guessed item and the content-area breakdown. Update your error log with the cause and the fix for each. Choose a short list of weak areas. |
| 12 | Heavy | Repair, first weak area | Targeted study of the first weak area, then timed question blocks on it. Read explanations for the options you rejected, not only the right answer. |
| 11 | Heavy | Repair, second weak area | The same for the next area, plus a mixed timed block so you keep switching topics like the real exam. |
| 10 | Medium | Mixed timed blocks | Timed mixed blocks in the current block length. Review errors and re-quiz items from your log. |
| 9 | Light or off | Planned rest | Take a half or full day off. Walk, eat properly and talk to someone who is not studying. Rest is part of the plan. |
| 8 | Medium to heavy | Optional second simulation | Only if your first result did not alarm you and you want a stamina rehearsal. If you do one, it should be the last. Skip it if a low result would hurt your confidence more than it would help. |
| 7 | Medium | Debrief or consolidation | Review the simulation, or run mixed timed blocks if you skipped it. Finish the study day early. |
| 6 | Medium | Weak areas, lighter | Short targeted sessions on remaining weak areas. Flashcards, if you use them, are maintenance only. |
| 5 | Medium to light | Logistics and cross-cutting content | Confirm permit details, ID and name match, test center route and arrival time. If your log shows gaps, touch cross-cutting topics from the official outline, such as biostatistics and ethics. |
| 4 | Light | Format rehearsal | A shorter timed set at the same time of day as your appointment. Rehearse your break plan: when you will eat, drink and rest. |
| 3 | Light | Skim | Read your error-log one-liners and your own short lists. No new topics. |
| 2 | Very light | Review and pack | Stop earlier than usual. Pack your bag, check permit and ID once more, and do something ordinary in the evening. |
| 1 | Rest | Rest and logistics | At most a short morning skim. Travel or visit the test center route if needed, eat a normal dinner and go to bed at your planned time. |
| Exam day | n/a | Execute | Arrive early, follow your break plan, and treat each block as a fresh start. |

> [!WARNING] If you are tempted to use the final days for a new question bank, a new deck or a long list of last-minute facts, resist it. In our opinion, this is the most common way candidates raise anxiety without raising readiness.

## Self-assessment timing in the last two weeks

- **Take your main full-length assessment around Day 14**, so you have time to review it, repair the gaps it shows and still rest. A second one on Day 8 is optional, and it should be the last.
- **Treat the result as one data point.** NBME describes its self-assessments as tools to evaluate readiness and practice for the exam. Third-party conversion charts are not official. See [NBME self-assessments](/wiki/exams/nbme-self-assessments) for how to read a report.
- **Avoid a full-length simulation in the last few days.** A result then is mostly a test of your nerves, and there is no time to act on it. This is our opinion.
- **A practice session at a test center is an option, not a requirement.** USMLE says registered examinees can book a practice session at a test center, for a fee, to get used to the environment. Consider it earlier than the final week if you have never used a computer-based testing center.

## Review strategy: what to do and what to skip

**Do:**

- Re-read your error log and re-quiz the items you missed more than once.
- Work through the official content outline and mark the areas where your log shows repeated errors.
- Do timed, mixed blocks so your brain keeps switching topics.
- Keep a one-page list of your own traps, such as misread stems, missed negatives and the wrong next step.

**Skip:**

- New resources of any kind.
- Re-watching whole lecture series.
- Comparing your assessment scores with other candidates.
- Late-night marathons. A tired brain misreads stems.

### What changes by exam

| | Step 1 | Step 2 CK |
|---|---|---|
| Emphasis in the last two weeks | Mechanisms and the basic science behind presentations, then rehearsing recognition | Next-best-step reasoning, management and breadth across disciplines |
| Typical error to fix | Missed the mechanism, confused look-alike options | Right diagnosis, wrong next step; two plausible answers |
| What the result means | Pass/fail only, so aim for a comfortable margin | A three-digit score that programs can see, but the same rest and taper rules apply |

## Sleep, food and body

These points are general practice and opinion, not medical advice.

- **Anchor your wake time to the exam.** Move your sleep schedule gradually so you are fully awake at your appointment time, rather than changing it the night before.
- **Keep caffeine and meals predictable.** Use what you already tolerate, in the pattern you plan to use on exam day.
- **Move your body.** A walk is not wasted study time.
- **Do not trade the last night's sleep for review.** If you cannot sleep, rest anyway; one poor night is survivable, and worrying about it makes it worse.
- **Pre-exam anxiety is common.** If it is stopping you from sleeping, eating or studying, read [exam anxiety and burnout](/wiki/exams/exam-anxiety-and-burnout) and consider speaking to a clinician.

## Logistics checklist

Rule (USMLE): you need your scheduling permit and an acceptable government photo ID whose name matches the permit, you should arrive early, and personal items go in a locker. The full rules are in the [exam day rules and checklist](/wiki/exams/exam-day-rules-and-checklist).

- Confirm your permit shows the right name, Step, eligibility period and test center.
- Check that your name on the ID matches the permit. USMLE says name change requests must be processed before a deadline ahead of the appointment, so fix a mismatch now.
- Plan your route and travel time with a buffer. If you are testing abroad, read [international test centers](/wiki/exams/international-test-centers).
- Know your break plan and which items are allowed in the room.
- If you are ill or unable to test, read the rules on rescheduling in [scheduling with Prometric](/wiki/exams/scheduling-with-prometric) before the day, not during it. USMLE advises examinees not to test if they are ill, under unusual personal stress or otherwise unprepared.

## If you are not ready

An honest answer two weeks out is better than a surprise on the day. If your assessment trend is below the threshold you set and your eligibility period allows, consider moving your date. A failed attempt appears on your record and counts toward the limit of {{fact:usmle_attempts_per_step}} attempts per Step, which is why many candidates treat the date as movable. Your decision should rest on data, so talk it through with someone who has seen your results.

## Step 3: what is different

Step 3 is a two-day exam given only in the United States and its territories, and Day 2 includes computer-based case simulations. The same principles apply (taper, error log, logistics), with three additions:

- **Treat the two days as separate.** Rest between them, and do not judge Day 2 by how Day 1 felt.
- **Keep CCS mechanics fresh, but do not start new cases late.** Run a case occasionally to stay fluent. For the details, see the [Step 3 CCS cases guide](/wiki/exams/step-3-ccs-cases-guide) and [how many CCS cases to practice](/wiki/exams/how-many-ccs-cases-to-practice).
- **Plan travel earlier.** Candidates traveling from abroad should arrive with time to spare. See the [Step 3 study plan](/wiki/exams/step-3-study-schedule) and [Step 3 guide](/wiki/exams/step-3).

## Common last-two-week mistakes

- Starting a new resource because a friend recommends it.
- Taking a full-length simulation the day before the exam.
- Studying until very late and arriving tired.
- Ignoring the name-matching and ID rules until the last minute.
- Skipping break rehearsal, then being surprised by how much a long session drains you.
- Comparing yourself with other candidates online.

## What to do next

Write Day 14 to Exam day on a calendar now, mark your last full-length assessment, and confirm your logistics. Then follow the plan, and keep the [exam day rules and checklist](/wiki/exams/exam-day-rules-and-checklist) open for the last week.
