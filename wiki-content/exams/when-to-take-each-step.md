For most IMGs the practical order is Step 1, then Step 2 CK, then Step 3, with Step 2 CK finished before application season so programs can see your score. The official rule is narrower: the Bulletin requires that you have passed Step 1 and Step 2 CK before Step 3, and it does not otherwise set a required order. The rest is planning.

## What the rules actually require

| Question | What the Bulletin says |
|---|---|
| Order of Step 1 and Step 2 CK | No required sequence stated beyond Step 3 requiring both |
| Step 3 | Requires passed Step 1 and Step 2 CK, plus a valid ECFMG Certificate for non-US graduates |
| Eligibility | You must qualify when you apply and on exam day |
| Scheduling | Seats open within your eligibility period; scheduling may not be available more than six months in advance |

See [USMLE eligibility for IMGs](/wiki/exams/usmle-eligibility-for-imgs) for the full list. Because policies are updated each year, read the current Bulletin before locking a plan.

## The standard sequence and why

1. **Step 1** builds the science foundation and is the gate for most of the path. See the [Step 1 guide](/wiki/exams/step-1).
2. **Step 2 CK** tests clinical knowledge and reports a score programs read. See the [Step 2 CK guide](/wiki/exams/step-2-ck).
3. **Step 3** comes later, usually tied to licensure and visa planning. See the [Step 3 guide](/wiki/exams/step-3).

## Work backward from the application

The decisive date is the application season, not the exam. Start with [the ERAS timeline](/wiki/application/eras-timeline), then count backward.

1. Decide the cycle you are targeting.
2. Decide when you want your Step 2 CK result in hand. Results take time: official guidance is to allow at least eight weeks.
3. Subtract study time and a buffer for a possible retake.
4. Check that your eligibility period and seat availability line up.

> [!WARNING] If your Step 2 CK might arrive after programs begin screening, you risk applying without a score. Some programs screen on it. Plan with margin.

## How far apart should Steps 1 and 2 CK be?

There is no official minimum gap. These are common-practice trade-offs, not rules:

| Gap | Advantages | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| Short | Knowledge is fresh; shorter total timeline | Burnout; little time to fix weak areas |
| Medium | Time for clinical review or experience | Needs discipline to stay in study mode |
| Long | Chance to do US clinical experience or research | Loss of momentum; the application window may slip |

Our opinion: choose the gap based on self-assessment trends and your clinical exposure, not on a standard number of weeks. See [NBME self-assessments](/wiki/exams/nbme-self-assessments).

## When Step 3 makes sense

Step 3 is not required to apply or begin residency. Reasons to take it earlier include visa strategy (see [Step 3 and H-1B timing](/wiki/visas/step-3-and-h1b-timing)) and licensure planning. Reasons to wait include limited study time during residency or not yet having the eligibility requirements. The Bulletin recommends, but does not require, a year of US postgraduate training before Step 3.

## Scenarios

### Recent graduate with fresh knowledge

Step 1 first, then Step 2 CK soon after, so the application includes a score. Spend any remaining time on clinical experience and research.

### Graduate who finished years ago

You may need more preparation time and a clearer plan. Consider the extra time cost in your [timeline](/wiki/start-here/how-long-does-it-take).

### Planning around H-1B

Work backward from the visa filing timeline and read the visa guides before deciding. Consider legal advice.

## Interactions to check

- **Eligibility period.** Each registration has a limited period; extensions need a request and fee. See [scheduling with Prometric](/wiki/exams/scheduling-with-prometric).
- **Retake rules.** A failed exam changes the whole plan; see [attempt limits and retakes](/wiki/exams/usmle-attempt-limits-and-retakes).
- **Cost.** Each registration is a separate fee: [total cost](/wiki/start-here/total-cost-of-matching).

## Common mistakes

- Booking Step 2 CK too late for the application season.
- Assuming Step 3 is required before applying.
- Ignoring the eligibility period when picking a date.
- Letting a gap grow because the previous exam went well.

> [!IMG TIP] Put the application opening date, your target Step 2 CK result date and the last date you could take a retake on one calendar. If the retake date is after the application opens, the plan has no safety margin.
