USMLE Step 2 Clinical Knowledge (CK) tests whether you can apply medical knowledge and clinical science to patient care, with emphasis on health promotion and disease prevention. Unlike Step 1, it still reports a **three-digit score**, so it is the exam where you can most visibly strengthen your residency application. The current minimum passing score is {{fact:step2ck_passing_standard}} for exams taken on or after July 1, 2025.

## Why Step 2 CK matters for IMGs

- **It is required.** ECFMG certification requires both [Step 1](/wiki/exams/step-1) and Step 2 CK.
- **It is the main scored exam.** With Step 1 reported as pass/fail, programs often look to Step 2 CK for a standardized number. For how programs read it, see [Step 2 CK scores and what programs look for](/wiki/exams/step-2-ck-score-and-what-it-means).
- **It is clinically oriented.** Much of the content follows what you saw on clerkships, so recent clinical work helps.

> [!IMG TIP] Treat the passing score as a floor. Your target should come from the programs and specialties you plan to apply to, not from the minimum.

## What the exam covers

The official content outline groups items by organ system, physician task and discipline. Questions are clinical vignettes asking about diagnosis, next best step, management, prognosis and prevention. The USMLE content outline and sample questions are the best guide to relative weighting; third-party summaries may be out of date.

## Exam format

Step 2 CK is a single-day, computer-based exam taken at a Prometric test center, with multiple-choice questions divided into timed blocks and scheduled breaks.

> [!CHANGED] For test dates on or after May 7, 2026, USMLE restructured the exam into more, shorter blocks with a different amount of break time and a shorter optional tutorial. Older prep advice that describes the previous layout may no longer match. Check the current numbers on the official Step 2 CK page.

Because block length and count have changed, practice with timed blocks that match the current structure and build stamina for a full day.

## Eligibility and registration for IMGs

To take Step 2 CK, you must meet the eligibility rules both when you apply and on the day of your exam. In general, that means being an enrolled student or graduate of a medical school that meets ECFMG eligibility requirements (listed in the World Directory of Medical Schools), plus other criteria in the Bulletin. See [USMLE eligibility for IMGs](/wiki/exams/usmle-eligibility-for-imgs).

The process changed in 2026:

1. ECFMG / Intealth continues to determine whether you are eligible for ECFMG certification.
2. Since January 12, 2026, FSMB manages USMLE exam services for IMGs, including registration. The Bulletin directs IMGs to apply through the FSMB website.
3. After you register, you receive a scheduling permit with your eligibility period and book your seat with Prometric. See [scheduling with Prometric](/wiki/exams/scheduling-with-prometric).

> [!WARNING] The eligibility period is limited. Extending it requires a request and a fee, and missing it means a new application and fees. Fees are non-refundable and non-transferable.

## Scoring, results and attempts

Results are reported on a three-digit scale. Scores usually arrive within about four weeks of your test date, but the official guidance is to allow at least eight weeks. Score reports remain available online for a limited time, after which you need an official transcript.

You are allowed {{fact:usmle_attempts_per_step}} attempts per Step, and timing rules apply between attempts. Read [attempt limits and retakes](/wiki/exams/usmle-attempt-limits-and-retakes) before you register. If you fail, see [what to do after failing a Step](/wiki/exams/what-to-do-after-failing-a-step).

## How to prepare

1. **Check your baseline.** Take a self-assessment early. See [NBME self-assessments](/wiki/exams/nbme-self-assessments).
2. **Choose one main question bank** and one review source. Constantly switching resources is a common time sink.
3. **Do questions in timed, mixed blocks** to match the real exam, then review every explanation.
4. **Study by error pattern.** Group misses by cause: knowledge gap, misreading, or time.
5. **Track the trend, not one score.** Repeat self-assessments at intervals to decide when to book or move your date.
6. **Simulate full days** in the final weeks, including breaks.

### Resources

USMLE publishes the content outline, sample questions and a practice session. The NBME sells official self-assessments. Commercial question banks are widely used, but this wiki does not rank them.

## Timing: when to take it

Many IMGs take Step 2 CK after Step 1, close enough that knowledge stays fresh, but the right gap depends on your clinical exposure and study plan. See [when to take each Step](/wiki/exams/when-to-take-each-step). Some also delay Step 2 CK until after finishing key US clinical experience; this is a trade-off, not a rule.

## Common mistakes

- Registering before you know your eligibility period is long enough for your study plan.
- Relying on a single self-assessment to decide readiness.
- Studying to the old format after the 2026 change.
- Ignoring stamina: a full exam day is long.
- Taking the exam too late to include the score in the application you are targeting. Check the [ERAS timeline](/wiki/application/eras-timeline).

## What comes next

After Step 2 CK, you move to [Step 3](/wiki/exams/step-3), which is often planned around residency and visa timing.
