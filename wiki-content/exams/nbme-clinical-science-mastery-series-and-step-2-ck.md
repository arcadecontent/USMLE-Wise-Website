NBME CMS forms (the Clinical Science Mastery Series) are practice exams that NBME describes as a way to check your readiness for its Clinical Science **Subject Exams**, the clerkship-style exams, and not for Step 2 CK. They can still be extra clinical practice during Step 2 CK preparation, but NBME does not present them as a Step 2 CK readiness check or a score predictor. For that job, NBME's comprehensive self-assessment is the closer match, and it is covered on our [NBME self-assessments](/wiki/exams/nbme-self-assessments) page.

> [!KEY] Short version: use CMS forms as targeted, timed practice in one clinical subject at a time. Do not use a CMS result to decide when to book Step 2 CK or to guess your score. Nothing from NBME supports that use.

## What are NBME CMS forms, and what are they called now?

NBME's current name is the **Clinical Science Mastery Series**. NBME's self-assessment listings also say "Clinical Mastery Series", and most older guides and forum threads say "CMS". All of these names point to the same family of [[nbme|NBME]] self-assessments, so do not be confused if a guide uses a different one.

| Name you will see | What it refers to |
|---|---|
| Clinical Science Mastery Series (CMS) | NBME's current name for these subject-area self-assessments |
| Clinical Mastery Series | An older or alternate name in NBME listings and older guides |
| NBME Clinical Science Subject Exams | A separate product that medical schools give to students; the CMS is meant to help you prepare for these |
| Comprehensive Clinical Science Self-Assessment | The NBME self-assessment aimed at Step 2 CK; see [NBME self-assessments](/wiki/exams/nbme-self-assessments) |

Each form covers one clinical subject area. NBME lists Clinical Neurology, Emergency Medicine, Family Medicine, Medicine, Obstetrics and Gynecology, Pediatrics, Psychiatry and Surgery. NBME says multiple forms are available for each area, with {{fact:cms_questions_per_form}} multiple-choice questions per form. It does not publish how many forms exist in each area, so we do not state a form count or numbering anywhere on this page.

## Are NBME CMS forms good for Step 2 CK?

Here is what NBME's own page says, in NBME's words: with the series, you can "assess your understanding of the clinical sciences and evaluate your readiness for an NBME Clinical Science Subject Exam." The page describes the assessments as designed for students taking an NBME Clinical Science Subject Exam.

Separating rule, fact and opinion:

- **NBME says:** the purpose is readiness for a Clinical Science Subject Exam.
- **NBME does not say (on the pages we reviewed):** that a Mastery Series result predicts a Step 2 CK score, or that the forms were built to match Step 2 CK.
- **Our opinion:** many Step 2 CK candidates use clinical subject practice anyway, because the content overlaps with what Step 2 CK tests. That is a reasonable way to practice. It is a poor way to forecast a score.

There is also a structural difference. Step 2 CK is a single computer-based day made of {{fact:step2ck_block_count}} blocks of {{fact:usmle_block_minutes}} minutes, in a session of about {{fact:step2ck_session_hours}} hours, and the official content outline spans all the clinical disciplines in that one exam. A CMS form is {{fact:cms_questions_per_form}} questions in one subject. A form can check how well you know one subject, but it does not rehearse the mixed-discipline stamina of a full Step 2 CK day. See [Step 2 CK](/wiki/exams/step-2-ck) for the official format.

## CMS forms versus the comprehensive self-assessment

The comparison below uses only what NBME states on its pages. Where NBME is silent we say so rather than fill the gap.

| | Clinical Science Mastery Series | Comprehensive Clinical Science Self-Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| NBME's stated purpose | Readiness for an NBME Clinical Science Subject Exam | An estimate of readiness for the matching USMLE Step (Step 2 CK) |
| Scope | One clinical subject area per form | Clinical science content across disciplines, not one subject |
| Questions | {{fact:cms_questions_per_form}} per form | See NBME's assessment page for the current length |
| Score feedback | A breakdown of incorrectly answered questions by content category, answer explanations, and a score interpretation guide with a conversion table to an approximated Subject Exam score | A report with performance by physician task, system and discipline; NBME says a lookup table for USMLE scores is included |
| USMLE score lookup table | No. NBME says this table exists only for the comprehensive Basic Science and Clinical Science self-assessments | Yes, per NBME's common questions page |
| Price | {{fact:cms_price}} per exam at the time of checking | Check the MyNBME portal for the current price |

> [!NOTE] NBME describes both as self-assessments for practice and feedback. NBME also says its estimates are not a guarantee of how you will do on the real exam. Treat every result as one data point.

## What the CMS score report gives you, and what it does not

According to NBME's Mastery Series page and common questions page, you get:

- a breakdown of incorrectly answered questions by content category, plus answer explanations;
- a score interpretation guide with a conversion table that translates an assessment score to an approximated total score **on the Subject Exam score scale**;
- results through NBME's INSIGHTS reporting, with longitudinal feedback across forms you take;
- access to your interactive score report for a limited period after you finish (NBME's FAQ says two years).

What it does **not** give you, on NBME's pages: a conversion to a Step 2 CK score, a percentile, or a prediction. The conversion table translates to the Subject Exam scale, which is a different exam with a different scale. We also found no NBME statement on how the CMS score itself is calculated, so we do not describe it.

If you want to know what a number means for Step 2 CK, USMLE publishes score information for the real exam. See [Step 2 CK scores and what programs look for](/wiki/exams/step-2-ck-score-and-what-it-means). Be careful with forum "CMS to Step 2 CK" conversion charts: they are not published by NBME, you cannot check what data they rest on, and an invented conversion can push you to book or cancel on bad information.

## Price, access and how to buy

- **Price:** NBME lists the Clinical Science Mastery Series at {{fact:cms_price}} per exam. NBME updates its prices, so confirm the current figure before you pay.
- **Where:** examinees buy in the MyNBME Examinee Portal. You do not need a school to buy.
- **Time to complete:** NBME says to complete an exam within one year of purchase, and you can pause and resume.
- **School vouchers:** medical schools buy vouchers through a separate NBME services portal. If you are still enrolled, check whether your school offers one; otherwise buy directly as an examinee.

Prices, forms and rules change. Open NBME's page before you buy, not an old forum post.

## Should you use CMS forms during Step 2 CK dedicated study?

This section is our opinion, not an NBME or USMLE rule.

**When CMS forms fit:**

- You have a **weak clinical subject** (for example, one you did not do a clerkship in, or did long ago) and want timed, NBME-written practice in that area.
- You already have a **baseline from a comprehensive self-assessment** and a question-bank trend, and you want to test whether targeted review worked.
- You want practice reading clerkship-style questions in a single discipline.

**When they do not fit:**

- You want one number to decide your exam date. Use the comprehensive self-assessment and your trend across several assessments instead.
- You are in the last days before your exam and have not done a full-length mixed practice. Rehearse the real block length and a full day first. See the [last two weeks study plan](/wiki/exams/last-two-weeks-study-plan).
- You are hoping to convert a result into a Step 2 CK score. NBME does not support that.

**If you do use them, a simple routine:**

1. Take a comprehensive self-assessment first so you know your overall position. See [NBME self-assessments](/wiki/exams/nbme-self-assessments).
2. Pick the one or two subjects where your errors cluster, and take a form in each as a **timed** set, in one sitting.
3. Review every explanation, including the questions you got right by guessing.
4. Log each miss by cause: knowledge gap, misread stem, or time pressure. Fix the cause, not just the fact.
5. **Do not repeat a form** after you have reviewed it. A repeat result measures memory, not knowledge.
6. Retest the weak area with different questions a few weeks later, and judge progress by the trend.

For how these practice sets fit into a full plan, see the [Step 2 CK study schedule](/wiki/exams/step-2-ck-study-schedule) and [how long to study](/wiki/exams/how-long-to-study). For where self-assessments sit next to commercial question banks, see [question banks compared](/wiki/exams/question-banks-compared).

> [!TIP] Spending a lot on practice forms is not the goal. A small number of well-reviewed, timed sets beats a stack of forms you rush through.

## Why this page does not rank forms or give answers

You may have arrived searching for which form is "hardest", a form-by-form comparison, or answers. This page deliberately gives none of those, for two reasons.

- **No official basis for a ranking.** NBME does not publish how many forms exist in each subject, how they are numbered, or how their difficulty compares. Any ranking you see online is unverified opinion, and it can be outdated because NBME updates forms over time. We would rather give you nothing than something that cannot be checked.
- **No answers.** Answers and retired content are NBME's confidential material. We do not provide them, link to them or discuss where to find them. They also defeat the purpose: you cannot measure readiness on questions you have already seen with the answers.

The USMLE Bulletin describes USMLE examination materials as the confidential, copyrighted property of the USMLE program and says reproducing or distributing them violates the program's legal rights. That rule concerns the real exam, not NBME's self-assessments. Our opinion is that the same caution applies: if anything is offered to you as leaked or retired exam content, stay away. For the consequences of integrity problems on the real exam, read the [irregular behavior policy](/wiki/ecfmg/irregular-behavior-policy).

## What is different for IMGs

NBME's self-assessments are available to examinees directly through MyNBME, so you do not need a US school to buy them. A few IMG-specific points, as opinion:

- If you finished clerkships some time ago, or your school's clinical curriculum differed from US clerkships, subject-by-subject practice can show **style gaps** (how questions are written) as well as content gaps.
- If you graduated years ago, take the comprehensive assessment as a baseline **before** buying a stack of subject forms. It tells you which subjects deserve the spending.
- Practice is only useful if you can get a seat for the real exam. Check your eligibility window and test center availability early. See [how to register for USMLE exams](/wiki/exams/how-to-register-for-usmle-exams) and [how to reschedule a USMLE exam](/wiki/exams/how-to-reschedule-a-usmle-exam).

## Common mistakes with CMS forms

- Treating a CMS result as a Step 2 CK score predictor.
- Using a third-party conversion table as if NBME had published it.
- Repeating a form after reviewing it and trusting the higher number.
- Spending every form early and having no clean, unseen assessment for the final readiness check.
- Skipping full-length mixed practice because the subject forms felt manageable.

## What to do next

If you do not yet have a baseline, start with the comprehensive assessment on the [NBME self-assessments](/wiki/exams/nbme-self-assessments) page, then use CMS forms for the subjects it flags. Build the rest of your plan around the [Step 2 CK study schedule](/wiki/exams/step-2-ck-study-schedule), and keep your exam date flexible until your comprehensive results and question practice agree.
