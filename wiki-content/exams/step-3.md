USMLE Step 3 is the last exam in the USMLE sequence. It assesses whether you can apply medical knowledge and biomedical and clinical science essential for the **unsupervised practice of medicine**, with emphasis on patient management in ambulatory settings. For IMGs, it matters mainly for licensure and, sometimes, for visa strategy.

## Why IMGs think about Step 3 early

- **Licensure.** Many state medical boards expect all three Steps for a full license, and some set time limits for completing them. Rules vary by state; check the board in the state where you want to practice.
- **H-1B planning.** Some employers and visa strategies are easier with Step 3 completed. Whether that applies to you depends on your program and legal advice. See [Step 3 and H-1B timing](/wiki/visas/step-3-and-h1b-timing).
- **Application signal.** For some applicants, a passed Step 3 is an extra credential. Whether it helps depends on the program, so do not assume it replaces a weak earlier result.

> [!NOTE] Step 3 is not required to apply to or start residency. Do not delay Step 2 CK or the application cycle for it.

## Eligibility for IMGs

According to the current Bulletin, to take Step 3 you must:

1. Have passed Step 1 and Step 2 CK (and the most recent administration of the required exams must be passed).
2. Hold an MD or DO from an accredited US school, **or** an MD-equivalent from a non-US school listed in the World Directory of Medical Schools.
3. If you are a non-US graduate, hold a valid, unexpired ECFMG Certificate.
4. Meet the other criteria in the Bulletin.

USMLE recommends, but does not require, that you have completed or be near completion of at least one year of postgraduate training in an accredited US program. See [USMLE eligibility for IMGs](/wiki/exams/usmle-eligibility-for-imgs).

> [!WARNING] Previously licensed US physicians may not be eligible, and some time-limit rules interact with state boards. Confirm your situation with your registration organization before applying.

## Format

Step 3 is a two-day computer-based exam.

| | Day 1 | Day 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Foundations of Independent Practice (FIP) | Advanced Clinical Medicine (ACM) |
| Content | Multiple-choice items on foundational science and clinical care | Multiple-choice items plus computer-based case simulations (CCS) |
| Length | A long single sitting with scheduled breaks | A longer sitting than Day 1, with breaks |

Item counts, block lengths and break times are set by USMLE and updated from time to time; see the official [Step 3 exam content page](https://www.usmle.org/step-exams/step-3/step-3-exam-content) for the current numbers and the date they apply from. USMLE also mentions a new test software experience, so check the official page before you prepare.

### Computer-based case simulations

CCS is the part many candidates fear because it differs from multiple-choice. You manage a simulated patient by ordering tests, treatments and follow-up while time advances. Practice with the official CCS orientation and learn the interface early so it is not new on exam day.

## Registration and scheduling

IMGs register for Step 3 through FSMB, which has managed USMLE services for IMGs since January 12, 2026. Step 3 is administered only in the US and its territories. After registration you receive a scheduling permit and book your seats at Prometric. See [scheduling with Prometric](/wiki/exams/scheduling-with-prometric).

If you are outside the US, you will need a visa to travel for the exam. See [B-1/B-2 for interviews and exams](/wiki/visas/b1-b2-for-interviews-and-exams). Visitor visa applications can take longer than exam registration.

## Scoring and attempts

Results are reported on a three-digit scale with a minimum passing score set by USMLE. The current figure is on the [USMLE scoring page](https://www.usmle.org/scores-transcripts/examination-results-and-scoring). The same attempt limits apply as for other Steps; see [attempt limits and retakes](/wiki/exams/usmle-attempt-limits-and-retakes).

## When should an IMG take Step 3?

There is no single right answer.

| Option | Advantages | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| Before residency | May help H-1B plans or application | Travel and visa needs; competes with other preparation |
| During PGY-1 | Clinical context and often institutional support | Study time is scarce with work hours |
| Later | More time to focus | May delay licensure or limit visa options |

Our opinion: decide by working backward from your visa and licensure plans, not from the date you happen to feel ready. See [when to take each Step](/wiki/exams/when-to-take-each-step).

## Preparing

1. Review the official content outline and sample items.
2. Use a question bank with ambulatory and patient management emphasis.
3. Learn CCS with official practice material.
4. Use a self-assessment close to your exam date. See [NBME self-assessments](/wiki/exams/nbme-self-assessments).
5. Plan rest between the two days.

## Common mistakes

- Taking Step 3 before understanding your visa or licensure need.
- Ignoring CCS practice.
- Underestimating a two-day exam.
- Assuming Step 3 can be taken abroad.

Review [Step 2 CK](/wiki/exams/step-2-ck) for the preceding step.
