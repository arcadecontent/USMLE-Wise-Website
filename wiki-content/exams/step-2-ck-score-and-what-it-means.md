Step 2 CK is reported on a three-digit scale, and the minimum passing score is {{fact:step2ck_passing_standard}} for exams administered on or after July 1, 2025. That number only tells you that you passed. What programs "look for" is a separate question with no single answer, and this page explains how to think about it without relying on rumours.

## What the number means

USMLE sets a minimum passing standard and reports results on a three-digit scale. A pass means you met that standard; it does not rank you. Your score report also shows how each content area compared with your overall performance.

Because the passing standard has changed over time, be careful with old advice. Always compare scores against the current standard on the [USMLE scoring page](https://www.usmle.org/scores-transcripts/examination-results-and-scoring).

## Why the score matters more now

Step 1 has been pass/fail since January 26, 2022, so Step 2 CK is the main scored USMLE exam that programs can compare. This does not mean every program uses it the same way:

- Some programs use filters at the screening stage.
- Others read the whole application and use the score as one input.
- Some weigh US clinical experience and letters as heavily.

See [how programs screen applications](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters).

> [!IMG TIP] Do not rely on a forum screenshot of "average scores" for your specialty. Use official data from NRMP and the programs' own websites, and check the year.

## How to find out what your target programs expect

1. **Read program websites** for stated requirements, such as minimum exam results or years since graduation.
2. **Use NRMP data** on matched applicants, including the program director survey, where available. Check the year and the specialty. Do not generalize from US MD data to IMGs without checking. See the [NRMP data pages](https://www.nrmp.org/match-data-analytics/).
3. **Look at specialty pages** such as [choosing a specialty](/wiki/specialties/choosing-a-specialty) and [IMG match rates by specialty](/wiki/specialties/img-match-rates-by-specialty).
4. **Ask mentors** who have recently matched, and treat the answers as anecdote, not rules.

## Set a target, not a hope

A useful target comes from the programs you plan to apply to, not from the passing standard. Our opinion (not an official rule):

| Step | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Before studying | Which specialties and programs am I aiming at, and what do they say about scores? |
| During studying | Are my self-assessment trends on track for that goal? See [NBME self-assessments](/wiki/exams/nbme-self-assessments) |
| After the exam | Does my score fit the list I planned? If not, what else can strengthen my application? |

## What if your score is lower than hoped

A lower score limits some options but not all. Programs also look at US clinical experience, letters, research, recency of graduation and fit. Consider:

- Widening your list and diversifying specialties within what is realistic. See [how many programs to apply to](/wiki/application/how-many-programs-to-apply-to).
- Strengthening other parts of the application: [USCE](/wiki/clinical-experience/usce-explained), [letters](/wiki/application/letters-of-recommendation) and [research](/wiki/research/why-research-matters-for-imgs).
- Being honest and specific about growth in your explanation of any weaker results.

> [!WARNING] You cannot retake Step 2 CK to raise a passing score. If you are weighing a delay to improve your preparation, decide before you take the exam, not after. See [attempt limits and retakes](/wiki/exams/usmle-attempt-limits-and-retakes).

## Timing of results

Score reports typically arrive within about four weeks, but you should allow at least eight weeks, and the report stays online for a limited period afterward. If your application timeline is tight, build in time for results. See [when to take each Step](/wiki/exams/when-to-take-each-step).

## Mistakes to avoid

- Using the passing standard as your goal.
- Trusting old averages from before the pass/fail change.
- Comparing yourself with US MD applicants without checking the IMG data.
- Treating a single self-assessment as your final score.
- Assuming a high score alone offsets other gaps.

## Putting it together

Treat Step 2 CK as one part of a profile. Read the full [Step 2 CK guide](/wiki/exams/step-2-ck) for format and registration, and use your results to plan the rest of the application.

> [!NOTE] Passing standards and score reporting are set by USMLE and can change. Check the official scoring page before you use any number from this wiki.
