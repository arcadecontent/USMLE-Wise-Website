There is no single best USMLE question bank, and we do not rank brands. What matters is whether a bank's questions, explanations and analytics suit how you learn, and whether you will finish it before your exam. This page gives you neutral criteria and a way to test any bank yourself. Prices and features change often, so confirm details on the provider's own site.

## What a question bank is for

A question bank does two jobs.

1. **It teaches.** Working through questions and reading explanations is a primary way many candidates learn and retain material. The step guides ([Step 1](/wiki/exams/step-1), [Step 2 CK](/wiki/exams/step-2-ck)) describe questions as the main study method.
2. **It measures.** Your performance tells you what to study next. But a bank's own percentage is a weaker measure of readiness than an independent, standardized assessment. See [NBME self-assessments](/wiki/exams/nbme-self-assessments).

Your content checklist should come from the official source. USMLE publishes content outlines and specifications for each Step on its [prepare your exam](https://www.usmle.org/prepare-your-exam) page. Use the outline to check any bank's coverage.

## Neutral criteria for comparing banks

| Criterion | What to look for | Questions to ask yourself |
|---|---|---|
| Question style | Clinical vignettes that resemble the length, reasoning and format of the exam | Do the questions make me reason, or just recall a fact? Do they feel like the official sample items? |
| Difficulty and calibration | A range of difficulty, with some questions harder than the exam and many close to it | Do I feel challenged but able to learn? |
| Explanations | Clear reasoning for why the right answer is right and why each wrong answer is wrong, plus visuals where helpful | Can I learn the concept from the explanation alone? Is it accurate and current? |
| Content coverage | Alignment with the official outline for your Step | Which disciplines or systems are thin? |
| Question formats | Support for formats currently used on the exam | Does it reflect recent item types? |
| Analytics | Performance by subject, system and question type, and tracking over time | Can I find my weak areas and see trends without effort? |
| Test modes | Timed and untimed, tutor mode, custom blocks, full-length simulation | Can I rehearse the block structure of the current exam format? |
| Interface | How close the testing screen feels to the real software, including tools like lab values | Will the real interface feel familiar? |
| Cost model | One-time purchase, subscription, access length, extensions | How long do I need access, and what happens if I need more time? |
| Access and language | Works in my region and device, and in my language of study | Can I use it reliably where I study? |
| Support and updates | How often content is reviewed and corrected, and how errors are handled | Is there a clear way to report errors? |

### Notes on a few criteria

**Question style.** Exam items are clinical vignettes that ask you to recognize a presentation and reason about mechanism, diagnosis or management. Questions that test isolated recall are less useful practice. Since 2026, Step 1 and Step 2 CK also use shorter {{fact:usmle_block_minutes}}-minute blocks in the current format, so check that the bank lets you practice in similar blocks.

**Explanations.** In our opinion this is the most important criterion for IMGs studying alone. If you cannot understand why you were wrong, the bank is a testing tool, not a teaching tool.

**Analytics.** Useful analytics show *why* you miss questions, not just *which topic*. If a bank's analytics are limited, supplement them with your own error log (see [how long to study](/wiki/exams/how-long-to-study)).

**Cost model.** We do not state prices, since they change. Compare total cost for the access period you actually need, not the headline price, and check whether renewal, extensions or add-ons cost extra. Use free trials and sample questions, but read what is limited in a trial.

## How to evaluate a bank yourself

1. **Define your exam and timeline.** Step 1, Step 2 CK and Step 3 need different coverage; know how many weeks you have.
2. **Check the official outline** and identify two or three areas you know you are weak in.
3. **Try a free trial or a sample block.** Do 20 to 40 questions in each of two or three subjects, including one weak area.
4. **Read every explanation**, including those for questions you got right. Mark each as clear, adequate or poor.
5. **Check an official sample question set** from USMLE and compare the style and difficulty with what the bank gives you.
6. **Test the interface.** Do a timed block. Try the tools you will use on exam day.
7. **Calculate how many questions you can realistically complete** by your exam date and whether the bank is large enough for your plan, without being so large you cannot finish.
8. **Decide on a primary bank and a measuring tool.** One main bank, plus independent self-assessments on a schedule.

## How to use a bank well

- **Do questions in mixed, timed blocks for most of your prep** once your base is built. Use tutor mode, if at all, early.
- **Review every question.** Reviewing takes as long as answering.
- **Log errors by cause**: knowledge gap, misread stem, reasoning error, timing. Fix the cause.
- **Do not memorize questions.** Repeating a bank to raise your percentage inflates your score without testing you.
- **Use independent assessments** to check readiness, since a bank's percent-correct varies with the mix of questions and how you use it.

> [!IMG TIP] If you already use a bank and it works for you, do not switch because someone online recommends another. A change late in preparation costs time and rarely adds more than finishing what you started.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Buying several banks and finishing none.
- Treating a percent-correct as a score prediction.
- Using the bank only to see right and wrong, not to learn.
- Choosing by price alone or by hype alone.
- Using unofficial sources that claim to share real exam questions. USMLE rules say you must keep exam content confidential, and trying to obtain or share it is treated as irregular behavior.
