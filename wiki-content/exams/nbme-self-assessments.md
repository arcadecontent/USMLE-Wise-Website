NBME self-assessments are practice exams produced by the organization that develops USMLE content, and they are the closest official practice tool to the real exam. Use them to find out where you stand and to track a **trend**: they give you an estimate of readiness, not a forecast, and NBME itself says its estimate is not a guarantee. This page is the hub for choosing NBME forms, timing them, reading the result and deciding what to do next.

## What the NBME self-assessments are

NBME offers one comprehensive self-assessment for each USMLE Step:

| Exam | NBME self-assessment |
|---|---|
| Step 1 | Comprehensive Basic Science Self-Assessment |
| Step 2 CK | Comprehensive Clinical Science Self-Assessment |
| Step 3 | Comprehensive Clinical Medicine Self-Assessment |

NBME says the assessments help you evaluate your readiness for the matching exam. You buy them through NBME's online portal (MyNBME) and take them online, from any location with a suitable computer and internet connection. They are not the same as USMLE's own practice session at a Prometric test center, which uses USMLE sample materials, must be taken onsite and gives limited feedback.

After you finish, you can review the items with answer explanations. NBME says you can review and print your performance profile for two years afterwards. The sample Clinical Science report shows performance by physician task, system and discipline, and a longitudinal view when you take more than one form.

Other companies publish their own assessments (UWSA is one commonly mentioned example). These are separate products, not official, and their scoring is not calibrated by NBME. Format details such as number of questions and timing are on each assessment's page and can change, so check before you purchase.

USMLE also provides free official sample questions. Those are for familiarization with the format, not score estimates. See [Step 2 CK](/wiki/exams/step-2-ck) for the official materials.

## Which NBME forms should you take?

NBME sells several forms of each comprehensive assessment, and the current list is in the MyNBME portal. On forums, forms are usually called by number. NBME has announced new forms and retired older ones in the past, so a form number from an old thread may no longer be on sale, and older reports may not match what you see today.

Our opinion, not an NBME rule:

- **Match the assessment to your Step.** The Basic Science assessment is for Step 1, the Clinical Science assessment is for Step 2 CK, and the Clinical Medicine assessment is for Step 3.
- **Take each form once.** Repeating a form after you have reviewed it inflates the result.
- **Do not spend every form early.** Keep at least one unseen form for your final check.
- **Do not compare scores across forms or across people as if they were the same.** Different forms have different content mixes. Compare your own trend and your content-area breakdown.
- **Check what the report includes.** NBME's Basic Science and Clinical Science pages describe an estimated probability of passing. The Step 3 assessment page describes answer explanations and longitudinal feedback; check that page for what its report shows.

> [!NOTE] The Step 3 self-assessment is described by NBME as multiple-choice questions. It does not replace practice on the case simulations. See the [Step 3 study plan](/wiki/exams/step-3-study-schedule) for how to practice them using USMLE's official materials.

## How to time an NBME self-assessment

NBME describes each form as multiple-choice questions divided into timed sections. It offers two pacing modes: standard-paced, which NBME says matches USMLE timing, and self-paced, which gives four times as long per question. NBME also lets you pause, which stops the timer, and says answers are final once you lock a section.

Practical points (our opinion where marked):

1. **Use standard-paced for any result you will use to decide your exam date.** Opinion: a self-paced result is a learning tool, and it is not comparable with a result under real time pressure.
2. **Take it in one sitting, in exam conditions.** Pausing is available, but for a realistic rehearsal limit yourself to the breaks you would have on test day.
3. **Do not assume it rehearses your exam-day rhythm.** NBME's current pages describe longer sections than the {{fact:usmle_block_minutes}}-minute blocks USMLE uses for exams in the new format. For Step 2 CK, that format applies to test dates on or after {{fact:usmle_step2ck_new_format_date}}. Practice the real block length separately.
4. **Block out the whole sitting, plus a separate review session.** The review is where the value is; see below.

## How predictive are NBME self-assessments?

Short answer: useful, but not precise, and NBME does not claim otherwise.

What NBME says:

- Its Basic Science and Clinical Science assessment pages describe an estimated probability of passing the matching USMLE Step, and add that the estimate is not a guarantee, because many factors (for example, changing levels of knowledge) can affect performance on the real exam.
- Its common questions page says those two assessments include a lookup table for USMLE scores.
- NBME sample score reports say that the total score estimates your performance and that, if you were retested under similar conditions, the result would fall within a stated range of points two-thirds of the time. They also highlight a low-passing band near the minimum passing level.
- Older NBME guides state that the self-assessment is provided for educational purposes only and that the number of incorrect answers should not be used to predict your score.

What we could not find: a current NBME-published figure for how closely self-assessment results track real exam results. We do not quote one, and you should be cautious about any "within X points" claim online, because you cannot check what data it rests on. Treat the results as one input alongside your question practice, your error log and your trend.

> [!WARNING] Score conversion tables, calculators and "predictors" on forums and social media are not published by NBME. Use them, if at all, as rough conversation points, never as a promise.

## NBME score conversion and score calculators

If you search for an NBME score calculator or conversion chart, you will find many. Only one source is official: the report you receive with your own assessment, including the score lookup table NBME includes with the Basic Science and Clinical Science assessments.

Why third-party tables disagree with each other and with your report:

- They are not built or checked by NBME, so you cannot tell what data they rest on.
- They may be built for an older form, older reporting layout or older exam format.
- They convert a single number, while NBME's reports describe a range and an estimate.

If you use a third-party table, treat it as a conversation point, never as a basis for booking or cancelling your exam.

## Why results vary

- **Exam version.** Different forms and different content mixes give different results.
- **Conditions.** Fatigue, timing, noise and nerves matter.
- **Familiarity.** Repeating an assessment after reviewing it inflates results.
- **Scale and format changes.** USMLE changed the Step 2 CK format for test dates on or after {{fact:usmle_step2ck_new_format_date}}, so older reports of how a given assessment felt may not match.
- **Measurement error.** Every score has a margin.

## How to use assessments well

### 1. Take a baseline early

Do one near the start of dedicated study. It will feel bad. That is the point: it shows where to focus.

### 2. Take assessments at intervals

Space them by weeks, not days, so you have time to study the results. Take them under realistic conditions: one sitting, timed, with proper breaks.

### 3. Review them thoroughly

Review the content-area breakdown and every question you missed, including those you guessed correctly. Sort errors into three groups:

| Error type | Fix |
|---|---|
| Knowledge gap | Restudy and do questions on the topic |
| Reasoning or misread | Slow down on stems; practice eliminating answers |
| Time pressure | Practice pacing in timed mixed blocks |

### 4. Track a trend

Write down date, score, and your three main weak areas for each assessment. A rising or flat-and-high trend across several assessments says more than one outlier.

### 5. Decide with rules, not feelings

Before the next test, write your rule, for example: "Book only if my last two assessments are above my target by a safe margin." This protects you from booking after one good day. It also protects your limited attempts; see [attempt limits and retakes](/wiki/exams/usmle-attempt-limits-and-retakes).

## How far out from the exam should you take them?

NBME's pages we reviewed give no guidance on when to take a self-assessment or how many to take, so this is our opinion and it should flex with your situation.

- **Baseline:** near the start of dedicated study.
- **Checkpoints:** every few weeks during the core and later phases, with enough days between them to study the results.
- **Final check:** early enough that you can still act on what it shows.

> [!IMG TIP] Take your last assessment far enough from exam day to still act on it. A result the week before is mostly a nerve test. For example plans built around these checkpoints, see the [Step 1](/wiki/exams/step-1-study-schedule), [Step 2 CK](/wiki/exams/step-2-ck-study-schedule) and [Step 3](/wiki/exams/step-3-study-schedule) study schedules.

## IMG-specific points

- If your school's curriculum differed from US clerkships, the clinical assessments may expose style gaps before content gaps. Adjust early.
- If you have been out of school for some time, take the baseline before you buy a long course or commit to a date.
- Cost counts. Plan the number of assessments in your budget; see [total cost of matching](/wiki/start-here/total-cost-of-matching).

## When to move your exam date

Consider moving if your trend is flat or falling, your weak areas are broad, or you cannot yet finish assessments under time. Moving costs a fee inside 45 days of the appointment, but a failed attempt costs far more. See [scheduling with Prometric](/wiki/exams/scheduling-with-prometric) and [when to take each Step](/wiki/exams/when-to-take-each-step).

## What if the result is low

A bad assessment is information, not a verdict. Break down the cause, adjust the plan and retest later. If you have already failed a Step, see [what to do after failing a Step](/wiki/exams/what-to-do-after-failing-a-step).

> [!NOTE] Assessment versions, formats and score reporting change. Read the information on NBME's page for each assessment before you buy.
