NBME self-assessments are practice exams produced by the organization that develops USMLE content. They are the closest official tool to the real exam and the best way to find out where you stand, but they are not a guarantee. Use them to track a **trend**, find weak areas and decide whether to keep your test date or move it.

## What exists

The NBME offers three comprehensive self-assessments for USMLE exams:

| Exam | NBME self-assessment |
|---|---|
| Step 1 | Comprehensive Basic Science Self-Assessment |
| Step 2 CK | Comprehensive Clinical Science Self-Assessment |
| Step 3 | Comprehensive Clinical Medicine Self-Assessment |

You buy them through NBME's online portal. Format details, such as number of questions and timing, are on each assessment's "Learn more" page and can change, so check before you purchase. Other companies publish their own assessments (UWSA is one commonly mentioned example). These are separate products, not official, and their scoring is not calibrated by NBME.

USMLE also provides free official sample questions and a practice session at a test center. Those are for familiarization with the format, not score estimates. See [Step 2 CK](/wiki/exams/step-2-ck) for the official materials.

## What the score does and does not mean

NBME sample score reports say that the total score estimates your performance and that, if you were retested under similar conditions, the result would fall within a stated range of points two-thirds of the time. They also highlight a low-passing band near the minimum passing level.

Older NBME guides state that the self-assessment is provided for educational purposes only, is not intended to predict performance on the real exam, and that translation to the exam scale is offered because users ask for it. The number of incorrect answers should not be used to predict your score. We could not find a current official statement that supports predicting a specific Step score from one result, so treat any conversion chart you find online as unofficial.

> [!WARNING] Score conversion tables on forums and social media are not published by NBME. Use them, if at all, as rough conversation points, never as a promise.

## Why results vary

- **Exam version.** Different forms and different content mixes give different results.
- **Conditions.** Fatigue, timing, noise and nerves matter.
- **Familiarity.** Repeating an assessment after reviewing it inflates results.
- **Scale and format changes.** USMLE changed the Step 2 CK format for test dates on or after {{fact:usmle_step2ck_new_format_date}}, so older reports of how a given assessment felt may not match.
- **Measurement error.** Every score has a margin.

## How to use assessments well

### 1. Take a baseline early

Do one near the start of dedicated study. It will feel bad. That is the point: it shows where to focus.

### 2. Take assessments at intervals

Space them by weeks, not days, so you have time to study the results. Take them under realistic conditions: one sitting, timed, with proper breaks.

### 3. Review them thoroughly

Review the content-area breakdown and every question you missed, including those you guessed correctly. Sort errors into three groups:

| Error type | Fix |
|---|---|
| Knowledge gap | Restudy and do questions on the topic |
| Reasoning or misread | Slow down on stems; practice eliminating answers |
| Time pressure | Practice pacing in timed mixed blocks |

### 4. Track a trend

Write down date, score, and your three main weak areas for each assessment. A rising or flat-and-high trend across several assessments says more than one outlier.

### 5. Decide with rules, not feelings

Before the next test, write your rule, for example: "Book only if my last two assessments are above my target by a safe margin." This protects you from booking after one good day. It also protects your limited attempts; see [attempt limits and retakes](/wiki/exams/usmle-attempt-limits-and-retakes).

> [!IMG TIP] Take your last assessment far enough from exam day to still act on it. A result the week before is mostly a nerve test.

## IMG-specific points

- If your school's curriculum differed from US clerkships, the clinical assessments may expose style gaps before content gaps. Adjust early.
- If you have been out of school for some time, take the baseline before you buy a long course or commit to a date.
- Cost counts. Plan the number of assessments in your budget; see [total cost of matching](/wiki/start-here/total-cost-of-matching).

## When to move your exam date

Consider moving if your trend is flat or falling, your weak areas are broad, or you cannot yet finish assessments under time. Moving costs a fee inside 45 days of the appointment, but a failed attempt costs far more. See [scheduling with Prometric](/wiki/exams/scheduling-with-prometric) and [when to take each Step](/wiki/exams/when-to-take-each-step).

## What if the result is low

A bad assessment is information, not a verdict. Break down the cause, adjust the plan and retest later. If you have already failed a Step, see [what to do after failing a Step](/wiki/exams/what-to-do-after-failing-a-step).

> [!NOTE] Assessment versions, formats and score reporting change. Read the information on NBME's page for each assessment before you buy.
