A solid Step 1 plan has three parts: **phases** that move from rebuilding to practicing to simulating, **checkpoints** where you measure yourself with independent assessments, and an **error log** that turns mistakes into a to-do list. How many weeks each phase takes is set by your starting point, not by a template. Below are the method, then three example timelines (about 3, 6 and 12 months). They are illustrations, not promises, and no schedule can guarantee a pass.

Step 1 is reported as pass/fail only (since {{fact:step1_pass_fail_since}}), and you get a limited number of attempts per Step ({{fact:usmle_attempts_per_step}}). Read the full [Step 1 guide](/wiki/exams/step-1) for format and eligibility first.

## What you are preparing for

According to USMLE's Step 1 page, exams on or after {{fact:usmle_step1_new_format_date}} use {{fact:step1_block_count}} blocks of up to 20 questions, {{fact:usmle_block_minutes}} minutes each, in one {{fact:step1_session_hours}}-hour session, with a minimum of {{fact:usmle_min_break_minutes}} minutes of break time. Earlier dates used seven 60-minute blocks. Confirm the format for your test date on the official page. Practice in blocks of that length so the timing is familiar.

## The method

### 1. Baseline first

Take an independent self-assessment early, under realistic conditions. See [NBME self-assessments](/wiki/exams/nbme-self-assessments). The aim is not a score to be proud of, but a map: which disciplines and systems are weak, and how fast you work.

Also check the official content outline and mark each area as strong, shaky or unseen.

### 2. Choose resources and cap them

One primary question bank and one core review source per weak subject is usually enough. See [question banks compared](/wiki/exams/question-banks-compared). More resources is not more learning.

### 3. Move through four phases

| Phase | Purpose | Typical activities |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation | Rebuild weak or unseen basic sciences | Targeted review of weak subjects, short question sets with explanations |
| Core | Make questions your main study method | Mixed, timed blocks; review every explanation; update the error log |
| Integration | Connect systems and sharpen test-taking | Timed full blocks across subjects, weak-area targeting, spaced review of the log |
| Final | Simulate and consolidate | Full-length practice days, a final self-assessment, rest and logistics |

### 4. Keep an error log

For every missed (and guessed) question, record: the topic, why you missed it, and what you will do about it. Categories that work for most candidates:

- **Knowledge gap:** I did not know the fact or mechanism.
- **Misread:** I knew it but missed a detail in the stem.
- **Reasoning:** I knew the facts but chose poorly between options.
- **Timing/stamina:** I rushed or faded.

Review the log weekly. If one category dominates, change your method, not your resources.

### 5. Add checkpoints

At fixed points, take an independent self-assessment and look at the trend, not a single result. Ask: Is the trend flat, improving or erratic? Do the weak areas match the error log? Is my result consistent with the level I decided in advance is safe? Self-assessment scores are estimates, so interpret them with the guidance in the NBME report.

### 6. Decide with rules you set in advance

Write down before you start: what trend and consistency would make you keep the date, and what would make you move it. See [how long to study](/wiki/exams/how-long-to-study).

## Three example timelines

These tables show how the same phases can be stretched or compressed. They are examples for different starting points. Do not copy a timeline; match the starting point first.

### Example A: about 3 months (recent graduate or student with a strong base)

Starting point: finished basic sciences recently, baseline assessment suggests few weak systems.

| Weeks | Phase | Focus | Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline | Self-assessment, outline mapping, resource choice | Baseline map |
| 2-3 | Foundation | Weak systems only, brief review plus questions | |
| 4-9 | Core | Daily mixed timed blocks, error-log review twice a week | Assessment around week 6 |
| 10-11 | Integration | Full-length practice, weak-area repair | Assessment around week 11 |
| 12 | Final | Light review, rest, exam logistics | |

### Example B: about 6 months (several years since basic sciences, mixed baseline)

Starting point: moderate base, some gaps, studying full time or most of the day.

| Weeks | Phase | Focus | Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Baseline | Assessment, outline mapping, plan | Baseline map |
| 3-9 | Foundation | Systematic rebuild by system with short question sets | Assessment around week 9 |
| 10-18 | Core | Mixed, timed blocks; error log; second pass of weak areas | Assessment around week 15 |
| 19-23 | Integration | Full blocks, endurance, cross-system integration | Assessment around week 21 |
| 24 | Final | Simulations, consolidation, rest | Final check |

### Example C: about 12 months (long gap, part-time study, or working alongside)

Starting point: weak or distant basic-science base, studying part-time around work or other duties.

| Months | Phase | Focus | Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline | Assessment, outline mapping, routine building | Baseline map |
| 2-5 | Foundation | Steady system-by-system rebuild; short daily question sets | Assessment end of month 4 |
| 6-9 | Core | Increase question volume; mixed timed blocks; log reviews | Assessment at months 7 and 9 |
| 10-11 | Integration | Full blocks, stamina, repair of repeated errors | Assessment at month 11 |
| 12 | Final | Simulations, consolidation, rest | Final check |

> [!NOTE] These timelines are not a recommended length for you. A long timeline is not a safer plan if you spend it passively, and a short one is not risky if your data supports it.

## Weekly rhythm (any plan)

- Daily: timed blocks and review, with a defined stop time.
- Twice weekly: error-log review and short re-quiz of past errors.
- Weekly: one longer block set to build stamina, and a plan for the next week.
- Every two to four weeks (core and later): independent self-assessment.
- Protect sleep and one rest day a week. Fatigue lowers practice quality.

## Logistics that change your timeline

- Your eligibility period and permit determine the dates you can test. See [scheduling with Prometric](/wiki/exams/scheduling-with-prometric).
- Seats can be limited. See [test centers outside the US](/wiki/exams/international-test-centers).
- A reschedule within 45 calendar days of the appointment carries a fee, according to the Bulletin, so decide early.
- Know the rules for [exam day](/wiki/exams/exam-day-rules-and-checklist).

> [!IMG TIP] Build a buffer between your planned date and your eligibility deadline. If the data says you need three more weeks, you want the ability to move without losing the registration.
