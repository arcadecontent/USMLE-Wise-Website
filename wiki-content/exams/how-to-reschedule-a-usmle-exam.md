To reschedule a USMLE exam, change your appointment on the Prometric website using your scheduling permit, as long as the new date falls inside your assigned eligibility period and you act before the exam date. A change made {{fact:reschedule_free_days_before}} before your first test day is free, and later changes cost more the closer you get.

This page separates **Rule** (what USMLE states) from **Practice** and **opinion** (ours). The rules apply to Step 1, Step 2 CK and Step 3 unless a row says otherwise. If you have not registered yet, start with [how to register for USMLE exams](/wiki/exams/how-to-register-for-usmle-exams).

> [!CHANGED] USMLE introduced an appointment change fee schedule on July 1, 2026, with a free tier for early changes. Older guides may describe a single fee for changes close to the exam, or an earlier fee structure. Use the current figures on usmle.org.

## How to reschedule a USMLE exam, step by step

1. **Find your permit and your Prometric confirmation number.** USMLE tells you to reschedule using the permit instructions and the confirmation number.
2. **Check when your eligibility period ends.** Your new date must fall inside it.
3. **Count the days to your first test day.** That count sets your fee (see the table below). USMLE says the local time at the Regional Registration Center for your Prometric region decides which tier applies, so count from that time zone.
4. **Check availability and closures** at your center and nearby ones on Prometric. See [test centers outside the US](/wiki/exams/international-test-centers).
5. **Reschedule on Prometric** and pay any fee it shows.
6. **Keep the new confirmation.** Only then change travel, leave from work or accommodation.
7. **If your window is too short,** look at the extension option below before the window ends.

## Reschedule fees by how much notice you give

These are the fees on USMLE's rescheduling page for all regions. They are charged per exam, and Prometric charges them, so what you pay is shown at the point you confirm.

| Notice before the first day of the exam | Fee |
|---|---|
| {{fact:reschedule_free_days_before}} | No fee |
| 31 to 45 days | {{fact:reschedule_fee_31_45}} |
| 6 to 30 days | {{fact:reschedule_fee_6_30}} |
| 5 or fewer days | Step 1 {{fact:reschedule_fee_late_step1}}, Step 2 CK {{fact:reschedule_fee_late_step2ck}}, Step 3 {{fact:reschedule_fee_late_step3}} |

The same schedule applies if you cancel your appointment or change your test center, not only the date. Cancelling the appointment does not refund your application fee, because the Bulletin says fees are non-refundable and non-transferable.

Practice (opinion): a late change is expensive, but a failed attempt is costlier in money, calendar time and your record. If you will not be ready, move the date as early as you can. See [attempt limits and retakes](/wiki/exams/usmle-attempt-limits-and-retakes) and the [NBME self-assessments](/wiki/exams/nbme-self-assessments) page for how to judge readiness.

## Can you change the time of your Step 2 exam?

Yes, until the day before. USMLE's FAQ says changes can be made up to the day before the exam, not on the exam date. The rescheduling page lists fees for rescheduling, cancelling and changing test center. A change to your date or start time is a change to your appointment, so expect the notice tiers above to apply, and check the fee Prometric displays before you confirm. We did not find USMLE guidance on moving only the start time within the same day.

If something goes wrong on the exam date itself, you cannot use the online change. See "If you miss your exam" below and the [exam day checklist](/wiki/exams/exam-day-rules-and-checklist).

## How many times can you reschedule?

The Bulletin, the rescheduling page and the FAQ that we reviewed do not state a limit on the number of reschedules. That is not the same as "unlimited". Check the current Bulletin and your permit before you plan on moving the date several times. The limits that clearly exist are practical: the date must fall inside your eligibility period, seats are first-come, first-served, and each late change costs money.

## The extension option

Rule (usmle.org Common Questions): you may request a one-time, contiguous eligibility period extension. The request must be made after your permit is issued, and it must be received no later than {{fact:usmle_extension_request_days}} after the end of your original eligibility period. A fee applies. We could not read FSMB's own pages, so we cannot give the extension fee for IMGs, and fee pages change.

If you miss both the original period and the extension, USMLE says a new application and fee are required.

Practice (opinion): treat the extension as insurance, and set your own deadline well before the end of your window. Do not rely on an extension to rescue a plan that has no slack, because the original fee is not refundable.

## If you miss your exam

Rule (usmle.org Common Questions): if you miss your exam and time remains in your eligibility period, contact Prometric to pay the late rescheduling fee. If no eligibility time remains, contact your registering organization. For IMGs that is FSMB.

Rule (usmle.org Common Questions): report test-day problems, including not completing your exam for any reason, within 10 days of your test date. See [scheduling with Prometric](/wiki/exams/scheduling-with-prometric). Also read the [attempt limits](/wiki/exams/usmle-attempt-limits-and-retakes) page, because an incomplete attempt can count, and ask your registering organization how a particular situation would be recorded before exam day, not after.

## If your test center closes

USMLE's rescheduling page describes a procedure for a center closed by inclement weather or other issues when you cannot test. It says a free extension of your eligibility period will be provided, if needed, so that you have time to reschedule. Prometric verifies the closure. The page says whom to contact, and that depends on your registering organization (FSMB for IMGs) and, for Step 3, on whether you had already started. Read the current page for the exact steps, and keep any messages from the center.

## What is different for IMGs

- **Time zone.** The fee tier is set by local time at the Regional Registration Center for your Prometric region, so count from that time zone, not your own.
- **Travel and visas.** If you travel to test, book travel only after your Prometric appointment is confirmed, and prefer refundable options. A visitor visa delay is the classic reason to reschedule early. See [B-1/B-2 for interviews and exams](/wiki/visas/b1-b2-for-interviews-and-exams).
- **Seat scarcity.** Seats outside the US can be scarce in popular months. Rescheduling is not guaranteed to find a seat before your window ends, which is why the extension matters.
- **Step 3.** Step 3 is given only in the US and its territories, so any change affects travel and visa planning. On the current schedule the late-notice fee is higher for Step 3 than for the other two Steps.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing the shortest eligibility period you think you need.
- Waiting until the final days to decide you are not ready.
- Counting days in your own time zone instead of the Prometric region's.
- Booking non-refundable travel before the appointment is confirmed.
- Assuming a number of free reschedules that USMLE does not publish.
- Forgetting the extension request deadline after your window ends.

> [!NOTE] USMLE has announced plans to reduce the number of Step administrations each year beginning in January 2029 (designated testing dates). It says rescheduling will continue, but that eligibility period length and scheduling arrangements may change. See the [USMLE designated testing dates page](https://www.usmle.org/designated-testing-dates) if you plan far ahead. Fees and rules change, so this page reflects official sources as reviewed in November 2026, and the current Bulletin and USMLE site are the final authority.
