In the {{fact:nrmp_match_year}} Main Residency Match, the National Resident Matching Program (NRMP) reported a PGY-1 match rate of {{fact:us_img_match_rate}} percent for U.S. citizen IMGs and {{fact:nonus_img_match_rate}} percent for non-U.S. citizen IMGs. These are averages over thousands of applicants and they are the right starting point, not a prediction of your own result. This page shows how to read the numbers correctly and where they mislead. All figures are from NRMP's *Results and Data: {{fact:nrmp_match_year}} Main Residency Match* unless noted. For the specialty-level view see [IMG match rates by specialty](/wiki/specialties/img-match-rates-by-specialty).

## US-citizen IMGs versus non-US-citizen IMGs

NRMP separates two IMG groups, and the difference matters:

- **U.S. IMG:** a US citizen who enrolled in, or graduated from, a medical school outside the United States that is not accredited by the LCME or COCA.
- **Non-U.S. IMG:** a non-US citizen who enrolled in, or graduated from, such a school.

NRMP's definition is by citizenship, not by where someone grew up or trained. The two groups are very different in size and trend.

| Group | Active applicants (2026) | Matched to PGY-1 (2026) | Match rate (2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. IMGs | {{fact:us_img_ranked}} | {{fact:us_img_matched}} | {{fact:us_img_match_rate}}% |
| Non-U.S. IMGs | {{fact:nonus_img_ranked}} | {{fact:nonus_img_matched}} | {{fact:nonus_img_match_rate}}% |

In the 2026 report, NRMP states that the PGY-1 match rate for U.S. citizen IMGs was 2.2 percentage points higher than in 2025, while the rate for non-U.S. citizen IMGs was 1.6 percentage points lower.

NRMP reports figures but does not explain why the two rates differ. Visa sponsorship limits, which programs consider an applicant and differences in applicant profiles are often discussed, but the data above does not isolate any one cause.

## What "active applicant" and "match rate" mean

The match rate in NRMP's headline tables is the share of **active applicants** (those who submitted a certified rank order list) who matched to a PGY-1 position when the algorithm ran. It does not count people who registered but never ranked a program.

That matters for IMGs. NRMP reports that 20,166 U.S. citizen and non-U.S. citizen IMGs registered for the 2026 Match, while the active IMG groups above total 16,154. So a few thousand registered IMGs are outside the match-rate denominator. NRMP notes that many IMG applicants register but do not submit a rank order list. <!-- fact-ok -->

NRMP also reports a **placement rate**, which includes positions obtained through [SOAP](/wiki/interviews-match/soap-explained). In 2026:

| Measure | U.S. IMGs | Non-U.S. IMGs |
|---|---|---|
| PGY-1 match rate | {{fact:us_img_match_rate}}% | {{fact:nonus_img_match_rate}}% |
| Active-applicant placement rate | 77.8% | 59.9% |
| All-applicant placement rate | 68.7% | 52.2% |

Three numbers, three different stories. None is wrong. They answer different questions: "of those who ranked, who matched in the algorithm," "of those who ranked, who found any position," and "of everyone who registered, who found any position."

## The trend: more competition among non-US-citizen IMGs

NRMP reports that between 2022 and 2026 the number of active non-U.S. citizen IMGs rose from 7,864 to 11,944 (a 51.9 percent increase), while active U.S. citizen IMGs fell from 5,048 to 4,210 (a 16.6 percent decrease). NRMP also reports 2.55 PGY-1 positions per active IMG applicant in 2026, compared with 1.96 per active U.S. MD senior. A ratio of positions to applicants is a crude measure: not every position is available to every applicant. <!-- fact-ok -->

> [!KEY] Trend data like this tells you that competition is not static. It does not tell you what will happen next year. Re-read the newest NRMP report each year.

## Survivorship pitfalls

Statistics built from people who "made it" can mislead you. Watch for these.

- **Match stories are filtered.** People who matched are more likely to post and mentor; people who did not match often go quiet. Forum impressions therefore skew optimistic, and some online pessimism skews the other way. Neither is a sample.
- **Denominator games.** "90 percent of applicants with a Step 2 CK above X matched" can be technically true while hiding that many similar applicants never ranked or never received interviews. Ask: out of whom?
- **Averages hide subgroups.** A {{fact:nonus_img_match_rate}} percent average mixes recent graduates and long-ago graduates, people with US experience and people without, applicants needing visas and those who do not.
- **Pooled data across specialties.** A single match rate combines easy-to-enter and very competitive specialties.
- **Self-reported datasets.** Some NRMP *Charting Outcomes* tables depend on applicants who consented to share their data, as the 2026 non-U.S. IMG report notes, so they describe that group, not all applicants.

## How to use the statistics sensibly

1. **Check the year and the definition** in every sentence you read. If a source does not give them, discount it.
2. **Compare yourself to the right group.** If you are a non-U.S. citizen IMG, the {{fact:nonus_img_match_rate}} percent figure applies to your category, not the {{fact:us_img_match_rate}} percent one.
3. **Think in terms of your own levers:** how many programs you apply to, how well your application fits what they screen for, and which specialty you target. See [how many programs to apply to](/wiki/application/how-many-programs-to-apply-to).
4. **Plan for the failure branch.** A rate well below 100 percent means you need a plan B in advance, not after Match Day. See [if you do not match](/wiki/interviews-match/if-you-do-not-match).
5. **Do not read the numbers as a verdict.** They describe past cohorts of mixed applicants; they are not a forecast of one person.

> [!IMG TIP] If someone quotes you a match rate, ask three things: which year, which group (ranked or all registered, US citizen or not), and whether it includes SOAP. If they cannot answer, treat the number as unverified.

## Where to find the primary data

NRMP publishes the main *Results and Data* report each spring and *Charting Outcomes* reports each summer. Download the latest versions from [NRMP's data pages](https://www.nrmp.org/match-data-analytics/residency-data-reports/) rather than relying on summaries on third-party sites.
