The most costly IMG mistakes are usually not about intelligence or effort. They are about **sequence** (doing things in the wrong order), **verification** (acting on a rule that has changed) and **wishful planning** (no plan B). The list below groups them by theme, with what to do instead. Where something is an official rule we say so; where it is common practice or our opinion we label it.

## Exams

**Registering or booking before checking eligibility and your window.** Official rule: you test within the eligibility period shown on your scheduling permit, and seats at some centers fill quickly. Read the [scheduling guide](/wiki/exams/scheduling-with-prometric) before you plan a date.

**Following outdated registration advice.** Since January 2026 IMGs apply for USMLE Steps through FSMB, while ECFMG still decides eligibility for ECFMG certification. Older videos and posts describe the previous process. Follow [usmle.org](https://www.usmle.org/usmle-service-transition-launching-january-2026).

**Taking an exam before you are consistently ready.** Practice: use self-assessments and your error log, not the calendar, to decide (see [how long to study](/wiki/exams/how-long-to-study)). The Bulletin says you should not feel compelled to test on a day when you are not ready, and attempts count toward the limit of {{fact:usmle_attempts_per_step}} per Step.

**Using too many resources.** Opinion: finishing one question bank and reviewing it deeply tends to beat starting four and finishing none. See [question banks compared](/wiki/exams/question-banks-compared).

**Skipping full-length practice.** A full USMLE exam day is a long sitting. Practice the stamina before the real day.

## Application

**Applying with an application that does not match what programs screen for.** Programs commonly filter on factors such as exam results, year of graduation and US clinical experience. See [program screening filters](/wiki/application/program-screening-filters).

**Applying to too few programs, or to the wrong ones.** A short list leaves little room for the interview outcomes you cannot control (see [IMG match rates by specialty](/wiki/specialties/img-match-rates-by-specialty) for how NRMP reports this). Build a list with a realistic range ([how many programs to apply to](/wiki/application/how-many-programs-to-apply-to)).

**Writing a generic personal statement or experiences section.** Programs read hundreds. Specific, honest, clearly written content outperforms generic claims. See the [personal statement guide](/wiki/application/personal-statement-guide).

**Weak letters of recommendation.** A letter from someone who has directly supervised you usually says more than a famous name who barely knows you. See [letters of recommendation](/wiki/application/letters-of-recommendation).

**Missing dates.** ERAS and the Match have fixed timelines that change each year. Confirm them in the [ERAS timeline](/wiki/application/eras-timeline) and on AAMC's current IMG timeline.

## Strategy and mindset

**Treating averages as a promise.** In the 2026 Main Residency Match, NRMP reported PGY-1 match rates of 70.0 percent for U.S. citizen IMGs and 56.4 percent for non-U.S. citizen IMGs among applicants who ranked programs. Those averages hide huge variation (see [IMG match statistics](/wiki/start-here/img-match-statistics)).

**No plan B.** Decide before the season what you will do if you do not match: SOAP, a reapplication, research, or a different route. See [if you do not match](/wiki/interviews-match/if-you-do-not-match) and [reapplicant strategy](/wiki/application/reapplicant-strategy).

**Choosing a specialty only by "easy to match."** A specialty you cannot tolerate for years is a poor trade. Check fit as well as odds ([choosing a specialty](/wiki/specialties/choosing-a-specialty)).

**Waiting for perfection.** Delay has costs: the longer since graduation, the more programs may screen you out. See [am I too old or too late](/wiki/start-here/am-i-too-old-or-too-late).

## Money and visas

**Underestimating the total cost.** Exams are only part of it: add clinical experience, applications, interview travel and relocation ([budget guide](/wiki/start-here/total-cost-of-matching)).

**Paying for services you cannot evaluate.** Opinion: before buying any paid resource, ask for specifics on what you get, what is guaranteed (no one can guarantee a match), and refund terms. Be wary of anyone promising a match or "inside" access.

**Ignoring visas until Match Day.** Many programs limit which visas they sponsor, and visa timelines are long. Read [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b) and the [visa timeline after the Match](/wiki/visas/visa-timeline-after-the-match) before you finalize your program list.

## A quick self-check

Before you take any big step (book an exam, pay a fee, submit an application), ask:

1. Which official page tells me this rule, and when was it last updated?
2. What is the cost of being wrong, in money and in time?
3. What is my fallback if this does not work?
4. Am I doing this because of evidence or because someone online told me to?

> [!IMG TIP] Keep a one-page log of decisions with the source you used (URL and date). When a rule changes, you can see immediately which of your plans it affects.
