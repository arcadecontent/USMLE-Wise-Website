A "research fellowship" for an IMG can mean a salaried postdoctoral post, a stipend-funded research year, an unpaid volunteer role with a fancy title, or a program you pay to join. The label tells you very little, so judge each offer by who pays you, who supervises you, what you will produce and which immigration status the host will use. This page explains the main types, the honest pay picture, how visas fit in at a high level, and the warning signs.

For whether research is worth your time at all, read [why research matters for IMGs](/wiki/research/why-research-matters-for-imgs). For finding a project in the first place, see [finding a research position](/wiki/research/finding-a-research-position).

## The main types

| Type | Typical pay | Who it suits | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salaried research assistant, coordinator or postdoctoral post | Paid by the institution or a grant | IMGs with relevant research skills or a PhD | Competitive; may require work authorization before you apply |
| Funded research fellowship (department, institute or grant) | Stipend or salary | IMGs with a named mentor and a clear project | Short terms; renewal depends on funding |
| Research scholar on a J-1 | Depends on the sponsor's funding | IMGs at an academic center that can sponsor | Sponsor rules, clinical-activity limits, visa bars |
| Unpaid volunteer or visiting researcher | None | IMGs who can support themselves, or work remotely | Terms vary widely; get duties and authorship expectations in writing |
| Remote or part-time project role | Usually none or modest | IMGs who cannot relocate | Check that there is a real mentor and real data access |
| Fee-based "research program" | You pay | Sometimes used for structure and mentorship | See the warning signs below |

> [!NOTE] This table describes common patterns, not official categories. Institutions use titles inconsistently, so read the offer letter rather than the job title.

## Paid versus unpaid, honestly

Paid research positions exist, but they are usually competitive and often prefer applicants who already have work authorization, a research degree or prior output. Many IMG research roles are unpaid or minimally paid, particularly volunteer and remote roles. That is common practice, not an official standard.

If a role is unpaid, ask yourself:

- Can you afford the time, and the cost of living if you relocate?
- Is the unpaid arrangement legal in your situation? Whether an unpaid role counts as employment depends on the host, your status and local rules, so ask the host's international office or human resources.
- Will the mentor commit to a defined project, regular meetings and a written authorship plan?

Opinion, labeled as such: an unpaid year with a strong mentor and a finished paper is usually a better use of time than a paid year with no supervision, but that only holds if you can sustain it financially.

## Visas, at a high level

> [!CHANGED] Immigration policy changed significantly during 2025 and 2026 and continues to evolve. This section is general orientation only, current as of November 2026. Confirm every detail with the host institution's international office, the sponsor and current guidance on the [US Department of State exchange visitor site](https://j1visa.state.gov/), and consider a qualified immigration attorney.

The status you hold determines whether you may be paid, and what you may do.

- **Visitor visas (B-1/B-2)** are for short visits. They generally do not allow employment. See [B-1/B-2 for interviews and exams](/wiki/visas/b1-b2-for-interviews-and-exams). Taking a research job on a visitor visa creates serious risk, so do not assume it is fine because a mentor says so.
- **J-1 research scholar** is an exchange visitor category for people doing research at a US institution. The State Department says the participant cannot be a candidate for a tenure-track position, cannot have completed a research scholar program in the prior 24 months, and generally cannot have been in a J program in the prior 12 months, with some exceptions. The sponsor is the host institution, which issues the DS-2019.
- **J-1 for clinical training** is a different category, sponsored by ECFMG, and is the route for residency (see [the J-1 visa process](/wiki/visas/j1-visa-process)). A research scholar J-1 is not a clinical training visa, and sponsors differ on what clinical contact they allow. Ask in writing what you may and may not do.
- **Other work visas**, such as the H-1B or O-1, are employer-driven and are covered elsewhere in the visas section.

Two cautions:

1. A J-1 of any kind can carry the two-year home-country requirement, depending on the annotation on your form and your country. See [the two-year home residency requirement](/wiki/visas/two-year-home-residency-rule).
2. Time on a research J-1 can interact with later J-1 eligibility through the 12 and 24 month bars. Ask the sponsor how a research year would affect a later residency J-1 before you accept.

## What a good offer looks like

- A named principal investigator who is accountable for your supervision.
- A defined project with a realistic timeline.
- Written terms: start date, hours, pay or stipend, status and visa, and who sponsors it.
- A written authorship understanding based on the [ICMJE criteria](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html). See [authorship and author order](/wiki/research/authorship-and-author-order).
- A track record: ask previous research fellows what they produced and where they went next.

## Warning signs

- **Authorship or publication is promised in exchange for money.** Legitimate authorship is earned by contribution. Buying it is misconduct (see [authorship and author order](/wiki/research/authorship-and-author-order)).
- **Large fees with vague deliverables.** A fee-based program is not automatically dishonest, but compare what you pay with what is written down.
- **Guaranteed match, interviews or letters.** Nobody can guarantee these.
- **No named supervisor** or a supervisor you cannot reach before paying.
- **Visa pressure.** Being told to enter on a visitor visa to work, or that the immigration side "will be handled later."
- **Publication in unknown journals as the main product.** See [predatory journals](/wiki/research/predatory-journals).
- **No written agreement** or reluctance to answer questions about the host institution.

For related scams in clinical placements, see [USCE red flags and scams](/wiki/clinical-experience/usce-red-flags-and-scams).

## When a research year helps and when it does not

NRMP reports applicant research counts as self-reported and unverified, and the data do not show a simple link between more research and matching. A research year can be worth it when it gives you a mentor, a letter, concrete output and a credible story. It can be a poor use of time when it replaces exam preparation you needed, costs money without producing output, or leaves a gap you cannot explain. How programs read it is covered in [how programs actually view research](/wiki/research/how-programs-view-research).

> [!IMG TIP] If you pursue a research year, schedule your exams and application timeline first, then fit the research around them. Research rarely compensates for an unresolved exam problem.

Mentored research programs, including some structured for IMGs, exist as an option; whatever you choose, apply the same checklist above before paying or committing.
