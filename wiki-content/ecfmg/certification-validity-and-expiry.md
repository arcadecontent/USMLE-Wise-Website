For ECFMG certification, you must satisfy the exam requirements within a seven-year window that starts with your first passed exam. If you miss it, ECFMG states that your earliest passing result stops counting. This page explains the rule in general terms, but we could not open every ECFMG page directly, so confirm the exact wording and exceptions in the current ECFMG Information Booklet.

## Two different seven-year ideas

People often mix up two rules that share a number.

| Rule | Who sets it | What it covers | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time limit for certification exams | ECFMG | Passing Step 1 and Step 2 CK, and completing the clinical skills Pathway, within seven years | ECFMG requirement for certification |
| Time limit for Steps 1, 2 and 3 | State medical boards | Licensure | USMLE says most licensing authorities require completion within seven years, starting when you pass your first Step, but states differ |

The USMLE site directs readers to FSMB for each board's rules. The licensing side is covered in [Step 3 and licensure](/wiki/licensure-career/step-3-and-licensure).

## How the ECFMG window works

Based on the 2026 Information Booklet as summarized from official pages:

- The seven-year period begins on the date of the first exam passed and ends exactly seven years later.
- If you do not meet all exam requirements within a maximum of seven years, your earliest passing performance is no longer valid for ECFMG certification.
- The medical science requirement is Step 1 and Step 2 CK.
- Accepted Pathways for the clinical and communication skills requirement are subject to the same seven-year requirement.
- The former Step 2 CS and older ECFMG Clinical Skills Assessment are not subject to the limit.
- ECFMG states it will not notify you of upcoming deadlines.

> [!WARNING] The window is measured from the date of your first passed exam, not the date you registered, applied, or were certified. Write down the exact date and a date six months before it, and put both in a calendar.

## A simple example

Suppose you pass Step 2 CK first, in a given month. Your seven-year window runs from that exam date. You still need Step 1 and the required Pathway completed before the window closes. If you let Step 1 slide past that date, the Step 2 CK pass would no longer count toward certification, and you would be rebuilding from there. ECFMG's booklet gives its own worked example, so check it for how the clock works in your sequence.

## What it means for ERAS and the Match

Your exam dates matter in three places:

1. **ERAS application.** You apply through [[eras|ERAS]] with an ERAS token from ECFMG. Programs see your exam results and dates, and old results or long gaps may prompt questions. See [years since graduation](/wiki/application/years-since-graduation).
2. **The Match.** NRMP expects IMGs to hold ECFMG certification by its certification deadline for the Match year, and ECFMG shares status with NRMP during the season. The exact date changes each year, so check the current NRMP page.
3. **Starting residency.** NRMP states IMGs must have completed all ECFMG certification requirements to begin training, so a window that closes between application and start date can threaten your position.

No source guarantees that an application with older exams is treated a particular way. Program expectations vary, and that part is opinion and experience rather than rule.

## Planning around the window

1. **Map the whole sequence.** Place Step 1, Step 2 CK, the Pathway and OET (if used) against your target ERAS and Match years. See [ECFMG certification timeline](/wiki/ecfmg/ecfmg-certification-timeline) and [when to take each Step](/wiki/exams/when-to-take-each-step).
2. **Count from your first pass.** If you passed an exam years ago, confirm the date on your transcript now.
3. **Leave buffer for failures.** Attempts are limited to {{fact:usmle_attempts_per_step}} per Step, and waiting periods apply. See [attempt limits and retakes](/wiki/exams/usmle-attempt-limits-and-retakes).
4. **Allow for administrative delays.** Credential verification and registration issues can take months. See [common ECFMG delays](/wiki/ecfmg/common-ecfmg-delays).
5. **Do not rely on exceptions.** Exceptions, if any, are decided by the relevant organization, so ask early and in writing.

## If your window is closing

- Check your transcript and your ECFMG record for exact dates.
- Ask ECFMG, through its official channels, what options apply to your situation.
- Consider which requirement is farthest from done and prioritize it.
- Remember that a passed Step may be retaken only to meet a licensing authority's limit and only after it has expired, according to USMLE.

> [!CHANGED] Since January 2026, IMGs register for USMLE through FSMB, while ECFMG decides certification eligibility, and exam formats changed in May 2026. The seven-year rule described here comes from the 2026 ECFMG booklet. Check the current edition for any update, and see the [policy tracker](/wiki/exams/usmle-policy-changes-tracker).

## Does the certificate itself expire?

ECFMG notes that certificates earned through some Pathways can be subject to expiration unless conditions are met for them to be valid indefinitely, as described in the [certification overview](/wiki/ecfmg/ecfmg-certification-overview). The rules on that point are detailed, so read the current Information Booklet and your certificate terms rather than relying on a summary.

## Bottom line

Treat the seven-year window as a hard planning constraint, track your first-pass date yourself, and verify your situation on ECFMG's pages before you make decisions that depend on it.
