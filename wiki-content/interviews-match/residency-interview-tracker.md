A residency interview tracker is one spreadsheet, with one row per program, that holds everything about your interview season in one place: invitations, dates, time zones, logistics, notes, thank-yous and your rank thoughts. You can download a free one below, open it in Excel or Google Sheets, and start using it in a few minutes.

It is built for the way IMGs experience interview season: many programs, different time zones, a mix of virtual and in-person formats, and a short stretch of time in which a lot of invitations arrive. For the rules and etiquette around invitations, see [managing interview invitations](/wiki/interviews-match/managing-interview-invitations).

## Why track your interviews at all

Interview season is busy, and the details are easy to lose.

- **Invitations arrive in bursts,** often through different platforms, and many slots fill quickly. A single list shows what you have answered and what you have not.
- **Time zones cause missed interviews.** Programs generally set times in their own time zone. Writing both times next to each interview protects you, as the [virtual interview setup](/wiki/interviews-match/virtual-interview-setup) page notes.
- **Programs start to look alike.** AAMC advises capturing impressions right after each interview, "taking notes or writing a summary of things that stood out to you the most (both good and bad)." AAFP warns that it is easy to mix up impressions by the time your rank order list is due.
- **Your notes feed your list.** The notes you write the same day are the raw material for your [rank order list](/wiki/interviews-match/rank-order-list-strategy).
- **IMGs often receive fewer invitations per application.** When each one is scarce, you cannot afford to lose track of any of them.

## The fields, explained

Here is every column in the tracker and what to put in it.

### Basics

| Column | What to enter | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Program** | The full program name | Similar names are easy to confuse |
| **City/State** | Location | Helps with travel, time zones and your own preferences |
| **Specialty** | The specialty and track | Useful if you apply to more than one. See [applying to multiple specialties](/wiki/application/applying-to-multiple-specialties) |
| **Status** | Invited, Accepted, Completed, Declined, Cancelled | A quick filter shows what still needs action |

### Invitation and scheduling

| Column | What to enter | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Invitation received** | The date the invitation arrived | Shows how quickly you responded |
| **Response sent** | The date you accepted or declined | Records that you answered |
| **Interview date** | The date, written year-month-day | Sorts correctly in any spreadsheet |
| **Start time (program time zone)** | The time in the program's zone, with the zone named | The authoritative time |
| **Start time (my time zone)** | The same time converted to yours | What you will see on your clock |

### Format and logistics

| Column | What to enter | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Format** | Virtual, In person or Hybrid | Drives your preparation |
| **Interviewers** | Names and roles, if known | For preparation and thank-you notes |
| **Coordinator contact** | Name, email and phone | Who to reach if something goes wrong |
| **Logistics notes** | Platform and link, address, travel, lodging, dress code, social events, visa notes | Everything you need on the day |

### Interview content and impressions

| Column | What to enter | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Questions asked** | The questions you remember | Shows patterns and what to prepare. See [common interview questions](/wiki/interviews-match/common-interview-questions) |
| **What I liked** | Specific positives | Reasons, not feelings |
| **Concerns** | Specific worries or open questions | Things to clarify, not conclusions |

### After the interview

| Column | What to enter | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Thank-you sent** | Yes, No, Not sending, or Specialty discourages | Prevents duplicates and gaps. See [post-interview communication](/wiki/interviews-match/post-interview-communication) |
| **Rank thoughts** | Your honest impression and how it compares | Your future self will need it |
| **Next step** | The next action, with a date if there is one | Keeps the row alive until it is done |

## How to use the tracker

1. **Add a row the moment an invitation arrives.** Fill in the program, specialty, the invitation date and the status "Invited."
2. **Reply, then record it.** When you accept or decline, enter the date and change the status.
3. **Record both times.** Write the start time in the program's zone, then convert it to yours. Check daylight saving changes, which happen in different weeks in different countries.
4. **Add the interview to your calendar,** with a reminder the day before and an hour before. The tracker does not send reminders.
5. **Fill in logistics once, then update.** For a virtual interview, add the platform and link. For an in-person one, add the address, travel and lodging.
6. **Write notes the same day.** Complete "Questions asked," "What I liked" and "Concerns" before the next interview, while you still remember the details.
7. **Record your thank-you decision** in the Thank-you column, whether it is "Yes," "No" or "Not sending."
8. **Write a first impression in Rank thoughts,** then return to it before you build your list.
9. **Set a next step** for every row that is not finished, and clear it when done.

## How to write notes you can use later

The quality of your notes decides whether the tracker is useful in January.

- **Write the same day.** Memory fades quickly, and programs blur together.
- **Separate facts from feelings.** "Residents rotate through a continuity clinic every month" is a fact. "I felt comfortable there" is a feeling. Record both, and label them.
- **Use the same prompts for every program.** The three columns, Questions asked, What I liked and Concerns, make comparison easier.
- **Record the answers to your own questions.** See [questions to ask programs](/wiki/interviews-match/questions-to-ask-programs) for ideas, and note what you were told and who told you.
- **Do not record the interview itself** unless the program says you may. Many programs prohibit recording or sharing interview content.
- **Write only what you would be comfortable seeing again.** Be fair about people. The tracker is for you, but notes about individuals can still be harsh when read cold.

## Tracking thank-you notes

Thank-you notes are customary at many programs but are not required, and some specialties discourage them. AAFP advises checking with a program representative about sending one. If you do send one, [post-interview communication](/wiki/interviews-match/post-interview-communication) explains what NRMP's code of conduct allows and what a short, specific note looks like. In the tracker, record the interviewer, the date you sent it, or a clear "Not sending" so you can see at a glance what is done.

## Using the tracker to think about your rank list

Opinion: build your list from your own honest preferences, using your notes as evidence. A sensible routine is:

1. After each interview, write a one-line impression in Rank thoughts, such as "high," "middle" or "low," with the reason.
2. Before certification, read the notes for every program in one sitting, and sort the sheet by your impressions.
3. Compare programs on the factors that matter to you: training, location, support, visa, family and fit.
4. Be careful with what programs say about ranking you. A program's statement is not binding and should not change your list. See [how the Match algorithm works](/wiki/interviews-match/how-the-match-algorithm-works) and [rank order list strategy](/wiki/interviews-match/rank-order-list-strategy).

The tracker does not make the decision for you. It makes sure you are choosing from complete information.

## Keep it private and safe

- **Do not share it.** NRMP's rules prevent programs from asking where else you interviewed or how you will rank them. Your tracker is for you, and you may share it with an advisor if you choose. See [interview season overview](/wiki/interviews-match/interview-season-overview).
- **Do not store passwords, passport numbers or other sensitive identifiers** in it. Keep those in a secure place.
- **Back it up** by saving a copy to a second location, and give each saved version a date in its name.
- **Be careful with cloud copies.** If you upload the file to an online spreadsheet, check who can view it, and do not share the link.

## Customizing the tracker

- **Add filters** so you can show only Invited rows, or only interviews in the next week.
- **Color-code the Status column** (for example, one color for "Invited" and another for "Completed") so the whole sheet is readable at a glance.
- **Freeze the top row and the Program column** so they stay visible while you scroll.
- **Sort by interview date** to see the order in which you will meet programs.
- **Add columns** if you need them, such as application fees paid, visa sponsorship answers or housing costs.

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting until the season is busy** to start the sheet.
- **Recording only the program time,** not your own.
- **Writing notes days later,** when two programs have merged in your memory.
- **Recording feelings without reasons,** which cannot be compared in January.
- **Letting what a program said about ranking you** override your own list.
- **Sharing the sheet,** or leaving it open on a shared device.

If invitations are slower than you hoped, the tracker still helps by showing which programs have responded and which have not.
