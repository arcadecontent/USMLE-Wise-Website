A second look is an optional visit to a program after your interview, before you certify your rank order list, and it usually does not change how the program ranks you. For IMGs it also costs real money and visa risk, so treat it as an information-gathering tool, not a way to move up a rank list.

## What a second look is

AAMC describes a second look as a visit that helps you learn more than interview day can show: shadowing residents, seeing the hospital and neighborhood, checking housing and commutes, and meeting the program director. Many programs also hold virtual or in-person "visit days" for applicants who have already interviewed. NRMP's 2026 Program Director Survey asked programs whether they offer a post-interview in-person visit, but the narrative report I read does not publish the answer, so I cannot say how common visits are this cycle. Ask each program ([questions to ask programs](/wiki/interviews-match/questions-to-ask-programs)).

## What the NRMP rules allow

The NRMP's Match Participation Agreement (2027 edition) says, before the Rank Order List Certification Deadline:

- Applicants may voluntarily communicate their interest to programs, but may not solicit verbal or written statements implying a commitment to rank them.
- A program may voluntarily tell an applicant they are viewed favorably and will be ranked, but may not solicit a statement implying a commitment from the applicant.
- Programs may not suggest that placement on a rank list depends on the applicant sending a statement of preference, and may not ask applicants to reveal their rank preferences or the programs they have applied to or interviewed with.

So a visit is allowed. Asking "where will you rank me?" or implying that you will rank them first in exchange for a promise is not. Read the current agreement for exact language, and see [post-interview communication](/wiki/interviews-match/post-interview-communication) for letters and thank-you notes.

## Does a visit influence ranking?

Rule: nothing in NRMP's agreement forbids programs from noting your visit, but they may not make a preference statement a condition of ranking. Practice: AAMC says that in most cases attending a second look will not change how a program ranks you, that a strong showing may strengthen their interest, and that a weak showing may cause doubts. NRMP's 2026 Program Director Survey lists "Second interview/visit" among factors programs could select for ranking, along with interactions with faculty and house staff during the interview and visit, but I could not extract how many programs selected it. Programs vary, and some specialties discourage post-interview contact, so check the program's own statement.

> [!NOTE] Opinion: do not treat a visit as part of the application. Go if the information would change your rank order, and skip it if you can already rank confidently.

## Costs and trade-offs

AAMC says visits often require travel, lodging and time away from rotations, and that the value of a visit is hard to estimate. AAMC's cost page, which is general and dated, suggests interview costs can be large, so do not assume you will recoup a flight. For IMGs the costs include:

- **Travel and lodging**, usually paid out of pocket.
- **Visa risk.** A visit may need a visitor visa, and short-notice appointments may not be available ([B-1/B-2 for interviews and exams](/wiki/visas/b1-b2-for-interviews-and-exams)). Policy and processing can change quickly, so confirm current rules with the State Department.
- **Opportunity cost.** Time away from a US rotation or job.

A virtual conversation with residents or the program director may answer most questions at far lower cost. AAMC says that if a visit is not necessary, a virtual conversation or the program's website and social media may be enough.

## When a second look may be worth it

- You are choosing between two or three similar programs and cannot separate them.
- You need to see the city, housing and call rooms before committing, for example with a partner or children ([couples match](/wiki/interviews-match/couples-match)).
- You are already in the US for a rotation or other reason, so the marginal cost is small.
- The program formally invites you and you can attend at reasonable cost.

## Etiquette

AAMC's guidance, plus common practice:

1. **Schedule in advance** and never show up unannounced.
2. **Prepare specific questions** about training, schedules, support and fellowship or job outcomes.
3. **Be professional with everyone**, including coordinators and residents.
4. **Decline gracefully** if invited and unable to attend. A short, polite reply is enough, and you can ask for a virtual call instead.
5. **Keep follow-up brief.** A thank-you note and only questions you need to rank.
6. **Do not ask about your rank** or announce a ranking commitment you cannot keep. A match is binding ([how the Match algorithm works](/wiki/interviews-match/how-the-match-algorithm-works)).

> [!WARNING] If a program asks where else you have interviewed or how you will rank them, or says that placement on its list depends on a statement of your preference, NRMP's agreement says programs are not authorized to do that. NRMP asks applicants to report violations. A demand that you attend a visit to be ranked is not addressed in the text I read, so ask the program in writing and, if unsure, contact NRMP.
