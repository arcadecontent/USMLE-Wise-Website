In the Couples Match, two applicants link their rank order lists so that they match to a pair of programs, and a match happens only if both partners match at a pair they ranked. NRMP's 2026 results show that most couples do well (1,113 of 1,258 couples both matched), but the process adds rules and risks, especially if one or both partners need a visa. This guide covers the rules, how to email programs to ask that your partner be considered, and how to talk about it in interviews.

## Couples Match at a glance

1. Both partners register individually in NRMP's R3 system for the same Match.
2. One partner sends a couple request and the other accepts; both fees must be paid.
3. You build paired rank lists of equal length and certify them before the deadline.
4. In the application season, decide whether to tell programs, and coordinate interviews.
5. A match happens only if both partners match at a pair of ranked programs.

## How couples are paired

Each partner registers individually in NRMP's R3 system. One partner then sends a couple request, and the other accepts. NRMP says applicants must be in the same Match to couple, that the fee is due when the request is sent and again from the accepting partner, and that coupling is complete when Couple Status reads "Accepted" and Fee Status reads "Paid". Fee amounts are on [NRMP's fee page](https://www.nrmp.org/residency-applicants/get-ready-for-the-match/couples-in-the-match/) and in [NRMP registration and fees](/wiki/interviews-match/nrmp-registration-and-fees), so I do not repeat them here. NRMP says couple fees are non-refundable, even if you later uncouple.

NRMP's Match Participation Agreement defines couples as any two individuals registered as a couple who agree to pair their lists to match to a ranked pair of programs, and it states that they can be sponsored or independent applicants. Partners may be in different specialties, different cities, and different types of applicant (for example, one U.S. graduate and one IMG).

## How the lists work

Your two rank order lists are combined into ordered pairs: "Program A for you with Program B for your partner" is one rank. The algorithm tries the couple's most preferred pair first, then moves down the list. NRMP's rules:

- **Same length.** Partners must have the same number of ranks on their respective lists.
- **Maximum.** Each partner can submit no more than 300 ranks.
- **No duplicate pairs.** The R3 system has a "Check for Duplicates" button on the ROL Compare tab.
- **"No Match" code.** If one partner ranks No Match, that partner will not match if the other matches at that pair. NRMP says such pairs should go at the bottom.
- **Supplemental lists (advanced programs).** If a partner ranks an advanced program, a list of preliminary programs can be attached. NRMP says supplemental lists are considered individually, not as a unit, and only after a match to the primary lists ([categorical vs preliminary](/wiki/specialties/categorical-vs-preliminary)).
- **Program-count limits.** NRMP's base fee covers a limited number of unique programs per partner, with extra charges per additional program. See NRMP's help page for the current limits, because I found two slightly different descriptions of how they split between primary and supplemental lists.

> [!KEY] If the couple does not match to a pair, NRMP's agreement says it will not uncouple the applicants and try their individual lists. Put pairs you could live with at every level, including combinations where only one partner is in the city you most want.

## What the 2026 data show

In the 2026 Match there were 1,258 couples. Of these, 1,113 (88.5 percent) both matched, 114 had one partner match and 31 had neither match, for a total couples Match Rate that NRMP reports as 93.0 percent (my calculation from these counts suggests this is the share of individual partners who matched). That is NRMP's overall picture, not a prediction for any pair, and it does not break couples down by IMG status.

## Should you tell programs you are in the Couples Match?

It is your choice. NRMP's December 2025 policy newsletter says applicants are not required under policy to disclose couple status, that any mention of it in your application is voluntary, and that it should not be required. The same newsletter says programs at the same institution may communicate with each other about couple applicants only if the status has been voluntarily disclosed or highlighted in application materials.

MyERAS also has a Match information selection in Personal Information where you indicate whether you intend to participate individually or as a couple. The AAMC guide does not say how programs use or see that answer, so do not rely on it as your only way of telling programs.

In our opinion (practice, not a rule), telling programs usually helps when you want them to coordinate interviews or consider both applications, because they cannot help with something they do not know. Keep the message short and professional, and do not make it the center of your application.

## How to email a program to ask that your partner be considered

Programs make their own decisions and no email can guarantee an interview. A good email makes one clear, polite request and gives the reader a reason to help. The two templates below cover the most common situations.

> [!NOTE] These templates are illustrative examples written by the USMLE Wise team. They are not anyone's real emails. Do not copy them word for word: replace every bracket with your own true details, remove anything that is not true for you, and change the wording so it sounds like you.

**Template 1: Asking the program where your partner applied to consider the application (illustrative)**

> **Subject:** Couples Match: [partner's name], [specialty] applicant
>
> Dear [Dr. surname or Program Coordinator],
>
> My partner, [partner's name] (AAMC ID [number]), has applied to your [specialty] program. I am applying in [my specialty] in [city or region], and we plan to participate in the Match as a couple.
>
> I am writing to ask that you consider [partner's name]'s application. [Partner's name] is particularly interested in your program because [one specific, true reason, such as a clinical focus, a rotation there or a mentor].
>
> We understand that every application is reviewed on its own merits, and we would be grateful for any consideration. Please let me know if there is anything else we can provide.
>
> Thank you for your time.
>
> Sincerely,  
> [your name]  
> [email and phone]

**Template 2: Asking a program where you have an interview to coordinate with your partner's program (illustrative)**

> **Subject:** Interview invitation: coordinating as a Couples Match applicant
>
> Dear [Dr. surname or Program Coordinator],
>
> Thank you for inviting me to interview on [date]. I am delighted and I look forward to meeting the team.
>
> I am participating in the Match as a couple. My partner, [partner's name], has applied to [program or department] at [institution or city] in [specialty]. If it is appropriate, would you be able to let that program know that we are a couple, or let me know whom I should contact? We would like to attend both interviews if possible.
>
> I understand if this is not something you can do, and I appreciate your help either way.
>
> Sincerely,  
> [your name]  
> [email and phone]

Tips for phrasing the email:

- **One request per email.** "Please consider my partner's application" or "Can you coordinate dates", not both plus a list of questions.
- **Be specific about interest.** One true reason is better than general praise.
- **Make no ranking promises and ask for none.** NRMP says programs cannot ask you to reveal your ranking preferences, so do not offer them. Saying you are "very interested" is fine; saying you will rank the program first is a commitment you should not make lightly.
- **Do not pressure.** Avoid wording that implies the program must take both of you.
- **Use your professional email and keep it short.** A reader skimming a long inbox should understand the ask in two sentences.
- **Follow up once** if you hear nothing after a reasonable wait, then let it go.

## How to talk about the Couples Match in interviews

Whether to bring it up is your choice. If a program knows, or you decide to say so, this approach works for most applicants (opinion and practice, not a rule):

1. **Say it plainly in one sentence.** "Yes, my partner and I are participating in the Match as a couple."
2. **Name your focus.** Mention the cities or regions you are both considering, so the program sees a practical plan.
3. **Return to the program.** Spend most of your answer on why you want to train there, not on logistics. Programs rank applicants who want their program, not only the location.
4. **Share only what you are comfortable sharing.** You decide how much to say about your partner's specialty or where they are applying.
5. **Do not promise a rank.** NRMP says programs cannot ask where else you are interviewing or how you will rank them. If asked, you can politely say you keep that confidential and return to your interest in the program. See [common interview questions](/wiki/interviews-match/common-interview-questions) and the [interview season overview](/wiki/interviews-match/interview-season-overview).

A framework (illustrative, not a script): *"Yes, we are applying as a couple, focused on [region]. I am especially interested in your program because [specific reason]. I am happy to answer any questions about how we are planning."*

If you are asked what happens if only one of you matches, answer practically: you have built your lists with that in mind, and you know the next steps ([SOAP explained](/wiki/interviews-match/soap-explained)).

## Certification and changes

Both partners must certify their lists before the Rank Order List Certification Deadline. NRMP's 2027 agreement says that if one partner fails to certify, that partner is notified and must certify within 24 hours of the deadline. If they do not, NRMP uncouples the pair, removes duplicate programs and "No Match" ranks, and processes the certified list alone. The partner with the certified list can ask in writing to be withdrawn within 24 hours of being told. A similar process applies if one partner is withdrawn or becomes ineligible after the deadline.

To switch to individual participation or withdraw before the deadline, both partners must first uncouple in R3, then each removes duplicates and recertifies. See the [rank order list strategy](/wiki/interviews-match/rank-order-list-strategy) page for general list-building.

## What IMGs and visa-requiring applicants should check

NRMP's agreement says non-U.S. citizen applicants requiring a visa are responsible for confirming the institution's willingness to sponsor the visa type they intend before certifying their rank order list. In a couple, that duty is double: both partners need pairs where each program can support their own status ([J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b)). NRMP's couples pages say nothing specific about visas, so these are common-practice points from the general rule:

1. **Check sponsorship for both partners** before you add a pair, and write down what each program told you.
2. **Do not pair a program that cannot sponsor you with a "dream" program for your partner.** If the pair matches, the match is binding on both of you.
3. **Think about a dependent's status.** One partner on a J-1 or H-1B may have a spouse who is not eligible to work. See [dependents on J-2 and H-4](/wiki/visas/dependents-j2-and-h4).
4. **Remember ECFMG certification timing** for any partner who is an IMG ([ECFMG certification timeline](/wiki/ecfmg/ecfmg-certification-timeline)).

## Practical tips

- Interview in clusters. Ask programs in the same city or region about interview dates so you can both attend. Disclosure is your choice, and NRMP says programs cannot ask you to reveal your ranking preferences.
- Build pairs in tiers: both in one city, both in the same state, then commutable pairs, then pairs where one partner is in a less preferred location.
- Sit down together after interviews and rank the pairs, not the individual programs.
- Know what happens in SOAP if only one of you matches. NRMP's materials say partially matched applicants can see the city, state and zip code of the partner's program in R3 ([SOAP explained](/wiki/interviews-match/soap-explained)).

> [!WARNING] A match is a binding commitment. Neither of you can decline a matched pair without NRMP's permission, so do not rank a pair you would not accept.
