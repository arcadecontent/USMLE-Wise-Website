In the Couples Match, two applicants link their rank order lists so that they match to a pair of programs, and a match happens only if both partners match at a pair they ranked. NRMP's 2026 results show that most couples do well (1,113 of 1,258 couples both matched), but the process adds rules and risks, especially if one or both partners need a visa.

## How couples are paired

Each partner registers individually in NRMP's R3 system. One partner then sends a couple request, and the other accepts. NRMP says applicants must be in the same Match to couple, that the fee is due when the request is sent and again from the accepting partner, and that coupling is complete when Couple Status reads "Accepted" and Fee Status reads "Paid". Fee amounts are on [NRMP's fee page](https://www.nrmp.org/residency-applicants/get-ready-for-the-match/couples-in-the-match/) and in [NRMP registration and fees](/wiki/interviews-match/nrmp-registration-and-fees), so I do not repeat them here. NRMP says couple fees are non-refundable, even if you later uncouple.

NRMP's Match Participation Agreement defines couples as any two individuals registered as a couple who agree to pair their lists to match to a ranked pair of programs, and it states that they can be sponsored or independent applicants. Partners may be in different specialties, different cities, and different types of applicant (for example, one U.S. graduate and one IMG).

## How the lists work

Your two rank order lists are combined into ordered pairs: "Program A for you with Program B for your partner" is one rank. The algorithm tries the couple's most preferred pair first, then moves down the list. NRMP's rules:

- **Same length.** Partners must have the same number of ranks on their respective lists.
- **Maximum.** Each partner can submit no more than 300 ranks.
- **No duplicate pairs.** The R3 system has a "Check for Duplicates" button on the ROL Compare tab.
- **"No Match" code.** If one partner ranks No Match, that partner will not match if the other matches at that pair. NRMP says such pairs should go at the bottom.
- **Supplemental lists (advanced programs).** If a partner ranks an advanced program, a list of preliminary programs can be attached. NRMP says supplemental lists are considered individually, not as a unit, and only after a match to the primary lists ([categorical vs preliminary](/wiki/specialties/categorical-vs-preliminary)).
- **Program-count limits.** NRMP's base fee covers a limited number of unique programs per partner, with extra charges per additional program. See NRMP's help page for the current limits, because I found two slightly different descriptions of how they split between primary and supplemental lists.

> [!KEY] If the couple does not match to a pair, NRMP's agreement says it will not uncouple the applicants and try their individual lists. Put pairs you could live with at every level, including combinations where only one partner is in the city you most want.

## What the 2026 data show

In the 2026 Match there were 1,258 couples. Of these, 1,113 (88.5 percent) both matched, 114 had one partner match and 31 had neither match, for a total couples Match Rate that NRMP reports as 93.0 percent (my calculation from these counts suggests this is the share of individual partners who matched). That is NRMP's overall picture, not a prediction for any pair, and it does not break couples down by IMG status.

## Certification and changes

Both partners must certify their lists before the Rank Order List Certification Deadline. NRMP's 2027 agreement says that if one partner fails to certify, that partner is notified and must certify within 24 hours of the deadline. If they do not, NRMP uncouples the pair, removes duplicate programs and "No Match" ranks, and processes the certified list alone. The partner with the certified list can ask in writing to be withdrawn within 24 hours of being told. A similar process applies if one partner is withdrawn or becomes ineligible after the deadline.

To switch to individual participation or withdraw before the deadline, both partners must first uncouple in R3, then each removes duplicates and recertifies. See the [rank order list strategy](/wiki/interviews-match/rank-order-list-strategy) page for general list-building.

## What IMGs and visa-requiring applicants should check

NRMP's agreement says non-U.S. citizen applicants requiring a visa are responsible for confirming the institution's willingness to sponsor the visa type they intend before certifying their rank order list. In a couple, that duty is double: both partners need pairs where each program can support their own status ([J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b)). NRMP's couples pages say nothing specific about visas, so these are common-practice points from the general rule:

1. **Check sponsorship for both partners** before you add a pair, and write down what each program told you.
2. **Do not pair a program that cannot sponsor you with a "dream" program for your partner.** If the pair matches, the match is binding on both of you.
3. **Think about a dependent's status.** One partner on a J-1 or H-1B may have a spouse who is not eligible to work. See [dependents on J-2 and H-4](/wiki/visas/dependents-j2-and-h4).
4. **Remember ECFMG certification timing** for any partner who is an IMG ([ECFMG certification timeline](/wiki/ecfmg/ecfmg-certification-timeline)).

## Practical tips

- Interview in clusters. Ask programs in the same city or region about interview dates so you can both attend, and tell programs that you are in the Match as a couple only if you choose to. Disclosure is your choice, and NRMP says programs cannot ask you to reveal your ranking preferences.
- Build pairs in tiers: both in one city, both in the same state, then commutable pairs, then pairs where one partner is in a less preferred location.
- Sit down together after interviews and rank the pairs, not the individual programs.
- Know what happens in SOAP if only one of you matches. NRMP's materials say partially matched applicants can see the city, state and zip code of the partner's program in R3 ([SOAP explained](/wiki/interviews-match/soap-explained)).

> [!WARNING] A match is a binding commitment. Neither of you can decline a matched pair without NRMP's permission, so do not rank a pair you would not accept.
