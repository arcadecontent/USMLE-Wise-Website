What to wear to a residency interview comes down to one idea: dress so that your answers, not your clothes, are what the interviewer remembers. For most applicants that means polished, comfortable, conservative clothing, a suit or equivalent in person and the same outfit, top to bottom, on video. This page covers in-person and virtual interviews, colors, ties, shoes, traditional and religious dress, weather and budget.

AAMC's interview guidance does not set a dress code, so most of what follows is custom and opinion. Each section says which is which. Nothing here is tied to a gender, and you can choose the pieces that suit you.

## How to dress for a residency interview: the short version

1. **Read the invitation.** If the program states a dress code, follow it. If it mentions a dinner or social, check whether it gives a separate dress note.
2. **Default to professional.** AAFP advises professional attire, a suit or equivalent, in neutral colors with minimal accessories.
3. **Prioritize fit and comfort.** A plain outfit that fits well and that you can sit, walk and speak in for hours beats an expensive one that does not.
4. **Be consistent across formats.** A virtual interview is an official interview. AAMC says to approach it as you would any formal interview.
5. **Ask if unsure.** AAFP calls the program coordinator "a great point of contact" for questions such as what attire to wear.

> [!NOTE] There is no single correct outfit. The aim is to look prepared and respectful without drawing attention. If your outfit is the first thing someone would mention about you, simplify it.

## Rule, practice and opinion

| Level | What it covers on this topic |
|---|---|
| **Rule** | A program's own instruction in its invitation or schedule. AAMC's interview guidance does not set an attire rule. |
| **Practice** | A suit or equivalent in neutral colors with minimal accessories, as AAFP describes. Neat, quiet, comfortable clothing. |
| **Opinion** | Specific colors, fabrics, ties, shoes, hair and packing advice from the USMLE Wise team. Reasonable people differ. |

If you meet a resident who says "nobody wears a suit here," treat it as information about that program's culture on that day. It is not a reason to under-dress at an interview, where the safer error is to look slightly more formal.

## What to wear to an in-person residency interview

| Item | Practical guidance (practice and opinion) |
|---|---|
| **Main outfit** | A suit, or an equivalent: a tailored jacket with matching or coordinating trousers or skirt, or a dress, tunic or formal traditional garment with a jacket if you wish. Choose a cut you can move in. |
| **Shirt or top** | Plain, pressed and well fitting. A solid light color is the lowest-risk choice. Avoid anything sheer, low cut or too tight. |
| **Neckwear** | Optional. If you wear a tie or scarf, keep it conservative and tidy. |
| **Shoes** | Polished, closed-toe, broken in. Interview days often include a tour with long walks, so comfort matters. |
| **Outerwear** | Bring a coat for the weather and leave it in the cloakroom or on a chair, not on your shoulders in the room. |
| **Accessories** | Minimal. Avoid jewelry that clinks or catches the eye. A simple watch is fine. |
| **Fragrance** | Opinion: none or very light. Hospitals and clinics are often scent-sensitive, and interview rooms can be small. |
| **Hair and grooming** | Neat, simple and out of your face when you speak. Keep nails clean and tidy. |
| **Bag** | A small, neat bag or folder rather than a large backpack. Keep your phone silenced and out of sight. |

AAMC also advises planning your route and checking transportation in advance for an in-person interview, so you arrive calm and not rushed. That is easier in an outfit you have already tried on and walked in.

## Colors and patterns: colors for a residency interview suit

Opinion, not a rule: dark neutrals such as navy, charcoal or black, or other muted solids, with a plain light top, are the lowest-risk combination. AAFP recommends neutral colors. A suit in a color you like, such as a deep green or a soft gray, is fine if it is muted and the rest of the outfit is plain.

- **Avoid loud patterns,** large logos and anything shiny or novelty.
- **Keep one point of personality at most:** a tie, scarf or simple piece of jewelry, not all three.
- **Check the color in daylight.** Some blacks and navies look different indoors and outdoors.
- **Match shoes and belt** or bag so the outfit looks finished.

## Ties, bolo ties, scarves and accessories: is a bolo tie professional?

A bolo tie is regional. In some parts of the United States it is ordinary professional wear, and in others it may look unexpected. Opinion: if you do not know the local culture, a conventional necktie or no neckwear is the lower-risk choice, because it asks interviewers to think about nothing except you. If a bolo tie, a brooch or another accessory is part of who you are and you are comfortable being asked about it, wearing it is your decision. A useful test is whether you would be happy for it to be the first thing someone says about you.

## Cultural, religious and traditional dress for IMGs

Many IMGs come from countries where professional and formal dress looks different from a Western suit. A saree, a shalwar kameez, a barong, a headscarf, a turban, a kippah and many other garments are formal dress for the people who wear them.

- **You do not have to look "American."** In our opinion, neat formal traditional or religious dress is professional dress, and you should not feel you need to hide who you are.
- **Keep the same standard of formality** that you would want from any interview outfit: clean, pressed, well fitting and quiet in color and pattern.
- **Check the invitation.** If it lists a dress rule, follow it. If you are unsure how a particular program will read an outfit, ask the coordinator the same way AAFP suggests for any attire question.
- **Think about the day, not only the room.** If your interview includes a hospital tour, make sure you can walk, climb stairs and, where needed, wear the personal protective equipment a unit asks for.

> [!IMG TIP] In many countries, interviews are formal and you may have worn a particular style for years. If you are choosing between a formal outfit that feels like you and a Western suit you have never worn, choose the one you can sit and speak in comfortably. Tailoring and wearing an outfit once before the interview reduces surprises.

## What to wear to a virtual residency interview

AAMC says a virtual interview is an official interview, and its applicant guidance asks you to approach it as you would any formal interview. For lighting, background, camera height and what to do if the connection fails, use the [virtual interview setup](/wiki/interviews-match/virtual-interview-setup) page. This section adds only what to wear.

- **Dress top to bottom.** A full outfit helps you feel the part, and you will not be caught out if you need to stand.
- **Wear solid colors** that contrast with your background. A white shirt on a white wall or a dark jacket against a dark wall can make you look flat. This is practice, not a rule.
- **Be careful with fine stripes and tight checks.** On some cameras they can shimmer. Plain fabrics are safer.
- **Check what the frame shows.** Usually it is your head and upper chest, so the collar, neckline, jacket and any scarf or necklace do most of the work.
- **Avoid noisy accessories.** Bracelets that tap the desk and long earrings that touch a headset show up on audio.
- **Test it.** Record 30 seconds in your real lighting and watch it back. Colors shift on camera, and glasses can glare.

Opinion: choose a virtual outfit you would be happy to wear in person too. It keeps the decision simple, and it prepares you if a program offers both formats.

## Dinners, socials and tours

Many programs hold a social event or dinner, in person or online, before or after the interview. Whether it is evaluated varies by program, so check the invitation.

- **If the invitation says "business casual,"** take it at its word and keep the outfit neat. Opinion: a more relaxed version of your interview outfit is a safe middle.
- **Virtual socials:** relaxed in tone, camera-ready in dress.
- **Tours:** comfortable shoes and an extra layer. Hospital and clinic temperatures can be cool.

Treat every contact with a program as part of the interview, as the [interview season overview](/wiki/interviews-match/interview-season-overview) explains.

## Weather, travel and packing for in-person interviews

Interview season can fall in the colder months in many regions, so check the forecast for each city and bring suitable outerwear.

- **Carry the outfit in hand luggage.** If a checked bag is delayed, you still have what you need.
- **Pack a spare shirt or top,** spare socks or hosiery, a small stain remover, safety pins and a lint roller.
- **Plan for wrinkles.** Many hotels have an iron or steamer. If not, hang the outfit in a steamy bathroom.
- **Break in shoes first,** and bring a second pair if the weather is wet.
- **Sort your travel documents early.** If you need a visitor visa for in-person interviews, see [B-1/B-2 visas for interviews and exams](/wiki/visas/b1-b2-for-interviews-and-exams). Do not buy an outfit or book travel until you know you can travel.

If you decide a second visit is worth it, see [second looks and visits](/wiki/interviews-match/second-looks-and-visits).

## On a budget: what IMGs actually need

You do not need a wardrobe. One outfit that fits well and is comfortable is enough for several interviews.

- **Change the shirt, neckwear or scarf** between interviews if you wish. Opinion: programs are unlikely to compare your outfit with what you wore elsewhere.
- **Prioritize fit.** A modest outfit with a good fit looks more polished than an expensive one that hangs badly. Allow time for alterations.
- **Borrow, rent or buy second-hand** if you would rather spend on travel. A colleague or friend may have an outfit that fits.
- **Buy once you have confirmed formats.** If most interviews are virtual, you may only need the visible part of the outfit and a good pair of neat shoes for the occasional in-person visit.

## Common mistakes

- **Wearing something you have not sat in.** You will notice every tight seam on a long day.
- **New shoes** on a day with a long tour.
- **Over-accessorizing,** or wearing a strong fragrance.
- **Ignoring the invitation.** A program that says business casual for dinner or gives specific instructions has told you what it wants.
- **Dressing for the camera only.** If you stand up for a technical problem, the rest of the outfit shows.
- **Stressing over the perfect outfit.** In our opinion, the wrong shade of navy will not decide an interview. Preparation and how you treat people will matter more. See [tell me about yourself](/wiki/interviews-match/tell-me-about-yourself) and [common interview questions](/wiki/interviews-match/common-interview-questions).

## A day-before checklist

1. Re-read the invitation for any dress instruction, the format and the start time in your time zone.
2. Try on the full outfit, shoes included, and check for stains, loose threads and wrinkles.
3. Press or steam the outfit, and lay it out.
4. For virtual interviews, test the outfit on camera in the lighting you will use and check the frame.
5. For in-person interviews, check the weather and pack outerwear, a second pair of shoes if needed and your travel documents.
6. Put your tracker row in order: time, link or address, contact and dress note. A free spreadsheet is on the [residency interview tracker](/wiki/interviews-match/residency-interview-tracker) page.
7. Stop. Over-planning the outfit steals time from practicing your answers.
