If you did not match, you have a short window to act and then a longer window to rebuild. First, if you are eligible, SOAP gives you a few days to pursue unfilled positions. After that, the decision is whether to reapply, change your plan or pivot. This page gives you a structure for both, without promising any particular outcome.

## First, the context

Not matching is common among IMGs. In NRMP's {{fact:nrmp_match_year}} Main Residency Match, among applicants who submitted a rank order list, {{fact:us_img_match_rate}} percent of U.S. citizen IMGs and {{fact:nonus_img_match_rate}} percent of non-U.S. citizen IMGs matched, compared with 93.5 percent of U.S. MD seniors. Many more IMGs register but do not submit a rank order list. For the data and definitions, see [IMG match rates by specialty](/wiki/specialties/img-match-rates-by-specialty).

It is not a verdict on your ability to practice medicine. It is a statement that the combination of your application and the seats available did not line up this cycle.

## Step 1: Understand your status at the start of Match Week

At the start of Match Week you learn whether you matched, but not where, if you did. You are:

- **Fully unmatched.** You did not match to any position.
- **Partially matched.** For example you matched only to a preliminary position, or only one partner of a couple matched.
- **Matched.** You will learn your program on Match Day.

Only unmatched or partially matched applicants who meet NRMP's conditions are SOAP-eligible. Read the rules in [SOAP explained](/wiki/interviews-match/soap-explained) and on NRMP's SOAP page.

## Step 2: Decide quickly on SOAP

A decision tree:

1. **Are you SOAP-eligible?** You must be registered, eligible to enter graduate medical education on July 1, and fully or partially unmatched on Monday of Match Week. IMGs must be verified by ECFMG as having met exam requirements. If you are not eligible, skip to Step 4.
2. **Do you have documents ready?** ERAS application, letters, USMLE transcript, personal statement, and any visa details.
3. **What are your limits?** Decide in advance: will you accept a preliminary year, a different specialty, a program that sponsors only a certain visa, or a location you did not choose?
4. **Apply to the unfilled programs where you realistically fit,** respond to contacts quickly, and treat every offer as a binding commitment.

In 2026, 2,851 positions were placed in SOAP, and IMGs together accounted for 34.3 percent of the positions filled. Many positions were one-year preliminary or transitional roles. See the detailed numbers in [SOAP explained](/wiki/interviews-match/soap-explained).

> [!WARNING] An accepted SOAP offer is binding under the Match Participation Agreement. Accept only what you will honor, and confirm visa sponsorship before accepting.

## Step 3: Think about what a preliminary or different-specialty offer means

- A **preliminary year** is one year of training. It may help your next application, but it does not guarantee a later categorical position. Read [[preliminary-year]] and [[categorical]], and ask the program about its pathways.
- A **different specialty** changes your long-term plan. Consider whether you could be happy in it, and whether it fits your visa.
- **Declining all offers** is a legitimate choice if the available positions are not right for you. Make sure you have a plan for the following year.

## Step 4: If you do not obtain a position

Give yourself a few days, then work through these steps.

1. **Ask for feedback.** Contact programs where you interviewed, a mentor or an advisor. Not all will answer, but some give useful feedback. Do not argue; ask what would make an application stronger.
2. **Diagnose honestly.** Common causes include too few programs, weak or missing US clinical experience, exam attempts, years since graduation, unfocused statements, visa limits, or too narrow a specialty list. NRMP's 2026 report for non-U.S. citizen IMGs found matched applicants ranked a median of 5 programs in their preferred specialty versus 2 for unmatched, suggesting the number of interviews is a key lever.
3. **Pick a plan for the year.** Options include:

| Option | What it can add | Cautions |
|---|---|---|
| More US clinical experience | Letters, familiarity with US practice | Quality and relevance matter more than count |
| Research position | Publications, a mentor | Not a substitute for scores or clinical letters |
| Strengthen your exam profile | A passed Step 3 can help some programs and visa timelines | Retakes and attempts are governed by USMLE rules; read them before planning |
| Switch or widen specialty | More programs to apply to | Needs a credible story and letters |
| Preliminary or transitional year | US training and a US reference | One year only; no guarantee after |
| Non-US pathway or a different career track | An alternative if the US path is closed | A serious decision; talk to mentors |

4. **Protect your timelines.** ECFMG certification requirements, visa documentation, years since graduation and exam validity windows can change what is possible. Check the current ECFMG rules on [ECFMG's certification page](https://www.ecfmg.org/certification/).
5. **Reapply with changes.** See the guide to [reapplicant strategy](/wiki/application/reapplicant-strategy) and revisit your [program list](/wiki/application/building-a-program-list).

> [!IMG TIP] Programs ask about what you did in the gap year. A clear, specific answer (clinical work, research, an exam, a rotation) helps. A blank year raises questions.

## Looking after yourself

An unmatched result is hard. Tell a mentor or friend early. If you are overwhelmed, speak to a health professional. A plan for the next step reduces the pressure, but you do not have to solve everything in the first week.

## When to get help

If you want a structured review of why your cycle went the way it did and a plan for the next one, a service such as SOAP support or match mentorship is optional. You can complete every step on this page on your own.
