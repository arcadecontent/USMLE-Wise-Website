This page logs US visa policy changes that affect international medical graduates, with the month, who is affected and an official link. Each entry is limited to what we could verify, and where we could not confirm something we say so. Rules and court orders can change faster than this wiki is reviewed, so use the log to know what to ask about, not as the final word. The last full update of this page was November 2026.

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: Two of the largest items are in litigation. The H-1B payment guidance was vacated by a federal district court in June 2026, and the rule ending "duration of status" for J-1 and F-1 holders was postponed by a federal district court in September 2026. Both could change again. See the entries below.

## Change log (newest first)

## Items to watch that we could not confirm

These are real topics, but we could not verify a current status from official sources when we wrote this page, so we send you to the source instead of stating a position.

- **Conrad 30 authorization.** USCIS posted in October 2025 that eligibility for J-1 physicians admitted on or after October 1, 2025 depended on Congress extending the program. Check the [USCIS page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/conrad-30-waiver-program) and your state health department. See [the two-year rule](/wiki/visas/two-year-home-residency-rule).
- **Whether a physician exemption exists for the H-1B payment.** The proclamation text we read does not name physicians. Ask the program what it is doing now.
- **Visa integrity fee.** Federal law created it, but we found conflicting reports about whether and where it is collected. Use the State Department's fee page.
- **Online presence review for H-1B and H-4.** The State Department has announced an extension of expanded vetting to these applicants; see its [announcement](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/News/visas-news/announcement-of-expanded-screening-and-vetting-for-h-1b-and-dependent-h-4-visa-applicants.html).
- **Immigrant visa pause for nationals of dozens of countries (January 2026).** It concerns immigrant visas (green card processing abroad), not residency visas, but it may matter for your long-term plan. Check the State Department's notice and your country.

## What each change means for your plan

### If you plan a J-1

Follow ECFMG's instructions, expect more attention to online presence at the interview, and check your country on the entry restrictions. Review the [J-1 visa process](/wiki/visas/j1-visa-process). Ask ECFMG and your program what the duration of status litigation means for your DS-2019 dates.

### If you plan an H-1B

Ask whether the program is cap-exempt, whether it will file for you at all given the payment rules, and when it needs Step 3 results. See [H-1B for residents](/wiki/visas/h1b-for-residents) and [Step 3 and H-1B timing](/wiki/visas/step-3-and-h1b-timing).

### If you plan a short visit for an exam or interview

Plan for an in-person visa interview and for processing in your country of nationality or residence. See [B-1/B-2 for exams and interviews](/wiki/visas/b1-b2-for-interviews-and-exams).

## How to monitor changes

1. **Bookmark the official sources**: the State Department's visa news and country pages, USCIS alerts and the H-1B pages, [j1visa.state.gov](https://j1visa.state.gov/), and the ECFMG and Intealth news pages.
2. **Ask your program's international office** what it has changed and what it expects from you. Programs react to new rules before most public guides.
3. **Check before each step**, not once. A date, fee, document or country list can change between the day you start and the day you act.
4. **Prefer primary sources.** Use university and law firm summaries as pointers, then open the official page they cite.
5. **Keep records.** Save screenshots or PDFs with dates of the guidance you relied on when you applied.
6. **Use an attorney for complex cases**, such as a previous refusal, an overstay, a country on a restriction list or a plan that depends on a waiver.

## About this log

This wiki is reviewed on a schedule, and entries are added when we can verify them. If you notice a change that is not here, contact your program's international office and check the official source, not this page.
