A cap-exempt H-1B employer can file a petition for you outside the annual H-1B numerical limit, so there is no registration lottery to win. For IMGs this matters because many residency employers are cap-exempt, and because the answer can change when you leave training. This page explains who qualifies and what to ask. For the basics of the visa, see [H-1B for residents](/wiki/visas/h1b-for-residents).

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: A weighted, wage-level selection process applies to cap-subject registrations starting with the fiscal year 2027 cap season, and a September 2025 proclamation set a payment requirement for some new H-1B petitions, which a court limited in June 2026. The USCIS pages we read on the payment do not say whether cap-exempt employers are treated differently, so ask the employer how it applies. See the [visa policy tracker](/wiki/visas/visa-policy-changes-tracker).

## Why the cap matters

Most H-1B petitions are subject to a yearly numerical limit. Employers that are cap-subject register in the spring, and USCIS selects registrations at random (now with weighting by wage level, per the USCIS cap season page). That calendar does not fit a July residency start. Cap-exempt employers skip the registration and can file when their own timeline requires. That is why programs at cap-exempt institutions are the ones most likely to offer H-1B to residents who have passed Step 3. See [Step 3 and H-1B timing](/wiki/visas/step-3-and-h1b-timing).

## Who is cap-exempt

Section 214(g)(5) of the Immigration and Nationality Act exempts several types of employer, and the regulation at 8 CFR 214.2(h)(8)(iii)(F) fills in the definitions. In summary:

| Employer type | How it qualifies |
|---|---|
| Institution of higher education | Defined by section 101(a) of the Higher Education Act of 1965. Universities and many medical schools fit. |
| Nonprofit related to or affiliated with a university | Meets one of four tests (below). |
| Nonprofit research organization | A nonprofit one of whose fundamental activities is basic or applied research. |
| Government research organization | A federal, state or local entity one of whose fundamental activities is performing or promoting research. |

A nonprofit is "related to or affiliated with" a university under the regulation if it:

1. is connected through shared ownership or control by the same board or federation;
2. is operated by an institution of higher education;
3. is attached to one as a member, branch, cooperative or subsidiary; or
4. has a formal written affiliation agreement with a university that establishes an active working relationship for research or education, and a fundamental activity of the nonprofit is to directly contribute to the university's research or education mission.

That last test is how many teaching hospitals qualify. A hospital with a residency and a medical school affiliation often documents a relationship with a university, but the hospital has to show it meets the regulation. A for-profit hospital or practice generally does not qualify as a nonprofit.

### Employees who work at a qualifying place

The regulation also says a worker not directly employed by a qualifying institution can qualify if at least half of their work time is at one and the job duties directly further its higher education, nonprofit research or government research purpose. Whether a particular setting meets this is fact specific and not something to assume.

## What it means for IMGs

- **During residency.** Many university-affiliated and academic hospitals are cap-exempt, which is why they can file H-1Bs for incoming residents. Community programs that are not affiliated with a university may be cap-subject. Ask each program, because the answer is not on a list.
- **After residency.** The regulation says that if cap-exempt employment ends and the worker is not the beneficiary of a new cap-exempt petition, the worker is subject to the cap unless they were already counted against it in the same six-year period of admission. Moving from an academic hospital to a private group can therefore be a different filing situation. The same applies to a waiver job: we did not find a cap exemption tied to waiver status in the sources we read, so ask whether the clinic or hospital is cap-exempt.
- **Concurrent employment.** The regulation allows a cap-exempt H-1B worker to take concurrent employment with a cap-subject employer without being subject to the cap, if specific conditions are met. This is relevant to moonlighting and academic appointments.
- **Time limits.** The regulation refers to a six-year period of authorized admission for H-1B workers. Talk to counsel about how your time on a J-1 and any prior H-1B time interact.

> [!WARNING] Cap exempt does not mean visa guaranteed. The employer must still choose to sponsor you, meet the other H-1B rules and be able to file in time. A hospital's cap status is the hospital's, not yours.

## Questions to ask a program

- Is the sponsoring institution cap-exempt, and on what basis?
- If it uses an affiliation agreement, with which university?
- Do you file H-1B for residents, and when must Step 3 be passed?
- Does your institution's counsel handle the payment-rule questions?
- Can the institution support a later change to another cap-exempt employer?

> [!IMG TIP] If you want an H-1B, put "cap-exempt" and "sponsors H-1B for residents" on the checklist you build for each program. Programs that say yes to both are a smaller set than the set that merely participate in the Match. See [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b) for the trade-offs.
