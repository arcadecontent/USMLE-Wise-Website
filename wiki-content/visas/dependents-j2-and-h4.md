The spouse and unmarried children of an IMG resident can usually accompany them, as J-2 dependents of a J-1 physician or H-4 dependents of an H-1B resident. The big difference is work: a J-2 spouse can apply for a work permit relatively early, while an H-4 spouse generally cannot work during residency. This page covers what each status allows and what changes at each stage. It is general information, not legal advice.

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: Visa processing rules have changed repeatedly since 2025, and the State Department has said it extended online presence review to H-4 applicants. Check the [visa policy changes tracker](/wiki/visas/visa-policy-changes-tracker), the embassy where you will apply, and your program's international office before you book travel for your family.

## Side by side

| | J-2 (dependent of a J-1 physician) | H-4 (dependent of an H-1B resident) |
|---|---|---|
| Who qualifies | Spouse and unmarried children under 21 | Spouse and children, as defined in the regulation |
| Form that supports the application | Separate DS-2019 for each dependent, issued by ECFMG | Derived from the principal's H-1B petition |
| Work for the spouse | Can apply for an employment authorization document (EAD) | No work authorization by status; EAD only in limited cases (see below) |
| Work for children | The ECFMG materials describe EAD eligibility for dependents generally; confirm age-specific rules with USCIS | Not eligible under the H-4 EAD rule, which is for spouses |
| Duration | As long as the principal maintains valid J-1 status | Same period as the principal's admission or extension |
| Two-year home rule | Applies if it applies to the J-1 | Not applicable (H-1B is not subject to it) |

## J-2 dependents

### Who is eligible and how they apply

ECFMG says it can sponsor a J-1 physician's spouse and unmarried children under 21. Parents, siblings, other relatives and nannies or caregivers are not eligible. Each dependent receives a separate DS-2019 after ECFMG reviews a request supported by a passport page and a marriage or birth certificate (with English translation where needed).

A DS-2019 alone does not grant status. If your family is abroad, each dependent applies for a J-2 visa at a US consulate with the DS-2019, a passport valid at least six months beyond the DS-2019 end date, and the marriage or birth certificate. They may enter with you or later. Canadian citizens are handled differently at the border, according to ECFMG. If a dependent is already in the US in another status, ECFMG describes a change of status through USCIS on Form I-539, and notes that leaving the country while that application is pending voids it.

### Work authorization

J-2 spouses and children may apply for an EAD by filing Form I-765 with USCIS. USCIS states that their income may not be used to support the J-1 holder, and ECFMG states the same. Once the EAD is issued, the dependent can apply for a Social Security number.

Processing times and fees change, so use [USCIS's I-765 page](https://www.uscis.gov/i-765) for current instructions. Many families plan on the EAD taking months, so do not assume your spouse can start a job immediately after arrival. Employment cannot begin until the EAD is issued.

### Other rules ECFMG highlights

- **Insurance.** J-1 physicians must maintain health insurance for themselves and their J-2 dependents that meets the sponsor's requirements.
- **Residence.** A J-2 dependent must live with the J-1 physician at the same US address.
- **Reporting.** The physician must tell ECFMG if a J-2 no longer needs sponsorship.
- **Duration.** A J-2 can stay as long as the principal maintains valid J-1 status.
- **Physician couples.** ECFMG cautions that J-1 sponsorship after a stay in J-2 status (and the reverse) should not be assumed. If both partners are physicians, plan together and ask ECFMG before one of you enters as a dependent.

## H-4 dependents

### Status and duration

Under the regulation, the spouse and children of an H-1B worker, if accompanying or following to join, may be admitted as H-4 nonimmigrants for the same period as the principal. H-4 status is derivative: it rises and falls with the H-1B holder's status. Dependents of an H-1B resident apply for the H-4 visa at a consulate, or request a change or extension of status in the US.

### Work

The regulation says H-4 status does not confer eligibility for employment authorization incident to status. The exception is limited to an H-4 spouse of an H-1B worker who either is the beneficiary of an approved immigrant petition or whose H-1B stay is extended under sections 106(a) and (b) of AC21. In practice, a spouse arriving with a first-time resident H-1B holder usually will not qualify at the start. The regulation refers to the H-4 spouse only, so H-4 children are not covered by that provision.

This changes if your employer later files for a green card and the case reaches the qualifying stage. See [green card paths for physicians](/wiki/visas/green-card-for-physicians) for the sequence and confirm the current EAD conditions with USCIS.

> [!IMG TIP] If a working spouse is central to your family's finances, ask early. Compare a J-1 (where a J-2 spouse can apply for an EAD) with an H-1B (where an H-4 spouse generally cannot work yet) as part of your program choice, alongside the waiver and green card questions in [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b).

## School and everyday life

Children of J-2 and H-4 holders can generally attend school in the United States, but we could not find a single official statement covering every case, such as public school enrollment documents or university admission. Ask your program's international office and the school what documents they need. Plan for health insurance, vaccination records, and translated birth certificates early.

## Travel

- **J-2.** ECFMG states that J-1 physicians and J-2 dependents who leave the US temporarily need a valid visa stamp, an unexpired passport and a travel-endorsed DS-2019 for reentry. The first-year DS-2019 is not automatically validated for travel, so request the endorsement before you leave.
- **H-4.** We did not find an official H-4 travel checklist to cite. As a general matter, dependents need a valid passport and a valid H-4 visa to reenter, so check the stamp's expiry before any trip and ask your employer's international office what documents to carry.
- **Both.** Apply in the country the State Department says you should, expect interviews, and keep your documents together in your carry-on.

## What changes at each stage

| Stage | What to watch |
|---|---|
| Before the Match | Know whether your target programs sponsor J-1, H-1B or both, and whether your family's plan depends on work authorization |
| After the Match | Ask when the program or ECFMG can request dependent documents; they often follow the principal's paperwork |
| First year | EAD filing for J-2, insurance, address reporting, travel endorsement before any trip |
| Renewals | Dependents' status follows yours; ECFMG renews J-1 sponsorship yearly |
| After residency | The J-2 is bound by the two-year rule if it applies to you, and J-2 and H-4 status end when yours do |
| Waiver or green card stage | Your family's plan changes with your status; see [Conrad 30](/wiki/visas/conrad-30-waiver) and [J-1 waiver options](/wiki/visas/j1-waiver-options) |

## Questions to ask

- Does the program sponsor dependents' paperwork or just the resident's?
- Is there health insurance coverage for family members, and who pays?
- Has the program had residents with working spouses, and how long did the EAD take?
- If the principal's visa is delayed, can the family arrive separately? See [visa delays and backup plans](/wiki/visas/visa-delays-and-backup-plans).

This wiki gives general information. For decisions about family status, an immigration attorney or your institution's international office can review your specific situation.
