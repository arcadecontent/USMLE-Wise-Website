After Match Day, a matched IMG who needs a visa has a series of steps that depend on other organizations, so the best plan works backward from your program start date and adds slack. Most residencies start around the end of June or beginning of July, but your own start date is in your contract and visa documents. This guide lays out a practical order of operations for a J-1 and an H-1B and the usual places for delays. It is general information, not legal advice.

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: Several rules that affect timing have changed since mid-2025, including online presence review for applicants, narrower interview waivers, the advice to apply in your country of nationality or residence, and entry restrictions for some nationalities. The dated log is on the [visa policy changes tracker](/wiki/visas/visa-policy-changes-tracker). Do not assume last year's timeline applies to you.

## Before Match Day

You can prepare without knowing where you will train.

- Check that your passport is valid for a long period beyond the planned start, and renew it early if it is not.
- Learn what your home country's health ministry requires for a Statement of Need, if you might use a J-1. Not every ministry works at the same speed.
- Collect your degree, transcripts and [[ecfmg-certificate]] information.
- If you hope for an H-1B, know your Step 3 status. See [Step 3 and H-1B timing](/wiki/visas/step-3-and-h1b-timing).
- Review your public social media profiles. The State Department has described online presence review for student and exchange visitor applicants.

## Right after the Match

1. **Read your contract and ask about the visa.** Confirm in writing which visa the program offers you, who files, and what it expects you to do.
2. **Ask for the program's deadlines.** Programs often need documents well before the legal deadlines.
3. **Check licensing.** Many states require a training license or permit before the visa can be finalized. Ask about timing and fees.
4. **Plan for nationality-specific issues.** Check the entry restrictions and consular guidance for your country on the [tracker](/wiki/visas/visa-policy-changes-tracker).

## The J-1 sequence

| Stage | What happens | Typical delay points |
|---|---|---|
| Program request | The program supports your sponsorship request to ECFMG | Program workload, missing documents |
| Statement of Need | Your health ministry issues it | Ministry speed, format, courier |
| ECFMG review and DS-2019 | ECFMG reviews and issues the DS-2019 | Errors in forms, name mismatches |
| DS-2019 delivery | The form goes to the institution's contact and then to you | Mail across borders |
| Visa application | DS-160, fee payment, interview | Appointment availability, extra review |
| Travel | You enter within the window shown on your documents | Late visa, flights, port-of-entry questions |

Details are in [The J-1 visa process](/wiki/visas/j1-visa-process). Work from the DS-2019 delivery date backward to see whether the Statement of Need needs to be in sooner.

## The H-1B sequence

| Stage | What happens | Typical delay points |
|---|---|---|
| Eligibility check | Step 3 passed, credentials in order | Step 3 result timing |
| Licensing | State training license or permit | State processing times |
| Employer filing | The employer prepares and files the petition | Legal review, institutional policy |
| Decision | USCIS decides; your employer may request premium processing | Requests for evidence, processing times |
| Visa or status change | If abroad, visa interview; if in the US, change of status | Appointment availability, proclamation questions |

Details are in [H-1B for residents](/wiki/visas/h1b-for-residents). Because H-1B rules and employer policies are in flux, ask the program how it is handling new petitions now.

## Build a backward plan

1. Write down your program start date and the earliest date you may enter, as shown in your visa documents.
2. Subtract time for travel and housing.
3. Subtract the time you think the visa interview and processing might take. Look at your embassy's own page, not a forum, for current waits.
4. Subtract time for the DS-2019 or petition approval.
5. Subtract time for the Statement of Need or licensing.
6. Compare the result with today's date. If the answer is already behind, tell the program early and ask what can be done.

> [!IMG TIP] Keep a one-page tracker with each step, the person responsible, the date you asked and the date you expect a result. Share it with your program coordinator. This is practice advice, not a rule, but it makes it easier to see when something has stalled and to ask the right person.

## If the visa is late

- Contact the program's international office and the GME office as soon as you see a risk, not the week before orientation.
- Ask what the program allows: a delayed start, a different start date, or other options. Policies differ, and a late start can affect your training dates and your DS-2019.
- If a consular officer asks for additional review, follow the instructions you are given and keep copies of everything you submit.
- Do not enter the US on a visitor visa to start work or training. That is not what the visa allows.

## What you can control

You cannot control consular schedules, security processing or court decisions, but you can control document accuracy, early starts and communication. Lack of a plan is the more common cause of last-minute stress.

## Who to ask

For visa questions, ask your program's international office, ECFMG or an immigration attorney.
