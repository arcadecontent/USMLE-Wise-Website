The visa interview is a short conversation, often a few minutes, in which a US consular officer decides whether you are eligible for the visa. For IMGs the best preparation is accuracy: documents that match your DS-160 and DS-2019 or petition, a clear statement of your training plan, and honest answers. This page explains what to bring, what the officer is deciding, and what the possible outcomes mean. For the whole J-1 sequence, see [the J-1 visa process](/wiki/visas/j1-visa-process).

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: Visa screening has changed since mid-2025, including online presence review for J applicants, narrower interview waivers and entry restrictions for some nationalities. These rules can change between the time you read this and your appointment. Read your embassy's page and the [visa policy changes tracker](/wiki/visas/visa-policy-changes-tracker).

## Before the appointment

According to the State Department's J-1 page, J-1 applicants submit the DS-2019 (issued by the sponsor after SEVIS registration), the online DS-160 application, a passport valid for the period the embassy requires, and a photo. Applicants should check the embassy or consulate website for extra items. A typical physician checklist, based on that page and on our [J-1 process guide](/wiki/visas/j1-visa-process):

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Passport | Check validity against your embassy's rule |
| DS-2019 (J-1) or approved petition notice (H-1B) | Signed as instructed; the details must match your DS-160 |
| DS-160 confirmation page | Answer every question yourself and accurately |
| Appointment confirmation and fee receipts | Fees and payment methods vary by embassy. See [[sevis]] for the program fee |
| Match and training documents | Your Match result or contract, program name and start date |
| Education and credentials | Medical degree, [[ecfmg-certificate]] and exam records |
| Evidence of finances | Contract or stipend information; ask your embassy what it expects |
| Ties to home | Evidence of family, property, a license to practise or a job you plan to return to |

> [!NOTE] Bring documents the officer might want, but do not hand over a large folder unless asked. Be ready to answer from memory and use documents to support your answers.

## What the officer is deciding

A J-1 physician is a temporary visitor. The State Department says applicants must show that they have binding ties to a residence in a foreign country and plan a temporary stay. The officer is deciding whether you will do what your DS-2019 says (train), whether you can support yourself, and whether you are otherwise eligible. Because many J-1 physicians later seek a waiver and a green card, some applicants are unsure how to answer questions about the future. The advice we can give is general: answer honestly, and be able to explain your plans in a consistent way.

The H-1B is different. It is a dual-intent category, so an intention to seek permanent residence later does not in itself disqualify you. Your answers should still be consistent with your petition.

For visitor visas for exams and interviews, see [B-1/B-2 for exams and interviews](/wiki/visas/b1-b2-for-interviews-and-exams), where the rules on proving ties are central.

## Questions to prepare for

Consular officers decide what to ask, and there is no official question list. Prepare clear, short answers on topics that commonly arise:

- What program and city are you going to, and what specialty?
- How did you match, and who is sponsoring you?
- How long is the program and what will you do after?
- Who is paying for your travel and living costs?
- What do your family and career look like at home?
- Have you worked or studied in the US before?
- Why this specialty and this program?

Answer in plain sentences. If you do not understand a question, ask the officer to repeat it. Do not memorize a script, and do not guess if you do not know.

## Online presence and the DS-160

The State Department announced in June 2025 that it was expanding screening of F, M and J applicants, including review of online presence, and asked applicants to set social media profiles to public. The DS-160 asks for social media identifiers. Review what your accounts show and be able to say honestly what they are. Our opinion: do not make sudden changes to accounts without asking your international office or an attorney, and never submit false information on any form.

## Outcomes and what they mean

| Outcome | What the State Department says |
|---|---|
| Approved | The consulate tells you how your passport and visa will be returned. Admission at the port of entry is a separate decision by border officers. |
| 221(g), more documents | A temporary refusal because information is missing. The State Department says there is a limited time to submit what is needed, after which you must reapply and pay again. |
| 221(g), administrative processing | A temporary refusal while the case is reviewed. The officer tells you at the interview, and timing varies. |
| 214(b) | A refusal because the officer was not satisfied you qualify, often because of ties. It is not permanent but cannot be appealed. You may reapply, usually after a significant change in circumstances. |

Do not rely on third-party estimates of how long administrative processing takes. The State Department's page says it varies. Tell your program and [[ecfmg]] if you receive a refusal or delay so they can help you plan around your start date.

## Practical steps

1. **Book early.** Wait times differ by city, and the State Department's J-1 page advises applying early. Check where you are allowed to apply.
2. **Submit the DS-160 carefully.** Errors cause delay.
3. **Practise your answers aloud** with a friend or a program coordinator.
4. **Arrive prepared.** Know the embassy's rules about electronics and bags.
5. **Keep copies.** Photograph or scan documents before you travel.
6. **Have a plan B.** See what your program does if your visa is delayed.

> [!IMG TIP] If your visa is late, tell the program coordinator and the international office early. Programs have seen delays before and may have options, such as a later start, that they cannot offer if they hear about it at the last minute.

Country-specific notes, including current entry restrictions that may affect some nationalities, are in the [country guides](/wiki/countries).
