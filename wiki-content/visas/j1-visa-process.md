Most IMGs who train in a US residency do so on a J-1 exchange visitor visa, and for physicians the sponsor is ECFMG, not the hospital. The path is: match, get your program's support, obtain the Statement of Need, have ECFMG issue a DS-2019 form, apply for the visa, enter the US, and renew sponsorship each year. This guide walks through the sequence and the places where IMGs lose time. For the choice between J-1 and H-1B, see [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b).

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: Visa processing rules for J-1 applicants have changed repeatedly since mid-2025 (online presence vetting, interview and waiver rules, travel restrictions for some nationalities, and a proposed end to "duration of status" that a court has postponed). The dated log is on the [visa policy changes tracker](/wiki/visas/visa-policy-changes-tracker). Confirm current requirements with [[ecfmg]], your program's international office and the embassy or consulate where you will apply.

## What the J-1 is, and who sponsors it

The J-1 is an exchange visitor category. For graduate medical education, the US Department of State designates [[intealth]]'s ECFMG program (the Exchange Visitor Sponsorship Program, EVSP) as the sponsor. The residency program must support the application, but ECFMG decides whether to sponsor you and issues the DS-2019 (the "Certificate of Eligibility for Exchange Visitor Status").

Two features matter for planning. First, J-1 sponsorship is tied to a specific program, specialty and training dates. Changing program or specialty generally requires ECFMG approval and may require a new Statement of Need. Second, most J-1 physicians are subject to the two-year home-country physical presence requirement. Read the [two-year home residency rule](/wiki/visas/two-year-home-residency-rule) before you accept a J-1 offer.

## Step by step

### 1. Match, then confirm the visa plan

After [[match-day]], confirm in writing which visa the program sponsors and who handles the paperwork. Many programs have a coordinator or institutional international office. The program typically starts the sponsorship request with ECFMG through ECFMG's online system. Ask for the program's internal deadlines, because they are usually earlier than the legal ones.

### 2. Meet ECFMG's eligibility requirements

ECFMG sets its own requirements for sponsorship, including requirements about your [[ecfmg-certificate]] status and your appointment letter. These details are updated by ECFMG, so read the current requirements on [ECFMG's J-1 sponsorship page](https://ecfmg.org/evsp/applying-general.html) rather than relying on an old forum thread. For the certification itself, see [ECFMG certification overview](/wiki/ecfmg/ecfmg-certification-overview).

### 3. Obtain the Statement of Need

A Statement of Need is a document from the health ministry (or equivalent authority) of your country of last legal permanent residence. It states that your country needs physicians trained in your specialty and supports your training in the US. ECFMG describes it as a J-1 sponsorship requirement. It is a frequent source of delay because ministries differ in speed, format and fees, and a new one may be needed if you change specialty or institution, or when a letter on file expires.

> [!IMG TIP] Start the Statement of Need process as early as ECFMG's instructions allow for your country. Ask ministry contacts, your school or others from your country who recently completed it how long it took. Treat that as practice, not a rule: ministry timelines vary and can change.

### 4. ECFMG issues the DS-2019

When ECFMG approves your sponsorship, it issues the DS-2019, which lists your program, dates and training level. ECFMG sends it to the training institution's contact rather than directly to you, and for initial applicants it is normally mailed, so allow time for delivery (especially across borders). Check that your name, date of birth, program and dates match your passport and offer.

### 5. Pay required fees and complete the visa application

You will pay several fees, which can include the SEVIS fee, the visa application fee and fees charged by the sponsor. Do not rely on a number from a blog. The visa application fee is on the [State Department fee page](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees.html) (current reference: {{fact:j1_visa_fee}}), and other fees are listed by the agency that charges them. Then complete the DS-160 online application and schedule an interview at a US embassy or consulate.

### 6. Visa interview

Bring your passport, DS-2019 signed as instructed, DS-160 confirmation, the SEVIS payment receipt, your appointment letter and ECFMG documents. The State Department's [J-1 interview and documents page](https://j1visa.state.gov/participants/how-to-apply/interviews-documents/) lists the standard documents. Since 2025, rules on where you may apply, who may skip the interview, and online presence review have tightened; see the callout below.

> [!CHANGED] Since June 2025 the State Department has described expanded screening of F, M and J applicants, including a review of online presence, and the DS-160 asks for social media identifiers. Interview waiver categories were also narrowed from October 2025, and the State Department says nonimmigrant applicants should apply in their country of nationality or residence. Official pages: [expanded screening announcement](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2025/06/announcement-of-expanded-screening-and-vetting-for-visa-applicants), [interview waiver update](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/News/visas-news/interview-waiver-update-sept-18-2025.html), [applying in your country of residence](https://travel.state.gov/en/newsroom/visas/2026/adjudicating-nonimmigrant-visa--niv--applicants-in-their-country.html). Check your embassy's own page for appointment availability.

### 7. Travel and arrival

You may generally enter the US only within the window before your program start date shown in the instructions that come with your DS-2019. Report to your program on time, since the sponsor tracks your arrival. ECFMG has stated that the first training year's DS-2019 is not validated for international travel and that you must request a travel-validated form if you plan to leave the US that year.

### 8. Stay in status

You renew J-1 sponsorship with ECFMG every year while you train, and a renewal may require an updated Statement of Need. J-1 status ends with your training; ECFMG's guidance describes a short period after the end date to depart. Moonlighting and other outside work are restricted, so ask the program before taking any extra work.

## If you are from a country affected by travel restrictions

A presidential proclamation effective January 1, 2026 fully or partially suspends visa issuance for nationals of certain countries, and the partial list includes exchange visas. Existing valid visas issued before that date were not revoked under it, but you must check the current country list and exceptions before you pay for a Statement of Need or an interview. See the [proclamation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/restricting-and-limiting-the-entry-of-foreign-nationals-to-protect-the-security-of-the-united-states/) and ask [[ecfmg]] and the program early.

## How long can you stay on a J-1?

ECFMG sponsorship covers the length of your training program, subject to the maximum allowed by the J-1 rules for graduate medical education. A rule to replace "duration of status" with fixed admission dates was published in July 2026 and postponed by a court in September 2026, so your admission paperwork may look different from past cohorts. See the [tracker](/wiki/visas/visa-policy-changes-tracker).

## Who to ask

For your own paperwork, rely on ECFMG, the program's international office and, for complex cases, an immigration attorney. This wiki gives general information and does not provide legal advice.
