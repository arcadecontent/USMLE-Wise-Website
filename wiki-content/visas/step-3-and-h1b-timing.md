If you want an H-1B for residency, treat Step 3 as the deadline everything else hangs on. In practice, an IMG resident H-1B requires that you have passed all three USMLE Steps before the employer files, so the real question is not "when should I take Step 3" but "when must a passing result exist for the programs I want." This guide shows how to work backward from that date. For the full requirements, see [H-1B for residents](/wiki/visas/h1b-for-residents), and for the exam itself, the [Step 3 guide](/wiki/exams/step-3).

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: H-1B rules for new petitions are in flux (a presidential proclamation payment, court rulings and employer policy), and some programs have changed whether they offer H-1B at all. Re-confirm each program's position before you commit to a Step 3 date, and see the [tracker](/wiki/visas/visa-policy-changes-tracker).

## Why Step 3 matters for the H-1B

Federal rules for foreign medical graduates, together with state licensing requirements and employer policy, mean that most programs will not file a resident H-1B until you have passed Steps 1, 2 CK and 3. That is a practice you should verify with each program, not a figure printed on a government card. A J-1 through ECFMG works differently and, in general, does not wait on Step 3, which is why the two visa routes lead to different exam timelines. See [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b).

## Start from the program's date, not your own

Ask each program these questions and write down the answers:

1. Do you sponsor H-1B for residents, and for which positions?
2. Do you require Step 3 passed before the Match, before ranking, before filing, or before the start date?
3. What is the latest date you can file and still start us on time?
4. Is the employer cap-exempt? If not, how does the registration timing work?
5. What do you do if the Step 3 result is late or a retake is needed?

Programs answer differently, and some state requirements push the Step 3 date earlier. Their answer is more important than any general rule of thumb.

## The backward plan

Put dates on a calendar from the right side to the left:

| Work backward from | Ask or look up |
|---|---|
| Program start date | Your contract and visa documents |
| Date you must arrive | Entry window in your visa documents |
| Visa processing time | The embassy's or USCIS's current information, not forum posts |
| Employer filing date | The program's deadline for the petition |
| State license application | The state board's processing time |
| Step 3 result date | USMLE's current score release time frame, plus a buffer |
| Step 3 exam date | Your test center availability and your readiness |

Do the arithmetic once, then add slack. Every line is subject to change, so check them again as you go.

## Before the Match or after?

This is a judgment call, not a rule. The considerations are:

- **Taking Step 3 early** widens the pool of H-1B programs and may reduce stress later. It also needs honest readiness, because Step 3 includes clinical content and a computer-based case simulation component. See the [Step 3 guide](/wiki/exams/step-3). Check the current USMLE eligibility and attempt rules before booking: the [Bulletin of Information](https://www.usmle.org/bulletin-information) is the authority.
- **Taking Step 3 after the Match** lets you target your preparation, and some IMGs have a long window before the start date. It carries timing risk if the program needs the result before it can file, and a failure leaves little time.
- **If you matched** and are weighing whether H-1B is still possible, talk to the program as soon as possible rather than guessing.

Opinion: if H-1B is a high priority and your Step 1 and Step 2 CK preparation is already behind you, plan Step 3 early enough that a retake is still possible within the program's timeline. That is a judgment, not a rule. For sequencing across the exams, see [When to take each Step](/wiki/exams/when-to-take-each-step).

## Step 3 access and eligibility

You must meet USMLE's eligibility requirements to register for Step 3, and they have their own documentation steps. Those requirements, and any waiting periods after a failed attempt, are published by USMLE, and you should confirm the current text in the Bulletin of Information. Do not book a flight or an exam date before you have your scheduling permit.

## Licensing is part of the timeline

Many states require a training license or permit for residents, and some tie it to Step 3 or to a time frame after the Match. The state board's processing time then sits between your exam result and the employer's filing. Ask the program which state requirements apply and when they apply.

## If Step 3 does not fit

If your Step 3 result will not exist when the program needs it:

- Ask whether the program will accept a J-1 instead. Many do. See [The J-1 visa process](/wiki/visas/j1-visa-process).
- Ask whether the start date can move. Some programs can delay, but many cannot.
- Consider whether a different set of programs fits your timeline better. A broader list that includes J-1 programs is common practice, though its effect on interview chances depends on your profile.
- Speak with an immigration attorney if you want to keep H-1B available for a later change of status.

> [!WARNING] Do not assume you can change from a J-1 to an H-1B later without checking the two-year home-country requirement. Most J-1 physicians are subject to it, which blocks a change to H status until it is fulfilled or waived. See [the two-year rule](/wiki/visas/two-year-home-residency-rule).

## Common mistakes

- Assuming "H-1B program" means Step 3 is not needed, or that it is needed on the same date at every program.
- Choosing a test date without asking when the result must be available.
- Taking Step 3 too early or too late for your readiness because of visa anxiety alone.
- Forgetting that the state license and the employer's legal review take time of their own.
