A J-1 physician who must satisfy the two-year home-country requirement usually has to either spend two years in the home country or obtain a waiver. The physician waivers fall into a few families: state Conrad 30 programs, federal agency programs, regional commission programs and the less common hardship and persecution bases. This page compares them at a high level so you know which questions to ask. It does not tell you which one fits your case. For the rule itself, see [the two-year home residency requirement](/wiki/visas/two-year-home-residency-rule).

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: Conrad 30 depends on a federal sunset date, and we could not confirm a plain statement of its current status from an official page. See [the Conrad 30 page](/wiki/visas/conrad-30-waiver) for what we verified. The regional commission and agency programs described below are not tied to that date, but we read mostly program pages dated before 2026, so confirm current terms with each agency.

## The waiver families at a glance

| Route | Who sponsors the request | Typical expectation | Main limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conrad 30 | A state health department | Full-time H-1B work at a shortage-area facility, at least three years | Up to 30 per state per fiscal year; state rules and deadlines vary |
| HHS clinical care waiver | HHS, on the institution's request | Primary-care or mental health practice in a designated shortage area | Narrow specialty list and site requirements; the institution files |
| Regional commissions (ARC, DRA and others) | A commission, with state support | Roughly three years of full-time practice in a designated shortage area | Only in the commission's region; extra recruitment evidence |
| Department of Veterans Affairs | The VA | Work for the VA in the way the request describes | Requires the VA to show a program need; not widely available |
| Other interested government agency | A US federal agency | Depends on the agency's program | Agency must show the physician is vital to its program |
| Exceptional hardship or persecution | You, through Form I-612 | None; these are not job-based | Demanding standards; hardship needs a US citizen or permanent resident spouse or child |

The State Department's Waiver Review Division makes the recommendation on most of these before USCIS acts. For the physician routes the key conditions in the USCIS Policy Manual are an H-1B job in a federally designated shortage area, a service commitment of at least three years, and a contract with the facility (with exceptions for specific federal employers).

## Conrad 30

A state sponsors you, and you work full time for at least three years at a qualifying site. Rules differ by state, and each state has a fixed number of waivers. See [Conrad 30 explained](/wiki/visas/conrad-30-waiver).

## HHS clinical care waiver

HHS runs a clinical care program for physicians. As HHS describes it, the institution applies on the physician's behalf, the physician first obtains a case number from the State Department, and HHS looks at the specialty (primary care and general psychiatry are the examples HHS gives), the site's shortage-area designation, recruitment efforts and a contract. HHS also expects a state health department letter. Specialty and site rules are narrower than Conrad 30's, so check the HHS instructions and the current checklist before assuming you qualify.

## Regional commissions

The Appalachian Regional Commission, the Delta Regional Authority and other regional bodies run waiver programs that sponsor physicians for practice in their region. ARC's policy says requests must be sponsored by a state in the Appalachian Region on the recommendation of that state's public health official, with a service commitment of about three years in a federally designated shortage area. Other commissions use similar structures with their own conditions. If a state's Conrad 30 slots are full or the job is in a region where a commission operates, this can be a second option, but you need the employer and the state's support.

## Department of Veterans Affairs and other agencies

Any US government agency can ask for a waiver on public interest grounds if it can show the physician is vital to its program. The USCIS Policy Manual notes the VA as one place where physicians fall under special rules: the three years can be with the VA and need not be in a shortage area. In practice VA requests are less common and agency specific, so ask the facility's human resources team and the agency before planning around one.

## Hardship and persecution

These two bases do not depend on a job offer. Exceptional hardship concerns hardship to a US citizen or permanent resident spouse or child if you had to leave the US or live in your home country. Persecution concerns a well-founded fear on certain grounds. Both are filed on Form I-612 and are decided on facts that are specific to you. They are hard standards, not a convenient alternative if a Conrad 30 slot is unavailable.

## What does not work

The no objection waiver, where the home government says it does not object, is not available to physicians who came to the US for graduate medical education. The USCIS Policy Manual says so, and the I-612 instructions say not to file that form for no objection or interested agency requests.

## How to choose

1. **Start with the dates.** Your DS-2019 end date and each agency's cycle decide what is realistic.
2. **List the states and regions you would accept.** Most waivers tie you to a facility for years.
3. **Check your specialty.** HHS, state programs and regional commissions treat specialties differently.
4. **Find the employer first, then the route.** Most routes need a job offer.
5. **Keep a backup.** Funding, authorization and policy can change. See the [policy tracker](/wiki/visas/visa-policy-changes-tracker).

> [!WARNING] Do not sign a waiver contract without understanding what happens if you leave early, are laid off or the facility changes. The service commitment is part of a legal waiver, and early exit can bring the home-country requirement back.

After the commitment, a physician may move toward permanent residence. See [green card paths for physicians](/wiki/visas/green-card-for-physicians).
