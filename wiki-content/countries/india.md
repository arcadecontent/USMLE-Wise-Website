Graduates of Indian medical schools are among the largest IMG groups applying for US residency, but no single official US source gives a clean, current count of them. This guide covers what we could verify about data, school eligibility, USMLE testing, documents and the visa process, and says where we could not confirm something. It applies to graduates of schools in India. If you are an Indian citizen who studied elsewhere, your school's country, not your citizenship, drives most of the rules below.

## What the data does and does not say

The NRMP counts applicants by type, not by country. In its {{fact:nrmp_match_year}} Main Residency Match report, an IMG is defined by where the school is located, and "non-U.S. IMGs" (non-U.S. citizens who attended a school outside the US and Canada that is not accredited by the LCME or AOA COCA) are reported as one category. The same report says {{fact:img_active_applicants}} active applicants in {{fact:nrmp_match_year}} were IMGs, counting both U.S. citizen IMGs and non-U.S. IMGs. It does not list Match results for graduates of Indian schools, and neither do the Charting Outcomes reports we reviewed.

ECFMG also tracks applicants by school, but we could not open any country-level tables (ecfmg.org returns errors to automated tools). If you need a figure, ask ECFMG or cite a published study with its year, and do not rely on forum posts. For what the numbers mean for your odds, see [IMG match statistics](/wiki/start-here/img-match-statistics).

## Checking your school's eligibility

ECFMG decides eligibility school by school and year by year. The steps:

1. Find your school in the World Directory and open the **ECFMG Sponsor Note**. Read the graduation years it lists. See [the World Directory guide](/wiki/ecfmg/world-directory-and-school-eligibility).
2. Understand the accreditation requirement. ECFMG requires accreditation by an agency recognized by the World Federation for Medical Education (WFME) for the relevant graduation years. See [the accreditation requirement](/wiki/ecfmg/accreditation-requirement).
3. Know the national body. India's regulator is the National Medical Commission (NMC), which replaced the Medical Council of India. On the WFME Recognition Programme list we read, the NMC appears with recognition status until September 2033.

Recognition of the agency does not settle your case. Your school's own status and your graduation year are in the Sponsor Note. If anything there is unclear, ask ECFMG, and ask your school's registrar for written confirmation of its accreditation history.

> [!KEY] Agency recognition and your eligibility are two different checks. Do both, and treat the Sponsor Note as the answer for your graduation year.

## USMLE testing from India

Since January 2026 IMGs register for the USMLE through FSMB, while ECFMG still decides eligibility for ECFMG Certification. USMLE says Step 1 and Step 2 CK are offered at Prometric centers worldwide and that Step 3 is given only in the US and its territories. See [the current announcement](https://www.usmle.org/usmle-service-transition-launching-january-2026).

What we could not verify is which Indian cities currently have centers and how many seats they offer. Third-party guides disagree, and the official USMLE pages we read do not list cities. Use the Prometric locator after your permit is issued, compare several centers and dates, and read [our test center guide](/wiki/exams/international-test-centers). If you plan to test outside India, confirm how the testing region on your permit works before you pay for travel.

## Documents and credential verification

ECFMG verifies your final medical diploma and transcript directly with the issuing school. Indian candidates should start early because universities and colleges can take time to respond, though we have no official statistic on this. Practical steps:

- Check the ECFMG Reference Guide for the exact diploma title required for your school and graduation year.
- Confirm that your name appears the same way on every document, including your passport.
- Ask your school who handles ECFMG verification requests and how to track them.
- Read [the EPIC and verification guide](/wiki/ecfmg/epic-credential-verification) and [common ECFMG delays](/wiki/ecfmg/common-ecfmg-delays).

We could not confirm from official pages whether ECFMG asks for any Indian registration certificate. Check the current Information Booklet rather than assuming.

## The US visa process, in brief

Most IMGs are sponsored by ECFMG for a J-1 visa, or by an employer for an H-1B. The J-1 path runs from a Statement of Need and DS-2019 through a visa interview at a US embassy or consulate. See [the J-1 process](/wiki/visas/j1-visa-process) and [J-1 versus H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b). Also read about [visa interview preparation](/wiki/visas/visa-interview-preparation) and [timing after the Match](/wiki/visas/visa-timeline-after-the-match).

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: We read the text of Proclamation 10998 (effective January 1, 2026). India does not appear on its full or partial suspension lists. That can change, so check the [White House proclamation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/restricting-and-limiting-the-entry-of-foreign-nationals-to-protect-the-security-of-the-united-states/) and travel.state.gov before each step. Other changes since 2025, such as online presence review and narrower interview waivers, apply to applicants of every nationality; see [the visa policy tracker](/wiki/visas/visa-policy-changes-tracker).

For a short visit to take Step 3 or attend an in-person interview, see [B-1/B-2 for exams and interviews](/wiki/visas/b1-b2-for-interviews-and-exams). Appointment waits at your embassy or consulate are published by the State Department and change often, so we do not quote them. This is general information, not legal advice. Ask the program's international office, ECFMG or an immigration attorney about your case.

## Community and professional resources

The American Association of Physicians of Indian Origin (AAPI) is a US-based physician organization for physicians of Indian origin. Programs and membership terms change, so read its [About page](https://aapiusa.org/about/) yourself. ECFMG also keeps a page listing ethnic physician organizations; check it for current entries. These groups are networking resources, not routes to eligibility or placement.

## What to do next

1. Look up your school and graduation year in the World Directory today.
2. Request your documents in the format ECFMG requires.
3. Check Prometric for seats before you fix a study end date.
4. Read [USMLE eligibility for IMGs](/wiki/exams/usmle-eligibility-for-imgs) and [the ECFMG certification overview](/wiki/ecfmg/ecfmg-certification-overview), and plan US experience with [the USCE guide](/wiki/clinical-experience/usce-explained).
