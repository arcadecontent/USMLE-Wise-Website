If you graduated from a medical school in Bangladesh, the most important check is not the exam or the visa. It is whether your school and graduation year are covered by ECFMG's eligibility rules. This guide shows how to check that, what is and is not publicly known about Bangladeshi IMGs in the Match, and what to plan for testing, documents and a visa. It is general information, not legal advice.

## What the published data say about IMGs from Bangladesh

NRMP counts IMGs by citizenship, not by country of medical school. In the {{fact:nrmp_match_year}} Main Residency Match, {{fact:us_img_ranked}} U.S. citizen IMGs and {{fact:nonus_img_ranked}} non-U.S. citizen IMGs submitted rank order lists, and {{fact:us_img_matched}} and {{fact:nonus_img_matched}} of them matched to a PGY-1 position ({{fact:us_img_match_rate}} and {{fact:nonus_img_match_rate}} percent). NRMP's {{fact:nrmp_match_year}} Charting Outcomes report for non-U.S. IMGs also groups applicants by specialty and Match status, not by country.

We found no Bangladesh-specific match figure in the NRMP reports we downloaded or in ECFMG's certification fact sheet, which reports totals only (13,431 physicians from 1,628 schools in 146 countries certified in 2024). So we do not quote one. Be wary of any site that does without naming its source and year. For how to read the numbers you do have, see [IMG match statistics](/wiki/start-here/img-match-statistics).

## Step 1: check your school and graduation year

ECFMG requires that your school is listed in the World Directory of Medical Schools with an ECFMG Sponsor Note, and that your graduation year is among the years the note covers. Since 2024, ECFMG has also required that the school be accredited by an agency recognized by the World Federation for Medical Education (WFME). The full steps are in the [World Directory guide](/wiki/ecfmg/world-directory-and-school-eligibility) and the [accreditation requirement explained](/wiki/ecfmg/accreditation-requirement).

Bangladesh's situation has a feature worth understanding:

- **The national regulator:** the Bangladesh Medical and Dental Council (BMDC) registers doctors. Registration is not the same thing as accreditation of a school by a WFME-recognized agency.
- **The accreditor:** the Bangladesh Medical Education Accreditation Council (BMEAC) was set up under a 2023 law, and The Daily Star reported that it accredited two government medical colleges for the first time. On WFME's list of agencies, BMEAC appears under agencies applying for recognition. We did not find it on the recognized list in November 2026.
- **What that means for you:** ECFMG's rule is about recognition by WFME. A national accreditation does not by itself satisfy it. Your Sponsor Note is the practical answer, because ECFMG applies the rule school by school and year by year.

> [!WARNING] Do not infer your eligibility from news about BMEAC, a school's own marketing or a social media post. Read your school's current Sponsor Note, compare the graduation years with yours, and ask ECFMG if anything is unclear.

If you are still a student, check before your final year. If the Sponsor Note does not cover your graduation year, ask ECFMG, not a third party, what your options are. See [ECFMG certification overview](/wiki/ecfmg/ecfmg-certification-overview).

## USMLE testing from Bangladesh

IMGs register for USMLE through FSMB since January 2026, and ECFMG still decides eligibility for certification. Step 1 and Step 2 CK are given at Prometric centers worldwide; Step 3 is given only in the US and its territories, per the USMLE Bulletin.

We could not verify a Bangladesh center on usmle.org or Prometric's own pages. Secondary sources mention a center in Dhaka, but those can be out of date. The reliable process is to receive your scheduling permit, then search the Prometric site for centers and dates, and have a backup in a nearby country if seats are scarce. See [USMLE test centers outside the US](/wiki/exams/international-test-centers) and [scheduling with Prometric](/wiki/exams/scheduling-with-prometric). If you test abroad, a visitor visa or other entry permission for that country is your responsibility, so check it early.

## Credentials and documents

ECFMG verifies your final medical diploma and transcript directly with the school that issued them. Delays at the school are the most common cause of slow certification, so ask your registrar early, learn who signs and sends the verification, and keep your contact details current. ECFMG's Reference Guide for Medical Education Credentials states the exact diploma title it requires for your school and year; check it rather than assume your degree name matches. See [EPIC credential verification](/wiki/ecfmg/epic-credential-verification) and [common ECFMG delays](/wiki/ecfmg/common-ecfmg-delays).

Some US state boards and programs also ask for registration or good standing documents from the national regulator. Ask the program or board which they want rather than collecting everything speculatively.

## The US visa process for Bangladeshi nationals

Most IMGs from Bangladesh who match need a visa to train: usually a J-1 sponsored by ECFMG, or an H-1B if the program sponsors it. The steps are in [J-1 visa process](/wiki/visas/j1-visa-process) and [J-1 vs H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b). Visa applicants should expect an in-person interview and review of their online presence; see [visa interview preparation](/wiki/visas/visa-interview-preparation). Some J-1 physicians are subject to the [two-year home residency rule](/wiki/visas/two-year-home-residency-rule), which affects what happens after training.

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: Proclamation 10998, effective January 1, 2026, fully or partly suspends entry for nationals of a list of countries. We read the proclamation text on whitehouse.gov and Bangladesh is not named in it. We did not find a later proclamation adding it. Lists can be amended, so check the [State Department](https://travel.state.gov) and our [visa policy tracker](/wiki/visas/visa-policy-changes-tracker) before you book travel.

Interview wait times and any additional document requests vary by post and by month, and we could not verify current figures, so check the appointment page of the US Embassy in Dhaka.

## Community and professional resources

We did not find an official, verifiable network we can recommend. Your school's alumni office, your own faculty who trained in the US, and ECFMG's and your program's published guidance are the safest starting points. Treat unofficial groups as a source of leads, not rules.

2. Ask your registrar how diploma and transcript verification works.
3. Plan your exams around Prometric availability and travel, not around a center you have not confirmed.
4. Only then plan [US clinical experience](/wiki/clinical-experience/usce-explained) and visa timing.
