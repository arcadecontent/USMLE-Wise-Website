Filipino graduates have a long history in US medicine, but official US data does not break results out by country, so this guide focuses on the checks you can do yourself. It covers what we could verify about numbers, school eligibility, USMLE testing, documents and the visa process, and says where we could not confirm something. It is about graduates of schools in the Philippines; your school's location, not your citizenship, drives most of the rules.

## What the data does and does not say

NRMP defines IMGs by where the school is located and reports non-U.S. IMGs as one group. Its 2026 Main Residency Match report says 16,154 active applicants were IMGs (U.S. citizen and non-U.S. citizen combined), with no country breakdown. We found no ECFMG or NRMP table with a current count for graduates of Philippine schools that we could open (ecfmg.org returns errors to automated tools), so we give none. Note that many Filipino physicians in the US trained decades ago; current applicant numbers are not the same as the size of that workforce. See [IMG match statistics](/wiki/start-here/img-match-statistics).

## Checking your school's eligibility

1. Search your school in the World Directory and open the **ECFMG Sponsor Note**. Read the graduation years it lists. See [the World Directory guide](/wiki/ecfmg/world-directory-and-school-eligibility).
2. Understand the requirement that your school be accredited by a WFME-recognized agency for your graduation year. See [the accreditation requirement](/wiki/ecfmg/accreditation-requirement).
3. Know the national bodies:

| Body | Role | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Commission on Higher Education (CHED) | Regulates higher education, including medical degree programs | CHED's own site for program recognition |
| Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) | Handles professional licensure | PRC's own site for licensure rules |
| Philippine Accrediting Association of Schools, Colleges and Universities (PAASCU) | Private accrediting association | WFME list: recognition status until April 2033 |

We confirmed PAASCU's status on the WFME Recognition Programme page and that CHED and PRC exist as national bodies. We did not open their official pages, so confirm their current roles and any programme recognition rules on their own sites.

WFME recognition applies to PAASCU as an agency. It does not tell you whether your school was accredited by PAASCU, or accredited in your graduation year. The Sponsor Note does. Ask your school for written confirmation of its accreditation history as well.

> [!KEY] The agency's WFME status, your school's accreditation and your graduation year are three separate checks. The Sponsor Note is the authoritative answer.

## USMLE testing

Since January 2026 IMGs register for the USMLE through FSMB, while ECFMG decides eligibility for ECFMG Certification ([announcement](https://www.usmle.org/usmle-service-transition-launching-january-2026)). USMLE says Step 1 and Step 2 CK are offered at Prometric centers worldwide, and Step 3 only in the US and its territories.

Unofficial sources name Philippine cities, but we could not confirm any from usmle.org or Prometric and do not repeat them. After you get a permit, use the Prometric locator, compare several centers and dates, and read [the test center guide](/wiki/exams/international-test-centers). If no seat fits your window you may need to test in another country, which can involve a visa and testing region questions; see [B-1/B-2 for exams](/wiki/visas/b1-b2-for-interviews-and-exams).

## Documents and credential verification

ECFMG verifies your final diploma and transcript directly with the issuing school; copies sent by you do not replace that. Practical steps:

- Check the ECFMG Reference Guide for the exact diploma title for your school and graduation year.
- Match names and dates across diploma, transcript and passport. Name order and middle names are a common mismatch source across any country.
- Ask your school's registrar which office answers ECFMG and keep a log.
- See [the EPIC and verification guide](/wiki/ecfmg/epic-credential-verification) and [common ECFMG delays](/wiki/ecfmg/common-ecfmg-delays).

We found no official ECFMG note specific to Philippine documents, and we could not confirm whether ECFMG asks for PRC licensure documents. Check the current Information Booklet.

## The US visa process, in brief

The usual route is an ECFMG-sponsored J-1 (Statement of Need, DS-2019, visa interview) or an employer-sponsored H-1B. See [the J-1 process](/wiki/visas/j1-visa-process), [J-1 versus H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b) and [visa interview preparation](/wiki/visas/visa-interview-preparation).

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: We read the text of Proclamation 10998 (effective January 1, 2026). The Philippines does not appear on its full or partial suspension lists. The lists are reviewed periodically, so check the [White House text](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/restricting-and-limiting-the-entry-of-foreign-nationals-to-protect-the-security-of-the-united-states/) and travel.state.gov before each step. Online presence review and narrower interview waivers since 2025 apply to all nationalities; see [the visa policy tracker](/wiki/visas/visa-policy-changes-tracker).

Appointment waits at the US embassy in Manila change often and are published by the State Department; we do not quote them. This is general information, not legal advice. Confirm your case with the program's international office, ECFMG or an immigration attorney.

## Community and professional resources

The Philippine Medical Association (PMA) is the national professional body in the Philippines; read its [About page](https://www.philippinemedicalassociation.org/about-pma/) for what it offers. Philippine physician groups also exist in the US, but we could not verify a national umbrella organization's current details, so we do not name one. ECFMG keeps a page of ethnic physician organizations that may list relevant groups. These groups offer networking only and cannot influence eligibility or placement.
