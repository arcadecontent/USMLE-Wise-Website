If you are a US citizen at a Caribbean medical school, you are an international medical graduate (IMG) for ECFMG and NRMP purposes, and you will need ECFMG Certification before residency. The visa questions in most IMG guides do not apply to you. The questions that do apply are whether your school's Sponsor Note covers your graduation year and how to judge a school fairly before you commit. This guide does not name or rank schools. It is general information, not legal advice.

## How US citizens from Caribbean schools are counted

NRMP defines a **U.S. IMG** as a US citizen who is enrolled in or graduated from a medical school outside the US that is not accredited by the LCME (Liaison Committee on Medical Education) or COCA (Commission on Osteopathic College Accreditation). The Caribbean is not a separate category. In the 2026 Main Residency Match:

- 4,210 U.S. IMGs submitted rank order lists and 2,949 matched to a PGY-1 position, a rate of 70.0 percent.
- 11,944 non-U.S. citizen IMGs submitted rank order lists and 6,733 matched (56.4 percent).
- NRMP says the number of U.S. IMGs who ranked fell by 838, or 16.6 percent, between 2022 and 2026.

We found no match figure for Caribbean schools or the region as a whole in the reports we downloaded, and NRMP's Charting Outcomes reports group applicants by specialty and Match status, not by school. Be sceptical of any school that advertises "Match rates" without defining who is counted (all enrollees, graduates who applied, or only those who ranked). See [IMG match statistics](/wiki/start-here/img-match-statistics).

## What US citizens still need

Citizenship removes the need for a visa and the J-1 sponsorship step. It does not remove ECFMG requirements: an eligible school and graduation year, the required USMLE Steps, the clinical skills requirement and credential verification. See the [certification overview](/wiki/ecfmg/ecfmg-certification-overview). Program directors may still ask about the school and the timing of your exams. Programs that sponsor visas for others may treat citizens differently, so ask programs what they require.

## Checking eligibility and accreditation

1. Find the school in the World Directory of Medical Schools and read its **ECFMG Sponsor Note** and the graduation years it covers. See [World Directory guide](/wiki/ecfmg/world-directory-and-school-eligibility).
2. Check the accreditation requirement. Since 2024 ECFMG has required accreditation by an agency recognized by the World Federation for Medical Education (WFME). See [accreditation requirement](/wiki/ecfmg/accreditation-requirement).
3. Confirm the agency on WFME's list. The agencies relevant to the Caribbean on WFME's list in November 2026 were:

| Agency | Scope as WFME lists it | Recognition shown until |
|---|---|---|
| CAAM-HP | Caribbean Accreditation Authority for Education in Medicine and Other Health Professions: a long list of jurisdictions including Antigua and Barbuda, Barbados, Dominica, Grenada, Jamaica, Saint Kitts and Nevis, Saint Lucia, and Trinidad and Tobago | May 2033 |
| ACCM | Accreditation Commission on Colleges of Medicine, domiciled in Ireland: including the British Virgin Islands, Cayman Islands, Sint Maarten, Saint Kitts and Nevis, and Dominica | August 2032 |
| GMDC | Grenada Medical and Dental Council | September 2032 |
| Others | IAAR (domiciled in Kazakhstan) lists Barbados, Dominica and Belize; AAEPO lists Curaçao | 2028 / 2032 |

Each agency is recognized for the countries listed on its entry. A school accredited by an agency for a country that is not on that list may not satisfy ECFMG's rule. Recognition also does not imply that every school in a country is accredited, so you need the school-level answer from the Sponsor Note and the school's own accreditation page.

> [!WARNING] Accreditation by a US state, a regional body that is not on WFME's recognized list, or a "pending" status does not by itself satisfy ECFMG's requirement. If a school's Sponsor Note does not cover the graduation year you expect, ask ECFMG before paying tuition for later semesters.

## Clinical clerkships and US clinical experience

Many Caribbean schools run basic sciences on their island campus and place students in clinical clerkships elsewhere, often in the US. We rely on official sources here:

- **Federal student-loan rules:** 34 CFR 600.55 sets conditions for foreign graduate medical schools whose students use US federal loans. It requires formal affiliation agreements for sites providing core clinical rotations, limits unapproved elective sites for students (no more than two electives totaling eight weeks), and exempts clinical sites in programs accredited by the LCME or AOA. Ask the school whether it meets these requirements.
- **State requirements:** some states have their own approval or reporting rules for clinical clerkships by foreign-school students. We did not verify a current list, so check the medical board of the state where the rotation takes place and the state where you want a license.
- **Your application:** programs read letters and clinical performance, not just where the rotation was held. See [USCE explained](/wiki/clinical-experience/usce-explained) and [how much USCE programs want](/wiki/clinical-experience/how-much-usce-programs-want).

## How to evaluate a school objectively

We do not name or rank schools. These are questions to ask any school, in writing, and compare:

- **First-time USMLE pass rates:** ask for first-time pass rates for Step 1 and Step 2 CK with the number of test-takers and the years. 34 CFR 600.55 requires foreign medical schools whose students use US federal loans to meet a first-time pass rate of at least 75 percent across Steps 1 and 2 as measured under the regulation, with small-sample rules. Know what a number does and does not count: it does not include students who leave before testing.
- **Attrition:** how many students who start each cohort graduate, and how many are dismissed, withdraw or repeat semesters.
- **Exam gatekeeping:** does the school require a pass or a score on a practice exam before letting students sit Step 1? If so, how many are held back?
- **Accreditation:** agency, scope, dates, and the Sponsor Note years.
- **Transparency:** does the school publish outcomes with definitions, name its clinical sites, and publish costs including repeats?
- **Residency outcomes:** ask for counts by year (applied, matched, and in what specialties) with the definition of each, not just a percentage.
- **Cost:** tuition, living costs and the cost of a delayed graduation. See [total cost of matching](/wiki/start-here/total-cost-of-matching).

> [!IMG TIP] A school that answers these questions clearly and in writing is easier to trust than one that answers with a slogan. Opinion: be cautious of any claim that cannot be checked against NRMP, ECFMG, USMLE or the school's own published, dated data.

## Entry restrictions and Caribbean nationals

US citizens are not affected by entry proclamations. If you are a Caribbean citizen rather than a US citizen, nationality matters. We read Proclamation 10998 on whitehouse.gov:

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: Effective January 1, 2026, Proclamation 10998 places Haiti in the full-suspension group, and lists Cuba, Antigua and Barbuda, and Dominica among partial-suspension countries that cover immigrant visas and B, F, M and J nonimmigrant visas, with limited exceptions. The restrictions are based on the nationality of the applicant, not on which school they attend. We found no later proclamation changing the lists. Check the State Department and our [visa policy tracker](/wiki/visas/visa-policy-changes-tracker).
