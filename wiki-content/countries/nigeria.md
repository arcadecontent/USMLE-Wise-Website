Nigerian graduates face two issues that other country guides in this series do not share: a school accreditation question that needs careful checking, and current US visa restrictions that affect J-1 and visitor visas. This guide covers what we could verify, and says where we could not. It is about graduates of Nigerian schools; your school's location, not your citizenship, drives most of the school rules.

## What the data does and does not say

NRMP defines IMGs by school location and reports non-U.S. IMGs as one group. Its 2026 Main Residency Match report says 16,154 active applicants were IMGs (U.S. citizen and non-U.S. citizen combined), with no country breakdown.

A JAMA Network Open study that linked ECFMG applicant records to the AMA Physician Masterfile reported 4,167 ECFMG applicants who were citizens of Nigeria from 2009 to 2018, counted by citizenship at entry to medical school. That is a count of applicants over ten years, not Match outcomes. We could not open ECFMG's own tables, so we give no current annual figure. See [IMG match statistics](/wiki/start-here/img-match-statistics).

## Checking your school's eligibility

1. Search your school in the World Directory and open the **ECFMG Sponsor Note**. Read the graduation years it lists. See [the World Directory guide](/wiki/ecfmg/world-directory-and-school-eligibility).
2. Understand the requirement that your school be accredited by a WFME-recognized agency for your graduation year. See [the accreditation requirement](/wiki/ecfmg/accreditation-requirement).
3. Know the national body. The Medical and Dental Council of Nigeria (MDCN) publishes lists of medical schools it accredits, including full and partial accreditation status ([MDCN list](https://www.mdcn.gov.ng/public/storage/documents/document_501308240.pdf)).

The important point: MDCN accreditation is a national license-to-train status. On the WFME Recognition Programme page we read, Nigeria does not appear among agencies with recognition status or among agencies applying. We found no ECFMG statement that confirms how this affects specific Nigerian schools by graduation year. That is exactly what the Sponsor Note shows, and it can differ from school to school.

If your Sponsor Note does not cover your graduation year, do not assume a workaround exists and do not pay anyone who claims to have one. Contact ECFMG, read the [accreditation guide](/wiki/ecfmg/accreditation-requirement), and ask your school about its accreditation plans. If you are still a student, check this before your final year.

> [!WARNING] Do not rely on forum posts or agents for your school's status. The ECFMG Sponsor Note for your graduation year is the answer.

## USMLE testing

Since January 2026 IMGs register for the USMLE through FSMB, while ECFMG decides eligibility for ECFMG Certification ([announcement](https://www.usmle.org/usmle-service-transition-launching-january-2026)). USMLE says Step 1 and Step 2 CK are offered at Prometric centers worldwide, and Step 3 only in the US and its territories.

We could not verify, from usmle.org or Prometric, which Nigerian cities have centers. After you have a permit, use the Prometric locator and compare several centers and dates. If you cannot find a seat in your window, you may need to test in another country, which brings visa and testing region questions; read [the test center guide](/wiki/exams/international-test-centers). Step 3 requires travel to the US, and the visa restrictions below matter for that plan.

## Documents and credential verification

ECFMG verifies your final diploma and transcript directly with the issuing school. Practical steps:

- Check the ECFMG Reference Guide for the exact diploma title for your school and graduation year.
- Match names and dates across diploma, transcript and passport.
- Ask your school's registry which office responds to ECFMG, and keep records of every request.
- See [the EPIC and verification guide](/wiki/ecfmg/epic-credential-verification) and [common ECFMG delays](/wiki/ecfmg/common-ecfmg-delays).

We found no official ECFMG note specific to Nigerian documents.

## The US visa process and current restrictions

The usual route is an ECFMG-sponsored J-1 (Statement of Need, DS-2019, visa interview) or an employer-sponsored H-1B. See [the J-1 process](/wiki/visas/j1-visa-process) and [J-1 versus H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b).

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: Proclamation 10998 (signed December 16, 2025; effective 12:01 a.m. EST, January 1, 2026) places Nigeria on its partial suspension list. For countries on that list, the text suspends entry on B-1, B-2, B-1/B-2, F, M and J visas, suspends immigrant visas, and tells consular officers to reduce the validity of other nonimmigrant visas to the extent permitted by law. It applies to people outside the US on the effective date who do not have a valid visa that day, and visas issued earlier are not revoked. Exceptions in the text do not include a general J-1 exception, but case-by-case waivers by the Attorney General, Secretary of State or Secretary of Homeland Security are provided for. The Secretary of State must report on the lists every 180 days. Read the [White House text](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/restricting-and-limiting-the-entry-of-foreign-nationals-to-protect-the-security-of-the-united-states/) and check travel.state.gov and the U.S. Mission in Nigeria for the current position.

What this means in practice, with the caveats that we could not open State Department pages and that outcomes are decided case by case: a new J-1 visa application could be affected unless an exception or waiver applies, a B-1/B-2 visit for Step 3 or an in-person interview could be affected the same way, and a visa already in your passport and valid is outside the suspension. The H-1B visa is not named in the suspension list, but see [the visa policy tracker](/wiki/visas/visa-policy-changes-tracker) for the separate H-1B entry restriction. Because stakes are high, ask the program's international office, ECFMG, or an immigration attorney early, and read [B-1/B-2 for exams and interviews](/wiki/visas/b1-b2-for-interviews-and-exams). This is general information, not legal advice.

We also saw reports that a separate USCIS hold on benefit applications for nationals of listed countries was vacated by a federal court in June 2026. We did not verify the court order or its implementation; check [USCIS](https://www.uscis.gov) before relying on either.

## What to do next

1. Read your school's ECFMG Sponsor Note for your graduation year.
2. If it does not cover you, contact ECFMG before spending on exams.
3. Check the proclamation and your visa status before planning any travel.
4. Read [the ECFMG certification overview](/wiki/ecfmg/ecfmg-certification-overview) and plan US experience with [the USCE guide](/wiki/clinical-experience/usce-explained).
