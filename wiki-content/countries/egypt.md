Egyptian graduates are a recurring group among IMG applicants to US residency, but US official data does not break results out by country. This guide explains what we could verify about numbers, school eligibility, USMLE testing, documents and the visa process, and where we could not confirm details. It is about graduates of schools in Egypt; your school's location, not your citizenship, drives most of the rules.

## What the data does and does not say

NRMP defines IMGs by school location and reports "non-U.S. IMGs" as one group. Its 2026 Main Residency Match report says 16,154 active applicants were IMGs (U.S. citizen and non-U.S. citizen combined), with no country breakdown.

A JAMA Network Open study that linked ECFMG applicant records to the AMA Physician Masterfile reported 4,181 ECFMG applicants who were Egyptian citizens from 2009 to 2018, counted by citizenship at entry to medical school. That is a decade of applicants, not Match outcomes, and not a count by school location. ECFMG's own tables were not accessible to us, so we give no current annual figure. See [IMG match statistics](/wiki/start-here/img-match-statistics) for how to read the national numbers.

## Checking your school's eligibility

1. Search your school in the World Directory and read the **ECFMG Sponsor Note** for the graduation years covered. See [the World Directory guide](/wiki/ecfmg/world-directory-and-school-eligibility).
2. Understand that ECFMG requires accreditation by a WFME-recognized agency for your graduation year. See [the accreditation requirement](/wiki/ecfmg/accreditation-requirement).
3. Know the national bodies. Egypt's university medical faculties operate under the Supreme Council of Universities, according to published research, and national accreditation is run by the National Authority for Quality Assurance and Accreditation of Education (NAQAAE). The WFME Recognition Programme list we read shows NAQAAE with recognition status until April 2029.

NAQAAE's recognition is a statement about the agency. It does not tell you whether your own faculty was accredited, or accredited in your graduation year. That is what the Sponsor Note answers. Ask your faculty for written confirmation of its accreditation dates as well.

> [!KEY] Check three things: the agency (NAQAAE and its WFME status), your faculty's accreditation, and your graduation year in the Sponsor Note.

## USMLE testing

Since January 2026 IMGs register for the USMLE through FSMB, while ECFMG decides eligibility for ECFMG Certification ([announcement](https://www.usmle.org/usmle-service-transition-launching-january-2026)). USMLE says Step 1 and Step 2 CK are offered at Prometric centers worldwide, and Step 3 only in the US and its territories.

Unofficial lists name Egyptian cities, but we could not confirm them on usmle.org or Prometric and do not repeat them. After you receive a permit, check the Prometric locator for several centers and dates, and read [the test center guide](/wiki/exams/international-test-centers). If no seat is available in your window, travel to another country may be needed. That brings visa and testing region questions, so look at [B-1/B-2 for exams](/wiki/visas/b1-b2-for-interviews-and-exams) early.

## Documents and credential verification

ECFMG verifies your final diploma and transcript directly with the issuing school, and mailed copies from you do not replace that. Practical steps:

- Check the ECFMG Reference Guide for the exact diploma title for your school and graduation year.
- Match names and dates across diploma, transcript and passport. If transliteration of your name varies, resolve it before you apply.
- Ask your faculty which office answers ECFMG requests and keep a record.
- Documents not in English need translations that meet ECFMG's rules. Read the current Information Booklet for the requirement, and see [the EPIC and verification guide](/wiki/ecfmg/epic-credential-verification) and [common ECFMG delays](/wiki/ecfmg/common-ecfmg-delays).

We found no official ECFMG note specific to Egyptian documents. Do not rely on forum advice about extra stamps or ministry certification; ask ECFMG.

## The US visa process, in brief

The usual route is an ECFMG-sponsored J-1 (Statement of Need, DS-2019, visa interview) or an employer-sponsored H-1B. See [the J-1 process](/wiki/visas/j1-visa-process), [J-1 versus H-1B](/wiki/visas/j1-vs-h1b) and [visa interview preparation](/wiki/visas/visa-interview-preparation).

> [!CHANGED] November 2026: We read the text of Proclamation 10998 (effective January 1, 2026). Egypt does not appear on its full or partial suspension lists. The Secretary of State must report on the lists every 180 days, so they can change. Check the [White House text](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/restricting-and-limiting-the-entry-of-foreign-nationals-to-protect-the-security-of-the-united-states/) and travel.state.gov before each step. Online presence review and narrower interview waivers apply to all nationalities; see [the visa policy tracker](/wiki/visas/visa-policy-changes-tracker).

Appointment waits at the US embassy in Cairo change often and are published by the State Department; we do not quote them. This is general information, not legal advice. Confirm your case with the program's international office, ECFMG or an immigration attorney.

## Community and professional resources

The Egyptian American Medical Association (EAMA) describes itself as a nonprofit group of physicians of Egyptian descent, founded in 2012, and lists mentoring and match guidance among its programs (see its [site](https://www.e-ama.org/en)). We could not verify its specific services, so ask the association directly. Networking and mentoring groups cannot influence eligibility or placement.
